San Francisco Poke Bowls

Eight San Francisco poke counters worth the walk-up line, ranked by how fast the fish disappears. Where locals go when the sushi bar wants forty bucks and an hour.

Poke Origin

716 Irving St, San Francisco. The Inner Sunset workhorse near Golden Gate Park, where the line moves fast and the mix-ins stay generous. You point, they build, you leave full for under fifteen bucks. Grab a bowl before you walk the park, not after, when everything good is gone. @thepokeorigin

Fish In The Box

800 Masonic Ave, San Francisco. This tiny Panhandle spot treats poke like a sashimi counter, bluefin toro and Hokkaido scallop over rice instead of the usual scoop-and-sauce. They close midday to reset, so time it or stand outside reading the hours like the rest of us. Worth the trip up Masonic. @fishinthebox_sf

Happy Poke

2760 Octavia St, San Francisco. A pocket-sized Cow Hollow counter where you fill out a little clipboard menu and they cut the fish silky, sashimi style. Snag one of the few outdoor tables near Union Street or eat it walking, nobody judges. Closed Mondays, so plan the craving around it. @happypokesf

I'a Poke

314 Church St, San Francisco. The Mission Dolores standby for a fat sushi burrito or a build-your-own bowl, right where the J-Church spits you out. Counter service, quick turnover, the kind of lunch you eat on the Dolores Park grass ten minutes later. Go before the weekend crowd claims the whole hillside. @iapokesf

Poke Delish

601 Mission Bay Blvd North, San Francisco. A poke stall parked inside Spark Social, the Mission Bay food yard with fire pits and picnic benches. Traditional Hawaiian bowls with a California hand on the sauce, best eaten out back while somebody's dog steals attention. Closes at five on Sundays, so it's a lunch play. @pokedelish

Poke Bowl

1250 Ocean Ave, San Francisco. The Ingleside neighborhood pick, a mark-the-sheet build-your-own that stays open till nine thirty when everything else near City College has folded for the night. No frills, big portions, the reliable one when you're out past the tourist map. Parking is easy out here, a small SF miracle. @thepokebowls

Da Poke Man

24 Willie Mays Plaza, San Francisco. Hawaiian poke slinging out of a truck by Oracle Park, and yes, they set up inside near section 112 on Giants home games. Catch it on a non-game day when the line isn't a hundred orange jerseys deep. Fresh, generous, and cheaper than a ballpark hot dog by a mile. @da_poke_man

Bimi Poke

135 4th St, San Francisco. Tucked in the Metreon by the AMC, a Hawaiian-Japanese counter doing poke bowls and sushi burritos for the pre-movie crowd. Downtown lunch that won't wreck the afternoon, order the burrito if you want to eat one-handed in a dark theater. Open late enough for the last showtime. @maywen1985

Eight for the poke crawl: Poke Origin and Poke Bowl for fast neighborhood scoops, Fish In The Box and Happy Poke for sashimi-grade cuts, I'a Poke and Poke Delish for the bowl-plus-burrito crowd, Da Poke Man and Bimi Poke for eating on the move. Which SF neighborhood gets your line next?
