San Francisco South Bay Japanese and Sushi

Eight South Bay Japanese spots worth crossing town for, from Campbell rolls to a Cupertino izakaya to a Mountain View omakase seat. The kind of counters where regulars know the chef's name and the parking lot is its own adventure.

Sushi Confidential — 247 E Campbell Ave, Campbell. Downtown Campbell's rowdy favorite for showy rolls and cocktails, the spot that turned one location into a South Bay handful. Come for happy hour on a weeknight, when the patio's lively but you can still hear your table. Weekends the wait tells you what the neighborhood already decided. @sushiconfidential

Kaita — 215 Jackson St, San Jose. A family-run counter in Japantown between 5th and 6th, known for handwritten daily specials and Japanese comfort food that doesn't chase trends. They cap the credit cards per table and wave off tap-to-pay, so bring a little cash and some patience. Closed Sundays and Mondays, and worth planning your week around.

Ozumo — 355 Santana Row, San Jose. The dressed-up end of the list, a contemporary izakaya doing sushi, share plates, and a serious sake list on Santana Row. Yes, you'll circle for parking behind the shoppers, and yes, the bill climbs. Grab a seat at the sushi bar, order a spread of small plates and something poured cold, and let the people-watching earn its keep. @ozumosantanarow

Gochi Japanese Fusion Tapas — 19980 E Homestead Rd, Cupertino. Hidden in a Homestead Road strip mall. That's the South Bay's favorite disguise for a great meal. It's small-plate izakaya fusion, packed most nights, and the regulars order like they've memorized the menu. Closed Mondays, no shortcuts on the wait, so put your name down early and settle in. @gochicupertino

Tanto — 1063 E El Camino Real, Sunnyvale. An izakaya running since 2000, which in Silicon Valley years makes it practically a landmark. This is where engineers unwind over sashimi, grilled specialties, and cold beer in a room that never pretended to be fancy. Dinner runs a tight window each night, so aim for the early seating or expect to loiter. @tanto_japanese_restaurant

Adachi — 409 San Antonio Rd, Mountain View. A calm sushi room by San Antonio Center where the omakase is the reason to come, the fish flies in from Tokyo's Toyosu market, and the seat at the counter is the one to book. Dinner runs in two seatings, so pick your hour, let the chef steer, and pace yourself before the nigiri hits its stride. The good spots go to people who planned. @sushiadachi_bayarea

HiroNori Craft Ramen — 4300 Great America Pkwy, Santa Clara. Craft bowls with noodles made in-house, tucked by Levi's Stadium where game days turn the whole area into a parking lot. Seating's tight enough that Santa Clara runs on reservations, so book your slot, order the tonkotsu, and add the seasoned egg. Aim for a non-game day unless sitting in traffic is your warm-up. @hironoriramen

Amazing Katsu — 2670 El Camino Real, Santa Clara. A specialist that made katsu its entire personality, and the shattering crunch backs up the swagger. Panko-fried pork, chicken, shrimp, plus a Korean cheese katsu that pulls into strings, packed in vented boxes so it stays crisp on the drive. It's takeout only, so grab a combo and thank yourself later at the kitchen table. @amazingkatsu_official

Eight for a South Bay Japanese crawl: Campbell rolls, Japantown comfort, Santana Row splurge, Cupertino tapas, Sunnyvale izakaya, Mountain View omakase, and a pair of Santa Clara ends for ramen and katsu. Half the fun of knowing an area is knowing which counter to drive to on a Tuesday. Which one's your table?
