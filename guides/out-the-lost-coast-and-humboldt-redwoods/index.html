Bay Area The Lost Coast and Humboldt Redwoods

There is a stretch of California coast so steep and wild that Highway 1 gives up and swings inland around it. That is the Lost Coast, and the drive there runs through the tallest trees on earth. Six hikes across the Lost Coast and the Humboldt redwoods, with the grade and route on each.

Lost Coast Trail, Mattole to Black Sands Beach — King Range Wilderness, Humboldt. 25.4 miles, 1,522 feet of gain, moderate, point to point, 4.9 stars. The classic three day black sand through hike, permit and bear canister required, time the tides. @mypubliclands

Punta Gorda Lighthouse via the Lost Coast Trail — King Range Wilderness, Humboldt. 6.5 miles, 383 feet, moderate, out and back, 4.6 stars. A day hike to an abandoned 1912 lighthouse and a seal colony.

Lost Coast Central Stretch — King Range Wilderness, Humboldt. 17.4 miles, 846 feet, moderate, out and back, 4.8 stars. The wilder southern beach section for a shorter overnight.

Bull Creek Flats Trail — Humboldt Redwoods State Park, Weott. 7.6 miles, 626 feet, moderate, loop, 4.8 stars. Through Rockefeller Forest, the largest old growth redwood flat on earth. @castateparks

Founders Grove and Gould Grove Nature Trail — Humboldt Redwoods State Park, Weott. 0.7 miles, easy, loop, 4.7 stars. A flat wheelchair accessible loop past the fallen Dyerville Giant.

Rockefeller Loop — Humboldt Redwoods State Park, Weott. 0.6 miles, easy, loop, 4.8 stars. The short stroll under the biggest trees, stroller friendly.

Six on the far North Coast: the Mattole through hike, Punta Gorda lighthouse, the central stretch, Bull Creek Flats, Founders Grove, and the Rockefeller Loop. The Lost Coast or the giant redwoods first?
