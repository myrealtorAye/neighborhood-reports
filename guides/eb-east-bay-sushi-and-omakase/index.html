East Bay Sushi and Omakase

You do not have to cross a bridge for a serious omakase anymore, the East Bay now has counters where the fish flies in from Japan and the price still undercuts the city. Eight East Bay sushi and omakase spots.

Delage — Old Oakland. A tiny omakase counter of eight or so courses, California ingredients on Japanese technique, a set menu that sells out. Book the moment seats drop.

Itani Sushi — North Berkeley. A daily changing omakase from chef Kyle Itani with fish flown from Nagasaki, Thursday through Saturday. The special occasion seat.

Kamado Sushi — 1400 Shattuck Ave, Berkeley. A Gourmet Ghetto room bringing freshness and variety without the pretension, nigiri and rolls done right. The reliable weeknight.

Kirala — 2100 Ward St, Berkeley. A Berkeley sushi and robata institution since 1989, a quiet traditional counter, no gimmicks, worth the wait. The old guard.

Ijji Sushi — Oakland. A sustainability focused sushi bar with a thoughtful omakase and house made touches, small and personal. The conscientious pick.

Kansai — 3945 Piedmont Ave, Oakland. A Piedmont Avenue neighborhood sushi spot with a deep menu and generous cuts, the dependable dinner. The everyday counter.

Geta — 165 41st St, Oakland. A Temescal Japanese spot with sushi and izakaya plates, easy and consistent. The casual option.

Live Sushi Bistro — San Leandro. A south county sushi bar with fresh fish and creative rolls, proof the good sushi is not all in Oakland. The southern outpost.

Eight for the omakase: Delage, Itani, Kamado, Kirala, Ijji, Kansai, Geta, and Live Sushi. Where do you sit at the counter?
