San Francisco Burgers

Seven San Francisco burgers worth crossing town for, the ones locals defend and tourists never find. A Marina smash patty, a lunch-only classic on rosemary focaccia, a West Portal bison burger most people drive past. Here's the SF crawl your group chat keeps relitigating. Bring napkins.

Causwells — 2346 Chestnut St, San Francisco. The Marina's grown-up burger, an Americana smash patty quietly collecting awards on Chestnut since 2014. Come for the burger, stay because the patio heaters make October feel like a personal favor. It's the kind of corner that sells the whole block in one bite. @causwells

Wesburger 'n' More — 2240 Mission St, San Francisco. Wes Rowe brought his Texas smash technique to the Mission, and the neon sign out front does not oversell it. Thin crispy-edged patties, chili con carne if you're committing, fries you'll fight over. Tiny room, big payoff, the kind of block that rewards anyone who wanders off Valencia. @wesburger_n_more

Zuni Cafe — 1658 Market St, San Francisco. Everyone name-drops the roast chicken, but the grass-fed burger on rosemary focaccia is the sleeper order, aioli, Zuni pickles, gone by mid-afternoon. It runs lunch only Friday through Sunday, so this one takes planning. A 1979 institution near the Symphony that has outlasted half the addresses around it. @zunicafe

Balboa Cafe — 3199 Fillmore St, San Francisco. Open since 1913, back when this corner was a working saloon, and still slinging the Balboa Burger on a baguette like it's the only sensible shape for one. Order it at the bar with an Espressotini and watch Cow Hollow do its thing. Proof a good address only improves with a century on it. @balboacafesf

The BullsHead — 840 Ulloa St, San Francisco. West Portal's throwback since 1979, where the western bison burger arrives with a salad bar nobody under forty remembers the rules to. The back room feels like a tavern out of a fantasy novel, dim and wood-paneled and gloriously stuck in time. Worth the ride to the far side of Twin Peaks. @bullsheadrestaurantsf

Gott's Roadside — 1 Ferry Building, San Francisco. The waterfront patio has been the reward at the end of a Ferry Plaza Farmers Market lap since 2004. Get the California Burger (fried egg, bacon, balsamic onions) and a shake, claim a communal bench, and let the Bay do the entertaining. Touristy, sure, but the locals never stopped coming. @gottsroadside

Nopa — 560 Divisadero St, San Francisco. The restaurant that named the neighborhood, still wood-firing at an hour when most kitchens have mopped up. The cheeseburger is the move, grass-fed Stemple Creek beef ground daily, house-baked brioche bun, pickled onions, basil parmesan aioli. Snag a late seat at the bar after a Divisadero night out. @nopasf

Seven for the great SF burger argument: Causwells for the Marina smash, Wesburger for the Mission char, Zuni for the focaccia lunch, Balboa for the baguette, BullsHead for the bison, Gott's for the waterfront, Nopa for the midnight patty. Which block gets your vote, and which one got left off?
