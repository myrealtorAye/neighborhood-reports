San Francisco Marin Seafood On the Water

Seven Marin seafood spots right on the water, the ones locals drive to instead of fighting the crowds at Fisherman's Wharf. Think Sausalito decks over the bay, Tomales oyster shacks, and a Tiburon classic worth the wait. Every stop comes with a view and a jacket worthy breeze off the water. Go on the next clear Saturday.

Sam's Anchor Cafe — 27 Main Street, Tiburon. The waterfront deck is the whole point, on a clear day you are staring straight at Angel Island, Alcatraz and the city skyline. They take reservations inside but never on the deck, so the move is to show up early and dig in. Weekend brunch here is a proper Bay Area institution. Take the ferry over from the city and skip the parking hunt. @sams_cafe

Fish — 350 Harbor Drive, Sausalito. A no frills waterfront spot on the harbor, order at the counter and grab a picnic bench right by the boats. The whole operation is dead serious about sustainable, local catch, and yes, the pup is welcome on the patio. It sits past the ferry landing, so it stays a locals find instead of a tourist stop. Come hungry and come early. @fishrestaurantsausalito

Scoma's of Sausalito — 588 Bridgeway, Sausalito. The dressier pick, a longtime waterfront landmark sitting right over the bay with unobstructed views back at the city. This is the spot for taking the parents or marking an occasion, think whole Dungeness crab, wild king salmon, and petrale sole done right. Book ahead and ask for a window table, it is worth the call. @scomassausalito

Barrel House Tavern — 660 Bridgeway, Sausalito. Built inside the old ferry terminal that carried folks to the city before the Golden Gate existed, so the character comes free. The back patio opens right onto the bay with the skyline dead ahead. Half a dozen oysters run twelve bucks at happy hour. That's the smart way in. Time your table for sunset and do not rush it. @barrelhousetavern

Hog Island, The Boat Oyster Bar — 20215 Shoreline Highway, Marshall. This is oyster country, a working farm on Tomales Bay where the outdoor tables sit feet from the water the oysters are grown in. Reserve Friday through Monday for the full cafe, or roll up Thursday to shuck your own by the dozen. Bring layers and something cold to drink. Summer reservations vanish fast, plan ahead. @hogislandoysterco

Nick's Cove — 23240 Highway One, Marshall. Further up the shoreline, all weathered wood charm with decks built right over Tomales Bay. The BBQ oysters are the order here, SFGate named them one of the best things they ate back in 2023. There are twelve cottages too, handy when the drive home feels long. Post oyster naps are undefeated. @nickscove

Saltwater Oyster Depot — 12781 Sir Francis Drake Boulevard, Inverness. The one that is a block off the bay, not over it, and it still earns a spot, a tiny Inverness oyster bar and wine shop out toward Point Reyes. They shuck oysters minutes after they leave Tomales Bay and pour regional wine straight from the tap. Weekends run a la carte, weeknights a prix fixe. Make it the reward at the end of the drive.

Seven for Marin seafood on the water: Sam's Anchor in Tiburon, Fish, Scoma's and Barrel House on the Sausalito waterfront, then Hog Island, Nick's Cove and Saltwater out on Tomales Bay. White tablecloths or a paper tray of oysters, the whole coastline delivers. Which end are you starting from, Sausalito or the oyster shacks?
