Bay Area Briones and Las Trampas Ridge Hikes

Eight East Bay ridge hikes where the Bay Area quietly shows off. Most people gun for Mission Peak and skip the Briones and Las Trampas crests next door, where 1,483-foot Briones Peak opens a 360 on the Delta, Tam, and Diablo. Contra Costa's best-kept ridges, mapped.

Briones Crest Trail — Briones Regional Park, Lafayette. The spine of the park's 6,256 acres, the ridgeline every other trail climbs to meet. Rolling grassland, big oaks, and grazing cattle that hold the right of way, over a view corridor locals blow past on the drive to Tahoe. @ebrpd

Mott Peak Trail — Briones Regional Park, Lafayette. The 1,424-foot high point most people never bother to name, tucked along the crest between the lagoons and Briones Peak. Short pull, big payoff, and on a weekday the summit is usually yours alone. Bring layers, the wind up here does not negotiate. @ebrpd

Briones Peak — Briones Regional Park, Lafayette. At 1,483 feet this is the park's true summit, and the district's own map promises a 360 on the California Delta, Mount Tamalpais, Mount Diablo, and Las Trampas. Clear winter mornings after rain are the move. Naturalist John Muir, who lived in nearby Martinez, hiked these hills, so you are in good company. @ebrpd

Sindicich Lagoons — Briones Regional Park, Lafayette. Twin ponds sitting right up on the crest, reached off the Lagoon Trail, one of the few flat quiet pockets you will find at this elevation. Bring a snack and take in the whole ridge from a spot most hikers skip while chasing the next summit. Golden hour on the water does the work for you. @ebrpd

Las Trampas Rocky Ridge — Las Trampas Wilderness, San Ramon. A paved service road climbs you toward the 2,024-foot summit, then the wind-sculpted stone outcrops take over, colored by lichen the district calls beautiful. Rocky Ridge View Trail bans bikes, so it stays calm. Give the full ridge and the Devil's Hole area a day if you have one. @ebrpd

Las Trampas Ridge Trail — Las Trampas Wilderness, San Ramon. The east ridge across Bollinger Creek, running toward 1,827-foot Las Trampas Peak, with the district crediting views of the Ygnacio, San Ramon, and Amador valleys plus Mount Diablo and the Carquinez Strait. Reach it via Chamise and the Bollinger Creek Loop. Boots recommended, and that is the park talking. @ebrpd

Eugene O'Neill Site and Bollinger Canyon — Las Trampas Wilderness, Danville. Walk in from Danville up the Iron Horse Trail into Las Trampas and finish at the Tao House, the National Park Service parcel of the Eugene O'Neill National Historic Site. The Park Service runs it by reservation, so call ahead before you count on getting in. Oaks and bay laurel the whole climb, and a canyon that feels a county away from I-680. @ebrpd

Elderberry Trail — Las Trampas Wilderness, San Ramon. A quieter hiker line off the Bollinger Creek valley floor, named for the elderberry that fills the chaparral alongside the manzanita and toyon. A low-key way to warm up a Rocky Ridge day before the grade kicks in. Signed and on the district map, unlike the bootleg paths they ask you to skip. @ebrpd

Eight for the Contra Costa skyline: Briones Crest, Mott Peak, Briones Peak, Sindicich Lagoons, Rocky Ridge, Las Trampas Ridge, the O'Neill canyon walk, and Elderberry. Ridge season in the East Bay runs clear and cool right after the first rains. Which crest do you take first?
