San Francisco Sushi and Japanese

Eight sushi counters that make San Francisco worth the wait, ranked by how fast the line forms and how quiet you get once the fish lands. From omakase temples to family-run neighborhood staples, here is where the rice runs warm and the catch came in that morning.

Ju-Ni — 1335 Fulton Street, San Francisco. One chef, four seats, an omakase built piece by piece while the knife works up close. It sits tucked near Divisadero, the kind of counter you book weeks out and clear your whole evening for. Reserve on Tock before the date you want disappears.

Akiko's — 430 Folsom Street, San Francisco. A sleek SoMa room where the nigiri leans modern and the sake list rewards a slow night. It lands walkable from the Embarcadero, an easy anchor for dinner before a waterfront stroll. Sit at the bar and let the chef read the table. @akikosrestaurant

Kusakabe — 584 Washington Street, San Francisco. Sushi in the Kaiseki style, a multi-course omakase that moves like a quiet performance near Jackson Square. The chef holds a world sushi title, so trust the pacing and drop the a la carte instinct. Two seatings a night, take the early one if you talk with your hands. @kusakabe_sf

Ebisu — 1283 9th Avenue, San Francisco. A family-run Inner Sunset staple that has fed the neighborhood for forty years, steps from Golden Gate Park and the N-Judah. No reservations, so add your name to the paper list and wander the block while you wait. That line out front is the review. @ebisu_sushi_sf

Live Sushi Bar — 2001 17th Street, San Francisco. Fast, fresh, and plated with care on the edge of the Mission and Potrero, a solid weeknight move when you want the good stuff without the omakase commitment. The uni here has a following. Grab a counter seat and start with whatever the chef points at. @livesushisf

Domo — 511 Laguna Street, San Francisco. A mighty little Hayes Valley counter where the chef has held the line on quality since 2008, market fresh and refreshingly unfussy. It stays small, so no reservations, which keeps it honest. Come early or come off-peak and settle in before the pre-theater rush finds it.

Saru — 3856 24th Street, San Francisco. A Noe Valley favorite tucked on a walkable stretch of 24th, walk-in only and proud of it. Put your name down, browse the shops, and come back for nigiri that punches above the cozy room. The specialty rolls are where the kitchen shows off.

Chisai Sushi Club — 3369 Mission Street, San Francisco. A small, warm counter down in the La Lengua pocket of Mission Street, omakase and inventive rolls without the downtown price of admission. Dinner only, running late on weekends. Book through Resy and bring someone who trusts your ordering. @chisaisushiclub

Eight for San Francisco sushi worth the detour: Ju-Ni, Akiko's, Kusakabe, Ebisu, Live Sushi Bar, Domo, Saru, and Chisai Sushi Club. Omakase splurge or neighborhood counter, which one gets your first reservation?
