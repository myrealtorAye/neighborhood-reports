San Francisco Burmese Restaurants

Eight San Francisco Burmese tables worth the Clement Street parking hunt, from a James Beard honored institution to a converted downtown fire house. Tea leaf salad is the gateway, then it gets deep.

Burma Superstar — 309 Clement St, San Francisco. The one that put Burmese food on the local map back in 1992, and the line out front still makes the case. Get the tea leaf salad tossed at your table and the samusa soup. They take no reservations, so put your name down and go wander Clement Street until they wave you in. @burmasuperstar

Mandalay — 4348 California St, San Francisco. The oldest Burmese kitchen in the city, open since 1984 and crowned a James Beard America's Classic. The room is unfussy and the regulars hold firm opinions about the sesame beef and the fish noodle soup. Come early, the kitchen keeps its own hours. @mandalayrestaurant_

B Star — 127 Clement St, San Francisco. The little sister up the same Clement strip from Burma Superstar, calmer and easier to walk into on a whim. It roams past Burma into Thai and Chinese comfort food, but the tea leaf salad and coconut rice hold the line. Weekend brunch here is the neighborhood's quiet secret. @bstarbar

Burma Love — 211 Valencia St, San Francisco. Burma Superstar's Mission spinoff, louder and glossier with a full bar for once. Order the pumpkin pork stew, the garlic noodles and a cocktail you cannot get out in the Richmond. Good for the group that argues about where to eat before anyone agrees. @burmalove

Burma Love Downtown — 8 Mint Plaza, San Francisco. The same kitchen tucked into a restored historic fire house off Mint Plaza, a short walk from Union Square. High ceilings, big cocktails and platters built for sharing after a day downtown. Slip in before a show at the nearby theaters. @burmalove

Burmese Kitchen — 3815 Geary Blvd, San Francisco. Once a Tenderloin fixture, it came back from the dead on Geary and the faithful followed west. This is the homestyle end of the spectrum, so go for the mohinga, the pork curry and whatever the specials board is pushing. Quietly one of the best values in the Richmond. @burmesekitchen

Inle Burmese Cuisine — 822 Irving St, San Francisco. Named for the lake back home, it traded Noe Valley for a bigger Inner Sunset room in 2023 and the seats fill fast. The tea leaf salad and mohinga are the tells, and the Thai half of the menu covers your picky friend. Easy stop before or after Golden Gate Park. @inlecuisine

Pagan — 3199 Clement St, San Francisco. Way out at the foggy end of Clement, this Burmese and Thai corner is the name locals drop when they want the flavors without the wait. Ginger salad, tea leaf salad, one good curry, and you will forget the drive out.

Eight for the Burmese map of San Francisco: the 1992 original, the 1984 James Beard classic, two Burma Loves and a foggy Clement sleeper. Which tea leaf salad wins your table?
