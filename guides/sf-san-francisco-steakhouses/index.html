San Francisco Steakhouses

Eight San Francisco steakhouses worth the splurge, and the tourist lists always circle the same handful. The city's beef runs deeper than a Bay Bridge view, tableside carving carts, Jackson Square jazz, Japanese Wagyu, Brazilian rodizio, Prime and Dungeness crab. This is the list for the next anniversary, closing dinner, or when the in laws roll into town. Here is where locals book.

House of Prime Rib — 1906 Van Ness Ave, San Francisco. An institution since 1949, and still the toughest reservation in town for good reason. There is one decision here, how big a cut of 21 day aged prime rib, wheeled over on a silver cart and carved to your call, with the spun tableside salad and a puff of Yorkshire pudding. Come hungry, go easy on the bread basket, and book weeks ahead. @thehouseofprimerib

Harris' Restaurant — 2100 Van Ness Ave, San Francisco. The grand dame of the genre, dark wood and white tablecloths since 1984, with live jazz drifting over from the bar most nights. Order the dry aged Porterhouse or the bone in New York, chase it with a cold martini, and let the room do the talking. Two blocks from House of Prime Rib, so Van Ness is basically the city's steak row. @harrisrestaurantsf

Bix — 56 Gold Street, San Francisco. Tucked down a lamplit alley in Jackson Square, this two story supper club has poured some of the city's best martinis since 1988. It reads as modern American, but the USDA Prime flat iron and the steak tartare hold their own, and there is live jazz every single night. No hard dress code, but this is a room that rewards a jacket. @bixsf

Epic Steak — 369 The Embarcadero, San Francisco. Yes, it is the one with the Bay Bridge lights practically in your lap, and yes, it earns the view. Prime dry aged cuts and craft cocktails right on the water, plus a move most people miss, weekday happy hour from 4 to 6, Wagyu cheeseburger and beef tartare gougeres at the bar. Snag a patio seat before the fog turns on. @epicsteak

5A5 — 139 Steuart Street, San Francisco. When a plain slab of Prime feels too predictable, this is the move, real Japanese A5 Wagyu seared in front of you, izakaya skewers, and a whisky list that reads like a collector's brag. The small plates mean you graze your way through instead of committing to one giant steak. Bring the friend who swears they have tried everything. @5a5stk

Boboquivari's — 1450 Lombard Street, San Francisco. Locals call it Bobo's, a candlelit Marina hideaway where surf and turf is the entire personality. The 12oz bone in filet gets seared in rosemary garlic butter, and the whole roasted Dungeness crab arrives in a secret garlic sauce worth the bib. It is closed Sunday and Monday, so plan your week around it. @boboquivarissf

Niku Steakhouse — 61 Division Street, San Francisco. The special occasion splurge, a tiny Michelin listed room in SoMa from the Omakase Restaurant Group, built entirely around Wagyu. Expect Japanese Wagyu treated with the same fuss as a tasting menu, so it is precision over portion size. Reservations run through Tock and vanish fast, so set a reminder before date night. @nikusteakhouse

Espetus Brazilian Steakhouse — 1686 Market Street, San Francisco. The all you can eat antidote to fussy, right where Market meets Gough. Gaucho chefs work the room nonstop, carving 14 cuts of fire grilled meat straight onto your plate, rodizio style, with a loaded salad bar for backup. The parade keeps coming until you wave the white flag. This is the move when everyone shows up starving. @espetus

Eight for the city's best beef: House of Prime Rib for the carving cart, Harris' for old guard glamour, Bix for jazz, Epic for the Bay Bridge, 5A5 and Niku for Wagyu, Bobo's for surf and turf, Espetus for the rodizio parade. Which is your special occasion anchor, and who is missing from the list?
