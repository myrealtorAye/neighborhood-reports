San Francisco Peninsula Ramen

Five Peninsula ramen shops that settle the where to slurp argument for good. Most folks still drive up to San Francisco for a bowl. Locals know San Mateo quietly anchors the Peninsula's ramen scene, and the tourists have no idea. Cash, elbows, steam on the windows. Bring an appetite.

Ramen Dojo — 805 S B St, San Mateo. The garlic pork bowl that built this block's whole reputation, and the reason a line forms on the sidewalk before the door even unlocks. Go early or go patient, the regulars have the timing down to a science. Your coworkers will smell it on you by two. Worth every minute.

Santa Ramen — 1944 S El Camino Real, San Mateo. Before ramen became a whole personality, this was the Peninsula's original, the counter that taught the neighborhood what real tonkotsu tastes like. Order it loaded, sit near the kitchen, and maybe skip the nice shirt. Some corners age well.

Kajiken — 112 South B Street, San Mateo. The brothless one. Abura soba, naked noodles you toss yourself with sauce, chashu and a soft egg until every strand goes glossy, so it's on you if it comes out bland. Add the vinegar, thank yourself later, and finding street parking on B counts as a win. @kajikenusa

Totoro Ramen — 856 N Delaware St, San Mateo. Tucked off the main drag where the rent makes more sense, this little counter simmers pork bones 18 hours and lets you order by iPad, no small talk required. Get the black garlic, watch the broth disappear, and yes, it's named after the big fuzzy forest guy.

Marufuku Ramen — 865 Middlefield Rd, Redwood City. Creamy Hakata tonkotsu with the thin, firm noodles, and open daily while the others keep banker's hours, so it saves your Tuesday when nowhere else will. Put your name down, walk the block, come back hungry. The wait list moves like a good open house. @marufukuramenofficial

Five for the Peninsula ramen crawl: Dojo for the line, Santa Ramen for the roots, Kajiken for the brothless rebels, Totoro for the quiet neighborhood bowl, Marufuku for when everyone else is dark. Which one earns your next weeknight, and who's missing from San Mateo's B Street?
