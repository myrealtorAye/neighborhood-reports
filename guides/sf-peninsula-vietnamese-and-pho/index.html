San Francisco Peninsula Vietnamese and Pho

Six Peninsula Vietnamese and pho spots where the broth earns the drive, Daly City down to Redwood City. None of them need a bridge, a reservation, or a trendy zip code, a spoon and an appetite. Tourists chase the SF names while the Peninsula quietly wins the broth. Going north to south.

Kevin's Noodle House

85 Southgate Ave, Daly City. Still flying under its old name Pho Huynh Hiep, this is the number three in a family that has fed foggy Daly City for years. Come for the beef pho, stay because the broth tastes like somebody's grandmother is watching the pot. The Southgate strip will not win design awards, but parking is free and the bowls are enormous.

Ben Tre Vietnamese Homestyle Cuisine

398 Grand Ave, South San Francisco. Grand Ave is having a quiet restaurant renaissance, and Ben Tre is the anchor everyone forgets to name. Homestyle is the operative word here, clay pot, lemongrass, the kind of shaking beef you plan a Tuesday around. Bright, unfussy room right on the main drag. @bentrerestaurant

Pho de Nguyen

586A San Mateo Ave, San Bruno. Chef Phong's oxtail pho is the reason San Bruno's old downtown strip smells like star anise by 11am. Order the bun bo hue if you like a little lemongrass fire with lunch. They will cater a party of seventy, though you will want that first bowl entirely to yourself. Closed Mondays, so plan accordingly.

PPQ Dungeness Island

195 El Camino Real, Millbrae. The splurge stop, whole roasted Dungeness crab over a mountain of garlic noodles, best eaten with sleeves rolled and dignity checked at the door. It is the Vietnamese seafood San Francisco's PPQ made famous, minus the city parking nightmare. Bibs on. Closed Tuesdays.

Asian Box

1401 Burlingame Ave, Burlingame. Right on Burlingame's tree lined shopping stretch, this is the fast, fresh end of the spectrum, build a box of grilled lemongrass chicken or pork over rice or noodles, then hit it with the tamarind sauce. Not a sit down pho night, more a between errands refuel that never tastes like a compromise. @asianbox

Pho Dong

2610 Broadway St, Redwood City. Downtown Redwood City poured real energy into its restaurant row, and Pho Dong quietly took the best pho crown while the newer spots were still hanging their neon. Clean, deep beef broth, the kind that fixes a gray afternoon. Walkable from Caltrain and the movie theater, so make an evening of it.

Six for a Peninsula pho crawl, El Camino to Broadway: Kevin's in Daly City, Ben Tre in South City, Pho de Nguyen in San Bruno, PPQ in Millbrae, Asian Box in Burlingame, Pho Dong in Redwood City. Bibs optional, appetite mandatory. Which bowl gets your rainy Tuesday vote?
