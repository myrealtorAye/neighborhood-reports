San Francisco Filipino Restaurants

Five San Francisco Filipino spots a local keeps in rotation, from a Tenderloin tasting counter to a Mission sisig burrito with a line out the door to turo turo pancit near Balboa Park. They stretch across SoMa, the Mission, the Tenderloin, and downtown, and every stop earns the trip.

The Sarap Shop — 171 Stillman St, San Francisco. Tucked into the SoMa Pilipinas cultural district, this family run spot keeps limited lunchtime cafe hours out of its test kitchen HQ, so timing is half the fun. The Impossible lumpia and halo halo built its name, and the crew runs a food stand at Chase Center too. Roll in early or roll in hungry. @thesarapshop

Senor Sisig — 990 Valencia St, San Francisco. What began as a 2010 food truck is now a Mission anchor for Filipino fusion, and the sisig burrito is the reason the line barely moves. Get it with garlic fries and do not overthink the rest. If Valencia is slammed, they also hold down the Ferry Building and Thrive City. @senorsisig

Ox and Tiger — 552 Jones St, San Francisco. An eight seat counter in a Tenderloin pocket where two chefs braid Filipino and Japanese cooking into a four course tasting menu, think kurobuta cheek with bagoong. Reservations only, Thursday through Sunday, three seatings a night, so it is a plan ahead kind of night out. Book the counter and let them drive. @oxandtiger

Turo Cafe and Grill — 2275 San Jose Ave, San Francisco. A no frills turo turo counter near Balboa Park where you point at what looks good and walk out full. Thrillist named their pancit the best in the Bay, and the regulars will not argue the call. Open mornings through early evening, closed Sundays, so keep it a weekday breakfast move. @turocafeandgrill

Foundation Cafe — 335 Kearny St, San Francisco. A family owned coffee shop in the Financial District plating California breakfast and lunch with a Filipino streak, from savory empanadas to the Filipino mango bars by the register. Call it the desk lunch hero hiding in plain sight downtown. Grab a mango bar with your coffee. @foundationcafe

Five for a San Francisco Filipino food crawl: The Sarap Shop, Senor Sisig, Ox and Tiger, Turo Cafe and Grill, and Foundation Cafe. Which neighborhood earns your first stop, SoMa or the Mission?
