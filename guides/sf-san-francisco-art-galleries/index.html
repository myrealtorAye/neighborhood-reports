San Francisco Art Galleries

The best free art in San Francisco is not behind the SFMOMA ticket counter. It is in the commercial galleries, and in 2016 a Dogpatch warehouse called Minnesota Street Project pulled the priced out ones together under a single roof so dealers could stay in the city. Eight rooms you walk into for nothing.

Jenkins Johnson Gallery — 1275 Minnesota St, San Francisco. Inside that same Minnesota Street Project building, Karen Jenkins Johnson has shown major contemporary artists since 1996, with real depth in work by the Black diaspora. Free to enter, museum level on the walls. @jenkinsjohnsongallery

Fraenkel Gallery — 49 Geary St, San Francisco. Four floors up in the old Geary Street gallery block sits one of the most respected photography galleries in the country, the kind that hangs Diane Arbus. Ride the elevator to the top and work your way down through the other galleries. @fraenkelgallery

Haines Gallery — 2 Marina Blvd, Building C, San Francisco. After decades downtown on Geary Street, Haines moved out to Fort Mason, swapping a high rise for a waterfront pier shed full of contemporary art with the Bay right outside. Pair it with a walk along the marina. @hainesgallery

Catharine Clark Gallery — 248 Utah St, San Francisco. Open since 1991 and the first gallery in the city with a dedicated media room, this Potrero Hill space shows politically sharp work and the video and new media art most dealers avoid. Give the back media room real time. @cclarkgallery

Creativity Explored — 3245 16th St, San Francisco. A Mission nonprofit since 1984 where artists with developmental disabilities make and sell their work, and the studio doubles as a gallery. The art is bold, affordable, and often the most joyful room in town. @creativityexplored

Gallery Wendi Norris — 436 Jackson St, San Francisco. A Jackson Square gallery that helped rewrite the history of Surrealism, with decades of shows built around Leonora Carrington and other overlooked women of the movement. Weekday hours, so slip in on a lunch break. @gallerywendinorris

Jessica Silverman — 621 Grant Ave, San Francisco. One of the city's leading contemporary dealers, now in a big corner space on the Chinatown edge of Grant Avenue, representing artists like feminist pioneer Judy Chicago. Bright, ambitious, and free to walk through. @jessicasilvermangallery

Berggruen Gallery — 10 Hawthorne St, San Francisco. John Berggruen opened in 1970 and became the first San Francisco gallery of national stature, and since 2017 it has sat directly across from SFMOMA, dealing modern masters from Picasso and Miro to Bay Area painters. See that caliber for free. @berggruengallery

Eight for San Francisco galleries: Jenkins Johnson, Fraenkel, Haines, Catharine Clark, Creativity Explored, Wendi Norris, Jessica Silverman, and Berggruen. Every one free to walk into. Which neighborhood are you starting in?
