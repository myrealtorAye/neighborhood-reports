Bay Area Peninsula Open Space Preserves

A green wall of protected ridgeline runs the length of the Peninsula, tens of thousands of acres saved from development between Silicon Valley and the sea. Eight Peninsula open space preserves to hike.

Rancho San Antonio — Los Altos. The Peninsula's most loved preserve, a walk to Deer Hollow Farm and a climb to a ridge, packed on weekends. The everyone goes there park.

Windy Hill — Portola Valley. Grassy twin summits with hang gliders overhead and a full bay to sea view, a lung testing climb. The big view hike.

Russian Ridge — La Honda. Rolling grassland that erupts in wildflowers each spring, Borel Hill the highest named point on the Peninsula. Come in April.

Purisima Creek Redwoods — Woodside. A redwood canyon dropping from Skyline down toward the coast, cool and green and steep. The redwood descent.

Wunderlich Park — Woodside. Shady redwood and oak trails on a former grand estate, popular with horses from the old stable. The gentle woods walk.

Huddart Park — Woodside. A redwood park with a climb up to Skyline and a nature loop, quiet and close to town. The easy forest day.

Edgewood Park — Redwood City. Serpentine grassland famous for spring wildflowers and rare native blooms, a moderate loop. The wildflower walk.

Sawyer Camp Trail — San Mateo. A paved lakeside path along Crystal Springs Reservoir past the ancient Jepson Laurel, flat and stroller friendly. The easy family ride.

Eight on the Peninsula: Rancho San Antonio, Windy Hill, Russian Ridge, Purisima Creek, Wunderlich, Huddart, Edgewood, and Sawyer Camp. Where do you start?
