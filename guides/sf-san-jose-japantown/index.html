San Francisco San Jose Japantown

San Jose's Japantown is among the last historic Japantowns in the United States, a few blocks that survived the wartime incarceration and never left. Eight San Jose Japantown stops.

Nichi Bei Bussan — 140 Jackson St. A Japanese goods store since 1902, kimono, fabric, and housewares over generations. Browse the fabric wall.

Shuei-Do Manju Shop — 217 Jackson St. A tiny shop making mochi and manju by hand for decades, a local treasure. Get a box of manju.

Roy's Station — 197 Jackson St. A coffee house in a restored 1930s gas station run by the founding family's descendants. Caffeinate in the old garage.

Minato — 617 N 6th St. A longtime family Japanese restaurant for tempura, teriyaki, and udon. The comfort dinner.

Kubota — Japantown. A classic Japanese American restaurant and sushi bar, a neighborhood mainstay. Sit at the counter.

Japanese American Museum of San Jose — 535 N 5th St. A museum telling the story of the community and the incarceration, moving and important. Take the time to read.

San Jose Buddhist Church Betsuin — 640 N 5th St. A grand temple at the heart of the community, host of the summer Obon festival. Time a visit for Obon.

Jackson Street — Jackson St. The walkable main strip of shops, restaurants, and a Sunday farmers market. Stroll the blocks.

Eight in Japantown: Nichi Bei Bussan, Shuei-Do Manju, Roy's Station, Minato, Kubota, the Japanese American Museum, the Buddhist temple, and Jackson Street. Where do you start?
