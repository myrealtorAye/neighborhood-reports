San Francisco Soul Food and Southern

Eight San Francisco soul food, Southern, and Creole kitchens still cooking the real thing, from a Richmond mainstay that opened in 1999 to a Bayview chef chasing a national gumbo. Everybody names the same couple of spots and misses the rest. Here they are, in the order you should eat your way through them.

Brenda's French Soul Food — 652 Polk Street, San Francisco. New Orleans by way of the Tenderloin, and the line out front is doing you a favor by buying you time to choose between the crawfish beignets and the shrimp grits. Get both. Open till 8, and it closes early on Tuesdays. @brendasfrenchsf

Brenda's Meat & Three — 919 Divisadero Street, San Francisco. The bigger sister on Divisadero runs on a Southern diner rule: one meat, three sides, and a long argument with yourself over which three. The heated back patio lets you linger from breakfast into supper. @brendasmeatandthree

Hard Knox Cafe — 2448 Clement Street, San Francisco. Chef Tony Hua opened the first Hard Knox in 1999, and the fried chicken and catfish still land piled past the edge of the plate. Bring an appetite you can be honest about and plan on a box to go. @hardknoxcafe

The Front Porch — 65 29th Street, San Francisco. On the Bernal and Mission line since 2006, this is the fried chicken and waffles you rearrange a night around, with beignets that show up under a snowdrift of powdered sugar and a tart raspberry dip. Dinner most nights, weekend brunch, and a room that fills the minute the doors swing open. @thefrontporchsf

Po'Boys Kitchen — 317 Connecticut Street, San Francisco. A Potrero Hill counter turning out Cajun-Creole plates, jambalaya, and dressed po'boys with a Bay Area lean. Small room, loud flavors, so call ahead if you want catering trays for the office. @poboyskitchen

Gumbo Social — 5176 3rd Street, San Francisco. Chef Dontaye Ball learned his roux from his grandmother, the one he calls The Big G, and now he wants gumbo named America's national dish from a corner of Bayview. Go Wednesday through Saturday and get the bowl with sausage. @gumbosocial

Old Skool Cafe — 1429 Mendell Street, San Francisco. A youth-run, jazz-themed supper club in Bayview where young people cook and serve international soul food while a live band plays nightly. Book a Wednesday through Saturday dinner and stay for the set. @oldskoolcafe

Peaches Patties — 1 Ferry Building, San Francisco. Chef Shani Jones bakes the flaky Jamaican patty the city did not know it was missing, packed with curried beef or callaloo, with jerk plates and rice and peas alongside. Grab one at the Ferry Building kiosk facing the water and eat it out on the pier. @peachespatties

Eight for soul food across San Francisco: Brenda's French Soul Food, Brenda's Meat & Three, Hard Knox, The Front Porch, Po'Boys Kitchen, Gumbo Social, Old Skool Cafe, and Peaches Patties. Which bowl, plate, or patty gets your Sunday first?
