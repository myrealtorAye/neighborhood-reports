San Francisco Himalayan and Nepalese Restaurants

Five Himalayan and Nepalese tables that prove San Francisco does momos as well as any city. Everyone races to the same Indian spots and walks right past the Nepali kitchens quietly steaming dumplings a few doors down. The city's first Nepalese restaurant opened here back in 2003, and the scene only got better. Start here.

Little Nepal — 925 Cortland Ave, San Francisco. The one that started it all, San Francisco's first Nepalese restaurant, anchoring Bernal's walkable Cortland strip since 2003. White tablecloths, warm welcome, momos and clay oven kukhura that regulars have loved for two decades. Book ahead on weekends, it is small.

Bini's Kitchen — 1001 Howard St, San Francisco. Chef Binita Pradhan came up through La Cocina and became the city's unofficial queen of momos, hand folding thousands of turkey and lamb dumplings a week in this bright SoMa room. Get the Khana plate and the famous tomato timur sauce. Lunch is the move. @bini_kitchen

Dancing Yak — 280 Valencia St, San Francisco. Nepali comfort food meets a real cocktail bar on lower Valencia, open since 2018 and built for a night out. Momos, sizzling platters and craft drinks under one roof in one of the city's most walkable pockets. Come hungry, share the momo sampler, stay for happy hour. @dancingyaksf_

Cuisine of Nepal — 3486 Mission St, San Francisco. A warm little room on the Mission stretch below Bernal, bringing Himalayan comfort to the table, momos, dal bhat and steaming bowls of thukpa built for a cold night. Featured on Check, Please Bay Area, and vegan friendly. Open evenings Tuesday through Sunday, so plan dinner and get the jhol momo. @cuisineofnepal

Sunset Indian Cuisine — 642 Irving St, San Francisco. Where the Inner Sunset goes for Himalayan and Indian comfort near Golden Gate Park, momos and tandoori and curry served generously and late. A dependable neighborhood table when the fog settles in over Irving. Go with a crew, order across the menu, take the leftovers home.

Five for a Himalayan and Nepalese run through San Francisco: Little Nepal, Bini's Kitchen, Dancing Yak, Cuisine of Nepal, Sunset Indian Cuisine. From the city's first Nepali dining room to late night momos in the Sunset, the dumplings travel well. Who makes your favorite momo?
