San Francisco Marin Bakeries and Pastry

Five Marin bakeries worth the drive, ranked by how hard it is to leave with only one thing. From Larkspur out to Point Reyes Station, this is the pastry crawl locals text each other about whenever someone new moves to the county.

Rustic Bakery — 1139 Magnolia Ave, Larkspur. The Magnolia shop is where the whole thing started in 2005, those sourdough flatbread crackers that now turn up on cheese boards statewide. Grab a morning bun and an Equator coffee, claim a patio seat, and watch Larkspur wake up. Weekend parking is its own sport. @rusticbakery

Emporio Rulli — 464 Magnolia Ave, Larkspur. Gary Rulli trained under pastry chefs in Milan and Turin, then brought it all home to downtown Larkspur in 1988, so the espresso and panettone taste like a detour to Italy. Order a sfogliatella, sit under the awning, pretend your afternoon has nowhere to be. @emporiorulli

M.H — Bread and Butter. 101 San Anselmo Avenue, San Anselmo. Organic flour, local farm eggs, and a laminated pastry case that thins out fast by mid-morning. The kouign amann is the move, though the croissants vanish for a reason. Come by 8, linger on the avenue, and you see why San Anselmo kept the kind of downtown people pay up to live near. @mhbreadbutter

Flour Craft Bakery — 129 Miller Ave, Mill Valley. Marin's gluten free bakery hides inside the Mill Valley Lumber Yard, and the tell is that people who can eat gluten still line up here. Scones, focaccia, seasonal galettes, none of the sad cardboard energy the category tends to carry. An easy post-hike stop near Old Mill Park. @flourcraft

Brickmaiden Breads — 40 4th Street, Point Reyes Station. Baking artisan sourdough and pastry in West Marin since 2000, tucked a block off the main drag where the whole town smells like a bake oven by 8am. Grab a morning bun and a loaf for later, then wander Point Reyes Station before the Saturday crowd rolls in from the coast. @brickmaidenbreads

Five for the Marin pastry crawl: Rustic and Emporio Rulli in Larkspur, M.H. Bread and Butter in San Anselmo, Flour Craft in Mill Valley, Brickmaiden out in Point Reyes Station. Which one earns your first stop, and who did I leave off?
