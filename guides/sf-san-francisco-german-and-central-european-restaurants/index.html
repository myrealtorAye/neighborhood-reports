San Francisco German and Central European Restaurants

Eight German and Central European tables worth staying in San Francisco for. Everyone assumes you leave the city for a real schnitzel or a proper boot of pilsner. You do not. From a Bavarian beer hall that outlived the 1906 quake to a Swiss chalet on Van Ness, the whole spread is here, FiDi to Fort Mason to the Mission. Where to start.

Suppenkuche — 525 Laguna St, San Francisco. The Hayes Valley anchor that opened in 1993 when nobody wanted the block, and still packs long communal tables for spaetzle, schnitzel and a wall of German taps. Go with a group, order the potato pancakes, make friends with strangers. @suppenkuchesf

Schroeder's — 240 Front St, San Francisco. Founded 1893, rebuilt after the 1906 fire, and still the Financial District's Bavarian beer hall, all dark wood, communal benches and liter steins. Duck in for the pork schnitzel and a cold German lager when downtown empties out. It has more character than anything on the block.

Biergarten — 424 Octavia St, San Francisco. Suppenkuche's little outdoor sibling, a gravel pocket in Hayes Valley with picnic tables, pretzels the size of your head and German beer poured till the fog rolls in. Grab a bratwurst, claim a bench, stay for one more. It is the move on a rare sunny afternoon. @biergarten_sf

Radhaus — 2 Marina Blvd, Building A, San Francisco. Tucked inside Fort Mason with a 30 foot bar and Golden Gate views, this Alpine beer hall does doppelbock, giant pretzels and proper bratwurst under one very handsome roof. Come at golden hour, get the sausage sampler, watch the bridge light up. @radhaus_sf

Rosamunde Sausage Grill — 2832 Mission St, San Francisco. A Mission fixture since 1998, this tiny German grill turns out currywurst, bratwurst and a rotating vegan sausage next to a serious craft beer list. Tuesday trivia gets loud and local. Grab a duck sausage and a Schneider Weisse and post up.

Paprika — 3324 24th St, San Francisco. The Mission's little taste of Prague and Budapest, family run since 2013, with a two time Michelin recommended pork goulash and Czech pilsner served in glass boots. Vegan options too. Bring people, order the goulash and a boot, stay for the live Balkan nights. @paprika_sf_restaurant

Matterhorn — 2323 Van Ness Ave, San Francisco. A full Swiss chalet hiding on Van Ness, wood beams and all, built around bubbling pots of cheese and chocolate fondue. This is a cold foggy night in edible form. Bring your person, order the Emmentaler fondue, take your time with the long forks. @matterhornsf

Europa Wine Bar and Kitchen — 4318 California St, San Francisco. A cozy Inner Richmond corner doing Eastern and Central European comfort food, schnitzel, pierogi and goulash, paired with rare Old World wines you will not find elsewhere. Low light, small room, great for a date. Go early, order the pierogi, let them pick the wine.

Eight for a German and Central European crawl across San Francisco: Suppenkuche, Schroeder's, Biergarten, Radhaus, Rosamunde, Paprika, Matterhorn, Europa. Beer halls, sausage grills, fondue and goulash, no bridge required. Which one are you starting on?
