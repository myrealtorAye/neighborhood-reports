San Francisco French Bistros

Seven San Francisco French bistros that never chase a trend, they just keep serving cassoulet. One has kept the same pot going more than eighteen thousand days. Skip the hyped new openings for a night. These are the neighborhood rooms where escargot, steak frites and a glass of Burgundy still feel like a small trip to Paris, no passport required.

Zazie — 941 Cole Street, San Francisco. A Cole Valley bistro built for slow mornings, American style brunch and Provencal dinners, with a back garden patio that feels miles from the fog. Brunch runs first come first served, so put your name down and wander Cole Street while you wait. Dogs are welcome in the garden.

Chez Maman — 1401 18th Street, San Francisco. A neighborhood French bistro locals have leaned on long enough that it now comes in two, the original on Potrero Hill and a second in Hayes Valley at 401 Gough. Unfussy and warm, the kind of established corner room you return to without thinking. If 18th Street is full, point yourself toward Gough.

Chapeau — 126 Clement Street, San Francisco. A husband and wife team have run this Inner Richmond bistro since 1996, and it shows in the escargots, the coq au vin and the cassoulet. Closed Mondays and Tuesdays, so make it a Wednesday through Sunday plan and save room for the creme brulee. @chapeau.sf

Absinthe — 398 Hayes Street, San Francisco. A Hayes Valley mainstay since 1998, this brasserie pairs turn of the century Paris decor with French classics and a serious list of cocktails. It sits in the middle of the arts district, so it is an easy pre show table or a late convivial one. The bar is a fine solo perch. @absinthesf

Chouquet's — 2500 Washington Street, San Francisco. A corner French bistro in Pacific Heights built for people watching, sidewalk tables for brunch, a banquette for a long dinner, a bar for a quick lunch. Weekends bring live jazz from 12 to 4, and most of the menu stays thirty dollars and under. Unpretentious and easy.

Le Central — 453 Bush Street, San Francisco. Opened in 1974, this is the oldest French bistro in the city, a one story brick holdout with a pink neon sign near Union Square. The cassoulet has been going for more than eighteen thousand days. Order the steak frites or the moules and settle in where regulars have sat for fifty years.

Cafe de la Presse — 352 Grant Avenue, San Francisco. Right across from the Chinatown gate, this bistro and old newsstand has anchored its corner of the old French Quarter for decades. A 1920s dining room, a full bar of French spirits, and a terrace made for a coffee and a quiche. Open all seven days, breakfast through dinner. @cafedelapressesf

Seven for a French night in San Francisco: Zazie in Cole Valley, Chez Maman on Potrero Hill, Chapeau in the Richmond, Absinthe in Hayes Valley, Chouquet's in Pac Heights, Le Central by Union Square, Cafe de la Presse at the Chinatown gate. Which one gets your Friday?
