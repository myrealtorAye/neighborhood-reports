San Francisco Thai Restaurants

Eight San Francisco Thai restaurants that reward you for skipping the pad thai autopilot. Two carry Michelin stars, one's fed the Inner Sunset since 1986, and none of them are the chain you're picturing. From Union Square to Glen Park, here's the short list worth crossing town for.

Kin Khao

55 Cyril Magnin St, San Francisco. Chef Pim Techamuanvivit tucks a Michelin star into the Parc 55 hotel lobby, which might be the most San Francisco move on this whole list. Come for the pink crab rice and the kua kling, and make peace with Union Square parking on the way in. Book on Resy early, since walk ups rarely land a table.

Nari

1625 Post St, San Francisco. Pim's second act lives inside Hotel Kabuki in Japantown, and it earned its own Michelin star in 2023, so the family holds two. This is the splurge end of Thai, all coconut and live fire and cocktails that photograph better than you do. Save it for the anniversary, then valet and glide in. @nari_sf

Lers Ros

307 Hayes St, San Francisco. This Hayes Valley room has fed the symphony crowd since chef Tom Silargorn carried his Cholburi recipes west in 2008. Push past the safe stuff into the wild boar, the fermented sausage, the whole fried fish, and name your spice level with respect. Grab a table before the curtain and you'll still make the show. @lersros

Farmhouse Kitchen Thai

710 Florida St, San Francisco. Chef Kasem won Chopped, then turned this Mission warehouse into a neon jungle where the drinks arrive smoking and the fried chicken comes with roti. It's loud, it's a scene, and the hat yai chicken earns every decibel. Bring a group, book ahead on weekends, sit outside if the fog naps. @thaifarmhouse

Marnee Thai

1243 9th Ave, San Francisco. Chef Chai has run this Inner Sunset staple since 1986, back when the block was cheaper and the fog was exactly the same. The angel wings and the coconut heavy curries are why regulars guard their usual tables. Walk over from Golden Gate Park with an appetite and cash for the tip.

Osha Thai

4 Embarcadero Center, San Francisco. Chef Lalita Souksamlane opened her first Osha on Geary back in 1996 and grew it into the name every downtown office already knows. The Embarcadero flagship pairs swanky plating and steady curries with a Bay view for the after work crowd. Grab a window table at sunset and let someone else expense it. @oshathai

Nakorn Thai

639 Clement St, San Francisco. This Inner Richmond hole in the wall took over the old King of Thai space, and the open kitchen still sends out fried rice under a crisp chicken cutlet that regulars order on repeat. Thirty years of wok time hide behind a plain storefront, so trust the char. Closed Thursdays, so time the craving around it.

One Waan Thai

2922 Diamond St, San Francisco. Step off the Glen Park BART and the kitchen plates Thai fusion with sweeping city views, the rare neighborhood spot that feels like fine dining without the downtown markup. Think spicy basil with Angus and tom kha that arrives in a little flaming pot. Reserve for dinner, since they open at 4:30 and skip Sundays. @onewaanthai

Eight for the San Francisco Thai short list: Kin Khao and Nari for the Michelin splurge, Lers Ros for Hayes Valley, Farmhouse for the scene, Marnee for the 1986 classic, Osha on the Embarcadero, Nakorn on Clement, One Waan in Glen Park. Which neighborhood did I miss?
