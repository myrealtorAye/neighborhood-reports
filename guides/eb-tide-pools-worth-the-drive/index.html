East Bay Tide Pools Worth the Drive

The East Bay's own tide pools sit right at Crown Beach in Alameda, and the deeper ones are a short drive over the hill to the San Mateo coast. Time it to a minus tide and the kids forget the iPad exists. Eight tide-pool spots, from the Alameda shoreline out to the coast.

Crown Memorial State Beach — 8th St and Otis Dr, Alameda. The East Bay's most accessible tide pools, beginner-friendly with parking right at the sand. Best at minus tides.

Crab Cove Visitor Center — 1252 McKay Ave, Alameda. Next to Crown Beach, with staff-led tide-pool walks on low-tide weekends.

Point Pinole Regional Shoreline — 5551 Giant Hwy, Richmond. A long shoreline with rocky pockets and far fewer crowds than the Alameda spots. Bring water shoes. @ebrpd

Pillar Point — 1 Johnson Pier, Half Moon Bay. Worth the 45-minute drive; the pools are deeper and the variety bigger. Minus-tide weekends are the move.

Fitzgerald Marine Reserve — 200 Nevada Ave, Moss Beach. Among the most famous tide pools in the region, with ranger-led walks on weekends.

Bean Hollow State Beach — 11000 Cabrillo Hwy, Pescadero. Smaller and less crowded, with rock formations that make it the photo stop.

Pescadero State Beach — Cabrillo Hwy, Pescadero. Tide pools south of the main beach; pair it with Pescadero Marsh for a half-day.

Ano Nuevo State Park — 1 New Years Creek Rd, Pescadero. Tide pools plus elephant seals in season; the guided walks December through March are the trip to plan around.

Eight for the minus tide: Crown Beach, Crab Cove, Point Pinole, Pillar Point, Fitzgerald, Bean Hollow, Pescadero, and Ano Nuevo. Pack water shoes and a tide app. What did your kid find last time?
