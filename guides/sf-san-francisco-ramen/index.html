San Francisco Ramen

Eight San Francisco ramen shops worth the fogged-up windows and the wait, and not one is a mall-food-court chain. The slurp order runs Japantown to the Mission to the Marina, tonkotsu to dashi to a beef-bone bowl almost nobody in town makes. Bring a napkin and a little patience. Save this before the next cold night rolls in off the ocean.

Marufuku Ramen

1581 Webster St, San Francisco. The Japantown mall hides a Hakata tonkotsu so thick and creamy it clings to the hair-thin noodles like a secret. Put your name down, wander the bookstore upstairs, and drift back when they text you. The wait here is a rite of passage, not a warning. @marufukuramenofficial

Nojo Ramen Tavern

231 Franklin St, San Francisco. The whole point is chicken, a golden paitan so silky it makes pork broth feel loud. Order a few yakitori skewers while you wait and you have built yourself a little Tokyo-alley dinner in Hayes Valley. Dim, cozy, and made for a rainy Tuesday with nowhere to be. @nojoramen

Mensho Tokyo

672 Geary St, San Francisco. This is the Michelin-listed one, a Tokyo legend's first US shop, and the line out front tells you the rest. The tori paitan is the move, though the lamb bowl has a cult that shows up at opening on purpose. Go early or make peace with standing on Geary a while. @mensho.usa

Taishoken

665 Valencia St, San Francisco. The Tokyo shop that invented tsukemen back in 1951 planted its flag on Valencia, so order the dipping noodles, not the standard bowl. Cold chewy noodles, a small cup of intense pork-and-fish broth, and you dunk. It feels like a hack the first time and a habit by the second. @taishoken.sf

Izakaya Sozai

1500 Irving St, San Francisco. A pint-sized Inner Sunset room where the tonkotsu quietly out-punches shops triple its size. Come for the ramen, stay for the yakitori and a cold Sapporo, and book ahead because the locals guard this table. Foggy-night comfort with a wait list attached. @izakayasozaisf

Ushi Taro Ramen

343 8th St, San Francisco. The rebel of the group runs on beef instead of pork, a rich gyukotsu broth with slices of brisket where the chashu usually sits. If you have slurped every tonkotsu in the city and want a new page, this SoMa spot is your reset button. Small room, loud flavor. @ushi_taro_ramen

Saiwaii Ramen

2240 Irving St, San Francisco. Deep in the Outer Sunset fog sits a neighborhood workhorse turning out honest tonkotsu and a spicy garlic bowl the regulars order on repeat. No scene, no gimmicks, portions that quietly end your night. The kind of place that makes a gray avenue feel like home. @saiwaiiramen

Hinodeya Ramen

3340 Steiner St, San Francisco. Family-style dashi ramen near Chestnut, lighter and cleaner than the tonkotsu crowd, built on bonito and kombu instead of pork fat. The wontons and the sudachi bowl are the tells that someone back there cares. Bright, breezy, and kinder on the walk-in wait. @hinodeya_sf

Eight San Francisco ramen bowls, one map: Marufuku (Japantown tonkotsu), Nojo (Hayes Valley chicken paitan), Mensho (Tenderloin, Michelin), Taishoken (Mission tsukemen), Izakaya Sozai (Inner Sunset), Ushi Taro (SoMa beef broth), Saiwaii (Outer Sunset), Hinodeya (Marina dashi). Save it, pick your fog level, go slurp. Which bowl gets your vote?
