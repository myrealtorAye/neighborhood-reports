San Francisco Hot Pot and Shabu

Eight San Francisco hot pot and shabu rooms where you cook dinner yourself and leave prouder than when you walked in. Fog outside, steam inside, the whole city misted over while your broth goes to work. From Chinatown to Japantown to the far end of the Sunset, here is where locals dip.

Nabe — 1325 9th Ave, San Francisco. Inner Sunset shabu where the broth is the whole personality and the sake list keeps pace. Organic meat, tidy pots, that foggy-avenue warmth the second you slide into a booth. Open Wednesday through Sunday, so plan the craving around it. @nabe.sf

Hotpot Champ — 801 Kearny St, San Francisco. All-you-can-eat on the Chinatown edge, where the beef and lamb roll out on a literal Ferris wheel and nobody judges a third plate. Bring the friends who order like it is a contest. Kitchen runs past midnight on weekends. @hotpot_champ_sf

Dragon Beaux — 5700 Geary Blvd, San Francisco. The Koi Palace family's Outer Richmond room does dim sum by day, then flips to hot pot at night with live seafood, Wagyu, and geoduck that arrives still forming an opinion of you. Reserve ahead, the lines are a Richmond institution. @dragonbeaux

Fiery Hot Pot and Grill — 2333 Irving St, San Francisco. Outer Sunset spot running smokeless hot pot and Korean BBQ on one table, A5 Wagyu on the menu, so your jacket will not smell like dinner tomorrow. All-you-can-eat, come hungry and a little competitive. @fiery_sf

Matsuyama Shabu House — 1726 Buchanan St, San Francisco. Japantown shabu done the quiet, careful way, thin Wagyu swirled through clean broth with an izakaya lean and a Michelin nod. Closed Tuesdays, packed on weekend nights, so book a table before the Japan Center crowd beats you to it. @matsuyamashabuhousesf

IPOT — 1420 Irving St, San Francisco. A bi-level Inner Sunset favorite since 2015, pairing all-you-can-eat hot pot with Korean BBQ on the same table and eighteen house broths to argue over. Grills and pots going at once, so the indecisive friend finally gets a win. Weekends fill fast. @ipot.food

Boiling Hot Pot — 5512 Geary Blvd, San Francisco. A Richmond all-you-can-eat where you tap four broths into one pot on an iPad and race a ninety-minute clock. Efficient, generous, priced like the neighbors want you back next week. Come early on a Friday, word got out a while ago.

Nan Hot Pot SF — 501 Broadway, San Francisco. Sichuan hot pot on the North Beach and Chinatown seam, mala broth with a numbing kick and a tidy ingredient list that respects a smaller table. Good for two, better for a booth of four after a night out on Broadway. @nanhotpotsf

Eight for steam season across San Francisco: shabu in the Sunset, AYCE off Chinatown, banquet hot pot in the Richmond, Japantown Wagyu, smokeless by the beach, mala on Broadway. Which broth gets you first when the fog rolls in?
