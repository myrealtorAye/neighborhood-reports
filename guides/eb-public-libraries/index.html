East Bay Public Libraries

The Berkeley and Oakland libraries both lend power tools, not just books, and most residents go years without finding out. Your card gets you into museums free too. Ten East Bay libraries with more going on than the checkout desk.

Berkeley Public Library

2090 Kittredge St, Berkeley. The art deco landmark downtown, a reading room that makes you smarter just sitting in it, and a Saturday tool lending library most people never clock. @friendsbpl

Oakland Main Library

125 14th St, Oakland. The flagship by the lake with the Oakland History Center upstairs, plus a Tool Lending Library and a Seed Library new residents miss for years. @oaklibrary

Lafayette Library and Learning Center

3491 Mt Diablo Blvd, Lafayette. The 2009 stone and glass showpiece, half library and half community campus, and the Friday film series is the local secret.

Hayward Public Library

888 C St, Hayward. The 21st Century building with a maker space, a recording booth, and a public tech lab, the branch built for right now. @haywardlibrary

Alameda Free Library

1550 Oak St, Alameda. The island's main branch, a big light filled atrium and a kids floor that earns the bike ride across the estuary.

San Leandro Main Library

300 Estudillo Ave, San Leandro. A freshly expanded children's wing, regular author talks, and a Friends bookstore in the basement for the bargain dig. @sanleandrolib

Rockridge Branch

5366 College Ave, Oakland. Walk in study space and a steady author calendar, with a children's section that punches well above a branch its size.

Walnut Creek Library

1644 N Broadway, Walnut Creek. The modern flagship beside Civic Park, floor to ceiling glass and a hold shelf that turns over fast.

Pleasanton Public Library

400 Old Bernal Ave, Pleasanton. The summer reading program pulls the whole Tri-Valley in, and the local history room runs deeper than you would guess. @pleasantonlibandrec

Lakeview Branch

550 El Embarcadero, Oakland. Storytimes for the under fives Tuesday and Saturday mornings, when the toddler crowd claims the rug early.

Ten branches worth the trip: Berkeley, Oakland Main, Lafayette, Hayward, Alameda, San Leandro, Rockridge, Walnut Creek, Pleasanton, and Lakeview. Which library program do you actually use?
