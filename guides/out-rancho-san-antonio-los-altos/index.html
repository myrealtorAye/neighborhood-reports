Bay Area Rancho San Antonio, Los Altos

Eight ways to spend a morning at Rancho San Antonio, the South Bay preserve everyone in Los Altos and Cupertino treats as their backyard. Over 25 miles of trail, a working farm, wild turkeys in the meadow, and a lot that fills by 8am on weekends. Dogs stay home here and water taps are scarce, so pack your own. Here is how to do it right, from easy stroll to real climb.

PG and E Trail — The wide gravel road most people start on, flat and easy the first mile out to the farm, friendly for strollers and little kids. Keep going and it drops the gentle act: the full climb runs 3.7 miles and 1,600 feet up to Black Mountain, fully exposed. Walk the easy mile, or commit to the grind and earn the Bay views up top.

Deer Hollow Farm — A 160-year-old working homestead a flat mile in, with pigs, goats, sheep, chickens and a cow, plus the restored Grant Cabin set up like the late 1800s. Free to walk through and a reliable bribe for anyone you dragged along under four feet tall. Open Tuesday to Sunday 8 to 4, Wednesday until 1, closed Mondays.

Rogue Valley Trail — The shaded creek stretch, cool and green when the exposed hills are baking, poppies and lupine along the edges in spring. This is the section the preserve puts on its own postcards, and you will see why. Take it as the mellow way to loop back, or the smart move on a hot afternoon when the ridge turns into an oven.

Wildcat Loop Trail — A three-mile loop, moderate, that follows the creek into a cool fern-walled canyon, climbs through chaparral to open meadows on the ridge, then drops you back at the farm. On a clear day the meadow hands you Mount Tam to the north and Mount Hamilton to the east. The classic Rancho half-day if you run only one loop.

High Meadow Trail — The climb that trades shade for a payoff, up through grassland to a wide-open ridge meadow where the whole South Bay spreads out below you. Moderate, and you feel the elevation in your legs. String it with Upper Wildcat and PG and E for a roughly 6.5-mile loop. Go on a clear winter morning when the air is scrubbed and the views run for miles.

Upper Wildcat Canyon Trail — Push past where the crowds turn around and this becomes the prettiest, quietest part of the preserve, deep canyon and oak woodland with barely a soul on it. Fold it into the full 7.6-mile Wildcat Canyon Loop, moderate, topping out near 1,050 feet with South Bay vistas. This is where Rancho stops feeling like a city park.

Coyote Trail — Down in the county park, an oak-shaded lower route and the one stretch open to horses, so expect the occasional rider. Easy to moderate, good shade, a calmer alternative when the main drag is packed with strollers and trail runners. Keep your eyes up, coyotes and wild turkeys both work this side of the park.

Chamise Trail to the Windmill Pasture — A 2.1-mile gradual climb to a tranquil hidden meadow at the north end, next to Hidden Villa, where a ranch windmill once stood. Bring a sandwich, this is the picnic spot most Rancho regulars never find. Crews cleared a fuel break along Chamise recently, so parts feel open and sunny now. One of the best quiet corners here.

Eight ways through Rancho San Antonio: PG and E Trail for the easy mile, Deer Hollow Farm for the animals, Rogue Valley for shade, Wildcat Loop for the classic, High Meadow for the views, Upper Wildcat Canyon for quiet, Coyote for the oaks, Chamise for the hidden meadow. Check openspace.org before you leave, come early or midday, bring water, leave the dog home. See you out there.
