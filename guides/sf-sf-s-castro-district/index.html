San Francisco SF's Castro District

The Castro is where Harvey Milk ran a camera shop and won office as an early openly gay elected official, in the country's most famous gay neighborhood. Eight Castro stops.

Castro Theatre — 429 Castro St. The 1922 movie palace with a mighty Wurlitzer organ, a neighborhood landmark freshly restored. Catch a screening or a singalong.

Harvey Milk's Castro Camera — 575 Castro St. The storefront where Milk ran his shop and campaigns, now marked with a mural and plaque. Stand where history was made.

Twin Peaks Tavern — 401 Castro St. The bar with the big plate glass windows, said to be the first gay bar in the country you could see into from the street. Toast at the corner.

Anchor Oyster Bar — 579 Castro St. A tiny marble counter serving oysters and cioppino since 1977, no reservations, worth the wait. Slurp a dozen.

Cliff's Variety — 479 Castro St. A beloved hardware and everything store for 80 years, glitter and lightbulbs side by side. Browse the wonderful clutter.

Hot Cookie — 407 Castro St. A cheeky little bakery famous for warm cookies late into the night. Grab a bag for the walk.

GLBT Historical Society Museum — 4127 18th St. A small museum of queer history with rotating exhibits, the first of its kind in the country. See the archives.

Rainbow Honor Walk — Castro St. Bronze sidewalk plaques honoring LGBTQ figures along the strip, plus the giant flag at Harvey Milk Plaza. Read the sidewalk.

Eight in the Castro: the Castro Theatre, Milk's camera store, Twin Peaks Tavern, Anchor Oyster Bar, Cliff's Variety, Hot Cookie, the GLBT museum, and the Rainbow Honor Walk. Where do you start?
