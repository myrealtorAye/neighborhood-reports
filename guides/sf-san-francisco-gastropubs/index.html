San Francisco Gastropubs

Eight San Francisco gastropubs where the beer list runs as deep as the dinner menu and nobody makes you pick one. From the Haight to North Beach to SoMa, this is where locals land when the fog rolls in and a good burger and a proper pour both sound right.

The Alembic

1725 Haight St, San Francisco. Upstairs from the Haight Street chaos, this narrow room pours a whiskey list longer than most lease agreements and plates small bites that punch well above dive-bar weight. The duck confit and a barrel-aged cocktail is the move. Grab a stool at the tin-topped bar before the after-work crowd claims them. @alembicsf

Magnolia Brewing

1398 Haight St, San Francisco. The neighborhood brewpub came back from the dead in 2024, and the basement tanks are pouring Kalifornia Kolsch and Proving Ground IPA again like nothing happened. House beer, a burger built for two hands, cocktails now too. Corner of Haight and Masonic, kitchen open til 10. Post up. @magnoliahaight

Comstock Saloon

155 Columbus Ave, San Francisco. The last standing bar of the Barbary Coast, all 1907 mahogany and pressed tin, where the bartenders take their classics as seriously as the kitchen takes its oysters Kirkpatrick. Come for a Martinez and stay for the pork chop. Aim for the back booths, the front fills fast at happy hour. @comstocksaloon

Bartlett Hall

242 O'Farrell St, San Francisco. A block off Union Square, this cavernous brick brewhouse makes its own beer and pours barrel-aged cocktails, a small miracle in a neighborhood built for tourists and overpriced hotel bars. The wings and a house lager after a day downtown earn their keep. Big groups fit easy, so bring the crew. @bartletthallsf

The Sycamore

2140 Mission St, San Francisco. Push through to the hidden back patio and you will forget you are on Mission Street at all. Rotating craft drafts, a kitchen slinging solid burgers and vegan plates, trivia Mondays and bingo Wednesdays for the regulars. It is the kind of low-key corner every neighborhood hopes it lands. Go on a sunny afternoon. @sycamorebeer

Southern Pacific Brewing

620 Treat Ave, San Francisco. A cathedral-sized Mission warehouse with a mezzanine, a dog-friendly patio, house pilsners and IPAs, and a kitchen leaning Cajun. This is your spot when a party of ten needs beer, burgers, and room to breathe, which the Mission rarely offers. Claim the patio before the sun drops. @southernpacificbrewing

The Willows

1582 Folsom St, San Francisco. A SoMa gastropub with a craft-beer room, a full bar, board games, and a pinball machine or two for when the conversation lags. The kitchen holds its own past midnight, a rare thing in this city. Rotating drafts, cozy back corners, open til 2 most nights. Land here when dinner runs late. @thewillowssf

The Pig and Whistle

2801 Geary Blvd, San Francisco. A proper British pub on Geary since 1991, pouring ales from around the world with fish and chips, bangers and mash, and shepherd's pie that tastes like it has nothing to prove. Trivia Wednesdays and Sundays, three-dollar happy-hour pints, dartboard in the back. Duck in out of the Richmond fog. @pigwhistlesf

Eight for when you want the pint and the plate to matter equally: Alembic, Magnolia, Comstock, Bartlett Hall, Sycamore, Southern Pacific, Willows, and Pig and Whistle. Which SF neighborhood still hides your favorite beer-and-a-real-meal spot?
