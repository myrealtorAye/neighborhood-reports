East Bay Circus and Aerial Arts

Modern aerial dance was invented in Berkeley. Terry Sendgraff hung the first single point spinning trapeze here in 1975 and launched a whole art form, and her low flying trapeze is still taught a mile away. Seven East Bay studios where you can actually leave the ground.

Kinetic Arts Center — 785 7th St, Oakland. A full nonprofit circus school since 2008, trapeze, silks, rope, hoop, and hand balancing under one West Oakland warehouse roof, drop in or sign up. @kineticartscenter

UpSwing Aerial Dance — 2525 Eighth St, Berkeley. Carrying Terry Sendgraff's low flying Motivity trapeze lineage since 2005, small classes in the Sawtooth Building. @upswingdance

Athletic Playground — 4770 San Pablo Ave, Emeryville. Fifty plus movement classes a week since 2008, silks, trapeze, lyra, acro, even parkour, for total beginners on up. @athleticplayground

Berkeley Pole and Dance — 1636 University Ave, Berkeley. Pole and aerial from true beginner to advanced on a sliding scale that keeps it open to everyone. @berkeleypoleanddance

Oakland Pole and Dance — 366 Grand Ave, Oakland. The Adams Point studio where a first timer books pole, silks, or lyra and learns to trust their own grip.

Skyhigh Odditorium — 1 W Barrett Ave, Richmond. An adults focused silks and hoop school that also stages dark circus variety shows, since 2012. @skyhighodditorium

The Crucible — 1260 7th St, Oakland. The country's largest nonprofit industrial arts school, where the aerial rig shares a building with fire performance and fire dancing. @the_crucible

Seven to fly: Kinetic Arts, UpSwing, Athletic Playground, Berkeley Pole, Oakland Pole, Skyhigh, and the Crucible. Silks, trapeze, or fire?
