East Bay Hiking, Worth Crossing the Bay for

The pole at the summit of Mission Peak is the most photographed spot in Alameda County, and the climb to it built East Bay hiking culture. These are the trails worth crossing a bridge for, the ones Marin does not have. Eight of them.

Mission Peak — Stanford Ave staging, 43600 Mission Blvd, Fremont. A roughly five-mile round trip to a 2,517-foot summit, and the pole at the top half of Alameda County has a photo with. @ebrpd

Mount Diablo via Mitchell Canyon — 96 Mitchell Canyon Rd, Clayton, in Mount Diablo State Park. The signature East Bay climb. On the clearest days the summit view reaches the Sierra crest. The seven-county look earns the work.

Sunol Regional Wilderness — 1895 Geary Rd, Sunol. A four-mile loop through grassland to a granite gorge the district itself calls Little Yosemite. The hidden canyon most Bay Area hikers never make the drive for.

Briones Crest Loop — Bear Creek staging, Briones Regional Park, Lafayette. About six miles of rolling green hills over three summits, with cattle, wildflowers, and Mount Diablo in the distance. The all-day workout.

Las Trampas Rocky Ridge — 18012 Bollinger Canyon Rd, San Ramon. A steep climb to a 2,000-foot ridge with the Bay to the west and the Central Valley to the east. The hard East Bay hike most people overlook.

Black Diamond Mines Regional Preserve — 5175 Somersville Rd, Antioch. Old coal-mining trails through some of the East Bay's strangest terrain, past Rose Hill Cemetery and abandoned mine shafts.

Pleasanton Ridge — Augustin Bernal staging, 8200 Golden Eagle Way, Pleasanton. Miles of ridge trail with olive groves, circling hawks, and the South Bay laid out below. The longest unobstructed views around.

Coyote Hills Regional Park — 8000 Patterson Ranch Rd, Fremont. The marsh-and-grassland combo hikers skip for the bigger names, with Ohlone shellmounds and sunset over the Bay.

Eight worth the drive: Mission Peak, Mount Diablo via Mitchell Canyon, Sunol, Briones Crest, Las Trampas, Black Diamond Mines, Pleasanton Ridge, and Coyote Hills. Which one finally sold you on this side of the Bay?
