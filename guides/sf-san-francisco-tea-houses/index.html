San Francisco Tea Houses

Imperial Tea Court opened in 1993 as the first traditional Chinese teahouse in America, and it still pours in San Francisco's Ferry Building. Skip the souvenir tins stacked in the Grant Avenue windows. These are the counters where the tasting is free and the leaf is single origin. Eight tea houses worth slowing down for.

Red Blossom Tea Company

831 Grant Ave, San Francisco. The Luong family flies to China and Taiwan every year to buy direct, so the hundred plus teas on the wall are sorted by harvest and varietal like a wine list. Ask for a sample size before you commit to a whole tin. @redblossomteacompany

Vital Tea Leaf

1044 Grant Ave, San Francisco. The Chinatown shop that runs on the hard sell of hospitality, steeping you cup after cup for free until an oolong or an aged pu erh finally clicks. Go with time and an open mind, not a shopping list.

Imperial Tea Court

1 Ferry Building, San Francisco. The Fongs' teahouse still runs full gongfu service at a table in the Ferry Building, tiny clay pots, many short steeps, no rushing. Sit down, watch the water hit the leaf, and taste why the ritual beats a paper cup. @imperialteacourt

Song Tea and Ceramics

2120 Sutter St, San Francisco. A hushed Lower Pacific Heights room where the tea is small batch and often exclusive to the shop, poured next to handmade cups you will want to take home. Book a tasting flight and leave with a leaf you cannot find anywhere else. @hellosongtea

Aroma Tea Shop

302 6th Ave, San Francisco. An Inner Richmond wall of canisters holding more than 150 loose leaf teas, from roasted oolong to blooming jasmine, with staff who will walk you through every one. You come in for a single tea and leave with five. @aromateashop

Stonemill Matcha

561 Valencia St, San Francisco. A calm Valencia Street cafe built around stone milled Japanese matcha whisked to order, served with a full Japanese breakfast on weekends. Come for the ceremonial grade, stay for the miso, rice, and grilled fish. @stonemillmatcha

Dartealing Lounge

470 3rd St, San Francisco. A Victorian styled lounge near South Beach pouring around 40 loose leaf teas with a proper tiered afternoon tea. Weekends only, so plan it, then settle in and let the second pot go long. @dartealinglounge

Lovejoy's Tea Room

1351 Church St, San Francisco. Noe Valley afternoon tea in a mismatched parlor that feels like an English cottage wandered off and settled in California. Reserve ahead, get the full service with scones and clotted cream, and give it a slow two hours. @lovejoys_tearoom

Eight for San Francisco tea: Imperial Tea Court, Red Blossom, Song Tea, Vital Tea Leaf, Aroma, Stonemill Matcha, Lovejoy's, and Dartealing. Grant Avenue gongfu or a Noe Valley scone, which one are you booking first?
