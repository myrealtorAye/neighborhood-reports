San Francisco Seafood and Oyster Bars

Seven San Francisco seafood and oyster spots where the ice stays loud and nobody rushes your dozen. Locals send you here, the guidebooks send you to the Wharf.

Swan Oyster Depot — 1517 Polk Street, San Francisco. An 18-stool marble counter that has held the same Polk Street address since 1912, which in this city counts as a miracle of tenure. Sicilian sashimi, crab back on sourdough, a chowder thinner than you expect. Bring cash, come before 10, and make peace with the line.

Hog Island Oyster Co — 1 Ferry Building, San Francisco. The Sweetwaters and Kumamotos ride in from the Tomales Bay farm, so freshness is a supply chain and not a slogan. Grab a seat facing the Bay Bridge, order a dozen and the Manila clams, and let the ferry crowd blur past. No reservations, which keeps everyone honest. @hogislandoysterco

Waterbar — 399 The Embarcadero, San Francisco. The widest oyster selection on the West Coast under a glass caviar chandelier, with the Bay Bridge lights doing the decorating for free. Happy hour runs 2:30 to 5 daily, the civilized hour to work a raw bar with a skyline in the window. Sit outside if the fog takes the night off. @waterbarsf

Leo's Oyster Bar — 568 Sacramento Street, San Francisco. Green palm wallpaper, a checkerboard floor, and a raw bar built for the Financial District's better lunch hours. It photographs like a 1940s garden party and pours cocktails to match, so dress a notch up. Weekday lunch and happy hour are the sweet spot before the after-work suits claim every stool.

Scoma's — Pier 47, Al Scoma Way, San Francisco. The Wharf spot locals will admit to, tucked on a working pier where the boats still unload out back. Order the cioppino or the lazy man's version and let someone else wrestle the shells. It has anchored this dock since 1965, longer than most of the Wharf's souvenir shops have held their leases. @scomassf

Sotto Mare — 552 Green Street, San Francisco. A tight North Beach room that bills its cioppino as the best one in the city and then makes you believe it. Oysters and clams on the half shell, seafood risotto, elbow-to-elbow tables, a wait you will happily eat through. Come early or come patient, since the block fills the moment the Beach empties out.

Woodhouse Fish Co — 1914 Fillmore Street, San Francisco. A New England fish shack that wandered onto Fillmore, all lobster rolls, oyster happy hours, and paper on the tables. Walk-ins welcome and open daily to 9:30, so it works for a solo counter dinner or a low-key group without the reservation choreography. Come Tuesday, Friday, or Saturday when they stay open later. @woodhousefish

Seven for the shuck: which San Francisco counter gets your first dozen, the 1912 marble at Swan or the Bay Bridge glow at Waterbar?
