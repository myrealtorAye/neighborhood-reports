San Francisco Antique and Vintage Home Shops

Gaslight and Shadows has anchored the same Clement Street corner since 1976, a half century argument that old San Francisco is not gone, just quieter. Eight vintage and antique home shops still worth the hunt, from the Mission to the Richmond.

Stuff by Luxe — 1830 Harrison Street, San Francisco. When the beloved Stuff closed, a former tech guy reopened the idea in the Mission, forty some dealers packed into room after room of mid century furniture, lamps, and oddball decor. Go on a weekend and give yourself an hour. @stuffbyluxe

De Angelis — 573 Valencia Street, San Francisco. A tight 20th century design gallery where the chairs have names, Paul Evans, Finn Juhl, Hans Wegner, each piece restored instead of just dusted off. More museum than shop, but everything is for sale. Look, do not lean. @deangelissf

Woodchuck Antiques — 3597 Sacramento Street, San Francisco. Chuck opened in 1974 and still deals almost only in lighting, bronzes, table lamps, and chandeliers from the 1870s to the 1930s. The hours are tiny, think weekday early afternoons, so time it right and knock.

Mureta and Co — 2418 Fillmore Street, San Francisco. Look for the pineapple door. Inside, estate jewelry and antiques since 1983, Georgian mourning rings to Deco diamonds, the kind of old cut stones you cannot fake. Good for browsers, dangerous for gift shoppers. @muretaandco

The Perish Trust — 728 Divisadero Street, San Francisco. A modern general store since 2008, part apothecary, part cabinet of curiosities, stacked with well worn homegoods, local ceramics, incense, and odd little heirlooms. Come for a gift, leave with something for your own shelf. @theperishtrust

Gaslight and Shadows — 2335 Clement Street, San Francisco. Fifty years on the same Richmond block, run with the patience that implies. Bakelite jewelry, glass cases of collectibles, and reportedly the largest stash of hand painted Limoges boxes in town. The kind of shop that rewards a slow lap. @gaslight_shadows

Grand Central Station Antiques — 360 Bayshore Boulevard, San Francisco. Worth the drive to the edge of town, five thousand square feet of European furniture, most of it shipped in from Belgium, heavy on 19th century French, English, and Flemish. Closed Tuesdays, so plan around it. @gcsantiques

Belle Cose and Molte Cose — 2200 Polk Street, San Francisco. A sunlit corner of curated vintage, uranium glass and old barware and home odds mixed in with the racks. A neighborhood fixture that recently moved up Polk to better light. Easy to lose twenty minutes in the glassware. @bellecose_moltecose

Eight for the San Francisco vintage hunt: Stuff by Luxe, De Angelis, Woodchuck, Mureta and Co., The Perish Trust, Gaslight and Shadows, Grand Central Station, and Belle Cose and Molte Cose. Which window would pull you in first?
