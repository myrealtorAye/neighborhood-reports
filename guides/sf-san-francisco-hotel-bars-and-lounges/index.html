San Francisco Hotel Bars and Lounges

Six San Francisco hotel bars you can walk into without booking a room. Some float on the roof, some hide off the lobby, and all of them out pour the bar down the block.

Top of the Mark — 999 California Street, San Francisco. The penthouse lounge atop the Mark Hopkins has been pouring on Nob Hill since 1939, and the wraparound windows still outclass any rooftop the tour buses chase. Come Saturday for the live band (there's a small cover) or chip away at the 100 martini list. It's the Golden Gate view Nob Hill charges rent for, no lease required. @topofthemarksf

Charmaine's — 1100 Market Street, San Francisco. The Proper Hotel's rooftop floats 120 feet over Market Street, all fire pits and greenery, and it's indoor and outdoor both so the fog won't chase you off. Find the elevator at the 45 McAllister entrance, not the grand doors. Fair warning, groups of three plus hit a $50 per person minimum after dark, so treat it as a view and a cocktail night. @charmainesrooftop

Starlite — 450 Powell Street, San Francisco. Take the elevator to the 21st floor of the Beacon Grand and you get 360 degrees of city, green velvet, and an all vinyl DJ most nights. Cocktails come from Scott Baird of Trick Dog fame; order the Cable Car, his spin on a Sidecar. Thursday through Sunday, 21 and up, and the rare Union Square room worth changing out of the hoodie for. @starlitesf

The Post Room — 450 Powell Street, San Francisco. Ride the same Beacon Grand elevator back to the lobby and you land at its brasserie bar, all vaulted ceilings and cold, exact martinis. The move is weekday happy hour: ten dollar cocktails, seven dollar wine, two dollar oysters, prices that feel smuggled in from another decade. Sit at the bar and order a dozen. @beacongrand

Pied Piper — 2 New Montgomery Street, San Francisco. The Palace bar is named for the Maxfield Parrish mural behind it, painted after the 1906 quake and hanging here since 1909. That makes it the most valuable wallpaper in any bar you'll drink in. Mosaic floors, dark wood, a serious whiskey list, and a Wagyu burger locals quietly rank among the city's best. Open nightly from five. @piedpipersf

Garden Court Lounge — 2 New Montgomery Street, San Francisco. Cross the Palace lobby from the Pied Piper and you reach the GC Lounge, where you sip beneath the landmark glass dome without booking the full sit down feast. Cocktails, wine, and dessert under Austrian crystal. That's a lot of grandeur for a Tuesday. Open afternoons to evening, Monday through Saturday. Bring someone you're trying to impress. @palacehotelsf

Six for a San Francisco hotel bar night, no room required: Top of the Mark and Charmaine's for the view, Starlite for the party, the Post Room for cheap oysters, Pied Piper for the mural, the Garden Court Lounge for the dome. Which gets your first round?
