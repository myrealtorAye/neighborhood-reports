San Francisco Independent Bookstores

City Lights published Allen Ginsberg's Howl in 1956 and then won the obscenity trial that made it legal, and the Beat landmark still sells books on Columbus today. Eight San Francisco bookstores worth a browse.

City Lights Booksellers — 261 Columbus Ave. The Beat Generation landmark and publisher since 1953, three floors of books and a poetry room upstairs, a literary shrine you can shop. Buy something upstairs.

Green Apple Books — 506 Clement St. A creaky Inner Richmond warren of new and used since 1967, floors that tilt and shelves to the ceiling. The lose yourself for hours store.

Dog Eared Books — 900 Valencia St. A beloved Mission shop of new, used, and remaindered books with a sharp local and queer section. The neighborhood favorite.

The Booksmith — 1644 Haight St. The Haight Street independent since 1976, a strong events calendar and a curated front table, counterculture in the stacks. Catch an author night.

Omnivore Books — 3885 Cesar Chavez St. A jewel box shop devoted entirely to food and cookbooks, new, used, and rare, with author dinners and a cult following. Chef heaven. @omnivorebooks

Bird and Beckett — 653 Chenery St. A Glen Park indie of books and records that has hosted live jazz every week since 2002, a bookstore that swings. Catch a Friday set. @bird.beckett

Fabulosa Books — 489 Castro St. The Castro's LGBTQ and general interest bookstore, warm and community minded, the shop that mails banned books to red states. Buy local, do good.

Borderlands Books — 1740 Haight St. A science fiction, fantasy, and horror specialist in the Haight, fiercely curated with a cafe next door, a genre reader's clubhouse. The genre one.

Eight to browse: City Lights, Green Apple, Dog Eared, the Booksmith, Omnivore, Bird and Beckett, Fabulosa, and Borderlands. Where do you buy your next read?
