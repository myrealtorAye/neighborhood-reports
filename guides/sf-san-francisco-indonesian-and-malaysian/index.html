San Francisco Indonesian and Malaysian

Eight San Francisco spots for Indonesian and Malaysian food, a category the city keeps quiet about. Rendang, laksa, sambal, kaya toast, all inside city limits, no bridge toll required. If you figured SF topped out at pho and dim sum, these are the ones worth crossing town for.

Fikscue — 7 Warriors Way, San Francisco. Indonesian spice meets Texas smoke at Thrive City, the rare brisket that tastes of sambal and lemongrass. It made the New York Times best list, then kept the line moving on Warriors nights. Go early on a game day or the good cuts sell out before tip off. @fikscue

Nusa — 3450 Third Street, San Francisco. A Bayview kitchen turning out rendang and nasi campur the way a grandmother in Java would nod at. Chef Jennifer Huang grew up on these flavors and it shows in the depth of every sauce. Catch the Saturday stand at the Ferry Building if Third Street is a trek. @nusa.sf

Rasa Rasa Kitchen — 2200 Bryant Street, San Francisco. The Mission spot known as R2K plates gado gado in a crispy tortilla bowl, which sounds like a gimmick until the peanut sauce wins you over. Beef rendang and soto round out a short, confident menu. Weeknights are calmer if you want a table without the wait. @sf.rasarasakitchen

Warung Nasi Indonesia — 1200 Market Street, San Francisco. A Mid Market counter doing homestyle Indonesian, the kind of nasi and sambal that regulars order without glancing at the menu. Portions lean generous and the heat is honest, so ask where you land before they ladle. Lunch is the sweet spot. @warungnasi.indonesia

Lime Tree Southeast Asian Kitchen — 836 Clement Street, San Francisco. A Richmond mainstay blurring the line between Malaysian, Singaporean, and Indonesian, with a roti canai that has quieted many a debate about who does it best. There is a second kitchen on Irving if the avenues are your side of town. Closed Mondays, plan around it. @limetreekitchensf

Penang Garden — 728 Washington Street, San Francisco. Tucked into Chinatown, a Malaysian and Singaporean menu that surprises anyone expecting another Cantonese room. Order the Hainan chicken or the char kway teow and settle in. It has cooked quietly through decades of the block turning over around it.

M&Y Malaysian Food — 1661 Tennessee Street, San Francisco. A tiny Dogpatch operation built around laksa, wraps, and bowls that travel well if the shipyard fog chases you back to your desk. Bold, colorful plates, portions that earn the trip out here. Weekdays open at noon when the neighborhood is hunting lunch.

Kopiku Indonesian Coffee — 1443 Lombard Street, San Francisco. The city's first Indonesian coffee bar, pulling shots from small Sumatran and Javanese farms and pairing them with kaya toast. Pandan lattes and a pour over worth slowing down for, tucked on a Marina stretch built for drive by. Seating is tight, so time it off peak. @kopikusf

Eight for the Southeast Asian corner SF forgets it has: Fikscue for smoke, Nusa and Rasa Rasa for rendang done right, Warung Nasi for the homestyle plate, Lime Tree for roti, Penang Garden for the Chinatown surprise, M&Y for laksa, Kopiku for the kaya toast finish. Which one are you booking first?
