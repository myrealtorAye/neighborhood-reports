East Bay Live Music

The Greek Theatre has been staging shows on the UC Berkeley hillside since 1903, eucalyptus and headliners under the same stars. Twelve East Bay live-music rooms worth the Friday night.

The Greek Theatre — 2001 Gayley Rd, Berkeley. An outdoor amphitheater carved into the hillside in 1903. Eucalyptus, open sky, and a bucket-list seat. @greekberkeley

Fox Theater — 1807 Telegraph Ave, Oakland. A restored 1928 art-deco palace on Telegraph. Mid-size touring acts and locals you will see headline elsewhere in a few years. @foxoakland

Paramount Theatre — 2025 Broadway, Oakland. An art-deco landmark from 1931, fully restored. Symphony nights, classic film series, arena-quality sound. @oakparamount

Yoshi's — 510 Embarcadero West, Oakland. Two sets a night in the dinner-jazz tradition, the room where Bay Area jazz history keeps adding chapters. @yoshis_oak

Freight and Salvage — 2020 Addison St, Berkeley. A folk and roots listening room running since 1968. No chatter while the artist plays, that is the rule. @thefreightberkeley

Ashkenaz — 1317 San Pablo Ave, Berkeley. A world-music and dance hall since 1973. Cumbia, klezmer, soul, and a wood floor with forty years of footwork on it. @ashkenazberkeley

The UC Theatre — 2036 University Ave, Berkeley. A restored vaudeville house turned all-ages music hall that has shaped Bay Area audiences for a decade.

The New Parish — 1743 San Pablo Ave, Oakland. An intimate 400-capacity room with a beloved upstairs patio where you can still hear the set. @thenewparish

Retro Junkie — 2112 N Main St, Walnut Creek. Tribute bands, karaoke, and pinball in an 80s-and-90s room, the 680-corridor live-music pick. @retrojunkiebar

Ivy Room — 860 San Pablo Ave, Albany. A 200-capacity independent room hailed as one of the best-sounding in the Bay, punk to jazz to Americana.

924 Gilman — 924 Gilman St, Berkeley. The volunteer-run, all-ages, no-alcohol punk collective that launched Green Day and Operation Ivy, running since 1986. @924gilman

Calvin Simmons Theatre — Henry J Kaiser Center, 10 10th St, Oakland. A 1,500-seat 1915 hall reopened after two decades dark.
