San Francisco Pottery and Ceramics Studios

San Francisco hides a public pottery studio inside Golden Gate Park. Sharon Art Studio has fired kilns in a historic park building for generations and runs some of the most affordable wheel classes in the city. You do not have to cross to Oakland to make a mug. Eight SF studios where a first timer can throw a bowl this weekend.

Sharon Art Studio — 300 Bowling Green Dr, San Francisco. The city's largest public art center, tucked into Golden Gate Park near the carousel, with ceramics classes for every age and level through Rec and Parks. Low prices mean sessions fill fast, so sign up the day enrollment opens. Bring a friend and split a bag of clay.

Ruby's Clay Studio and Gallery — 552A Noe St, San Francisco. A nonprofit community studio in the Castro run by and for potters, with classes, member shelves and a small gallery up front. The vibe is welcoming and unpretentious, exactly what a first wheel class should feel like. Try a beginner handbuilding night. @rubysclaystudio

Clayroom — 1431 17th St, San Francisco. A big, friendly Potrero Hill studio with wheel classes, memberships and a woodshop, plus paint your own pottery if you just want a low key afternoon. Good for total beginners and returning potters alike. Book a single wheel session and see if it grabs you. @clayroomsf

Wheelhouse Clay Studio — 2201 Sutter St, San Francisco. A Filipina and woman of color owned studio that treats clay as community, with six week wheel courses and one time Clay Play classes for the merely curious. Small groups, real instruction, zero snobbery. Grab a two hour Clay Play wheel spot and make a mess. @wheelhouseclaysf

SMAart Studio — 3135 24th St, San Francisco. Over 10,000 square feet in the Mission with wheel, hand building, sculpture and even Raku firing, plus one day workshops if you want to try before committing. Members get 24 hour access. Start with a one day wheel class, then chase the Raku night. @smaartstudio

Clay By The Bay — 1618 Pacific Ave, San Francisco. A roomy studio built around beginners, open seven days a week, known for Friday and Saturday try pottery nights where you make two pieces in one sitting. No experience, no problem. Bring a date and see who centers the clay first.

Mel Rice Studio Ceramica — 6138 Geary Blvd, San Francisco. A sustainable Richmond studio that mixes its own glazes and recycles every scrap of clay, opened in 2016 by a San Franciscan artist. Classes stay small and the ethos is low waste. Take a handbuilding workshop and leave with something you made. @melriceceramica

Terra Mia Decorative Art Studio — 1314 Castro St, San Francisco. The original NorCal paint your own pottery spot, no experience or appointment needed, just pick a piece and glaze away. Great for kids, birthdays and a foggy Noe Valley afternoon. Walk in, paint a mug, pick it up a few days later.

Eight for San Francisco clay: Sharon, Ruby's, Clayroom, Wheelhouse, SMAart, Clay By The Bay, Mel Rice, and Terra Mia. Which studio gets your first bowl?
