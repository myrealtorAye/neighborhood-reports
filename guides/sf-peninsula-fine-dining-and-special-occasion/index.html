San Francisco Peninsula Fine Dining and Special Occasion

Eight Peninsula restaurants worth the good reservation, the kind you book when the occasion matters. Anniversary, closing dinner, the promotion you haven't announced yet. Woodside down to the Half Moon Bay coast, Michelin stars to a lobster roll with an ocean view. This is the list for when someone asks where to celebrate.

The Village Pub — 2967 Woodside Road, Woodside. Don't let the humble name fool you, there's a Michelin star behind that cozy sign and a wine list running past 3,500 bottles. It's where Woodside quietly celebrates the deals it'll never post about. Book the Friday lunch if you want the room without the evening wait. @thevillagepubwoodside

Protege — 250 California Avenue, Palo Alto. It's two restaurants in one, a 10 seat bar with an a la carte lounge up front, a seven course tasting in the dining room out back. A Master Sommelier runs the wine, so trust the pairing over your own guessing. They take no phone calls, email only. That's peak Palo Alto. @protegepaloalto

Flea Street — 3607 Alameda de las Pulgas, Menlo Park. Jesse Cool has run this farm to table room since 1980, back when organic was a philosophy and not a price tag. It's candlelit, a little hard to find, and quietly one of the most romantic tables on the Peninsula. Ask about the garden the kitchen cooks from. @flea_street

Evvia Estiatorio — 420 Emerson Street, Palo Alto. The Greek sister to San Francisco's Kokkari, and the table downtown regulars book when they want warmth instead of white linen. Wood grilled fish, a fireplace, a room that's clearly full of repeat customers. Sit at the chef's table by the open kitchen if you like some theater with dinner.

Madera — 2825 Sand Hill Road, Menlo Park. It's inside Rosewood on Sand Hill Road. That means the term sheets at the next table are probably real. The kitchen works over a wood burning grill and the terrace catches the sunset over the hills. Dress smart casual, hand the keys to the valet, and let someone else pour the award winning wine. @rosewoodsandhill

Vina Enoteca — 700 Welch Road, Palo Alto. Handmade pasta and a deep Italian wine list, tucked near Stanford where you'd never think to look. There's a little market attached, so you can carry home the sauce you fell for at dinner. Come for the happy hour at the bar, stay because you didn't want to leave. @vina_enoteca

Wakuriya — 115 De Anza Boulevard, San Mateo. A one chef Japanese kaiseki counter in a strip mall. That's exactly the sort of address that tends to hide the best meal in town. Reservations open a month ahead, by phone, message only, and they fill fast. Set the reminder, call on the right date, and eat whatever the season decides.

Sam's Chowder House — 4210 North Cabrillo Highway, Half Moon Bay. The one that swaps white tablecloths for a deck over the Pacific, and earns the trade. The lobster roll made a national best sandwich list, the sunset handles the rest. Not fine dining, the place you book when the occasion is the view. @samschowderhouse

Eight Peninsula tables for the nights that matter: Village Pub and Madera for the milestone, Protege and Wakuriya for the splurge, Flea Street and Evvia for the romance, Vina Enoteca for the easy yes, Sam's for the view. Which one's getting your next anniversary?
