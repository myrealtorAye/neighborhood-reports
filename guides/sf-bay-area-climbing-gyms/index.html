Bay Area Climbing Gyms

Seven Bay Area climbing gyms worth the chalk, and the best story might belong to a former power plant. Say climbing and people picture Yosemite, but the Bay quietly built a world class indoor scene, three of them right here in San Francisco. Ropes, boulders, saunas, real community. Where to tie in first?

Mission Cliffs — 2295 Harrison Street, San Francisco. The one that started it all, the city's first climbing gym and still a Mission anchor three decades on. Fifty foot lead walls, a bouldering section, yoga, a weight room and a hot sauna for after. First timers and veterans share the same walls. Regulars swear by a weekday morning session. @missioncliffs

Dogpatch Boulders — 2573 Third Street, San Francisco. No ropes, all problems. Touchstone's largest bouldering gym stacks more than 300 problems on seventeen foot walls in a walkable corner of the Dogpatch. They reset often, so the wall you flailed on last month feels new. Upstairs weights and a roomy sauna seal it. @dogpatchboulders

Movement San Francisco — 924 Old Mason Street, San Francisco. Climbing with a Golden Gate Bridge view, tucked into the Presidio above Crissy Field. Twenty five thousand square feet, rope walls to forty five feet, two floors of bouldering, a Kilter board and two yoga studios. Send hard, then walk it off along the water. @movementgymsbayarea

The Oaks — 1875 Solano Avenue, Berkeley. They built a bouldering gym inside the historic Oaks Theater on Solano, so North Berkeley climbs under ninety year old movie house bones. Slabs, steep overhangs, dihedrals and aretes, 160 plus problems, and monthly movie nights that nod to the address. Character no new build can copy. @oaksclimbing

Berkeley Ironworks — 800 Potter Street, Berkeley. An East Bay staple with a forty five foot lead wall and, members say, the best natural light of any gym around. Fourteen thousand square feet of routes and boulders, plus a members only training center across the street with boards and world cup style terrain. @berkeleyironworks

Great Western Power Co — 520 20th Street, Oakland. Set inside a former steam power plant in Uptown, all historic brick and forty eight foot lead lines. Eleven thousand square feet, calm and quiet by day, social by night, and an easy walk from BART. An established favorite regulars quietly rank as their top Touchstone. @greatwesternpowerco

Hyperion — 801 Willow Street, Redwood City. The newest room here, and the Peninsula finally has a serious one. More than ten thousand square feet of bouldering, 250 plus problems, a real comp wall, with rope walls on the way. If you live south of the city, this is the move. @hyperionclimbing

Seven for the Bay Area climber: Mission Cliffs and Dogpatch in the city, Movement in the Presidio, the Oaks and Ironworks in Berkeley, Great Western in Oakland, Hyperion down the Peninsula. Three never make you cross a bridge. Which wall are you tying in on first?
