Buying and selling: the questions that come up every time

These answers are the same whichever neighborhood you are looking at, so they live here rather than being repeated in every monthly report. Where a figure appears it is drawn from the current month's data for Acampo and is there to show the shape of the calculation.

How much do I actually need for a down payment?

Less than most people think, and more than the headline number suggests. The minimums are 3 percent on a conventional loan, 3.5 percent on FHA, and zero on VA and on NACA. But the down payment is not the whole cash requirement. You also need closing costs, commonly around 2 to 3 percent of the price, plus an impound account funded at closing, plus the inspections you pay for during the contingency period. At the typical value here, $774,585, a 3.5 percent down payment is $27,110 and 20 percent is $154,917.

What are closing costs, and who pays what?

In a California transaction the costs split roughly along these lines, though every one of them is negotiable in the contract. The buyer typically carries the lender fees, the appraisal, the inspections, the loan-related title policy and the prepaid interest, taxes and insurance that fund the impound account. The seller typically carries the owner's title policy, the county and city transfer tax where custom splits it, any negotiated credits, and the brokerage compensation agreed in writing. In Acampo the transfer tax alone is $852 on a home at the typical value. It deserves its own line instead of being buried in an estimate. Escrow produces the actual figures for a specific deal, and I would want to see that estimated settlement statement before anyone commits to a price, not after.

How long does escrow take in California?

Thirty days is the common target on a financed purchase, and it moves from there. A cash purchase can close in a week or two because there is no loan, no appraisal and no lender timeline. A loan with any complication, a condo with an HOA questionnaire, a property with a title issue, or anything involving a court confirmation, all run longer. In a probate sale the court calendar sets the pace, not the parties. What I watch is the contingency calendar and not the closing date, because the deadlines for inspection, appraisal and loan approval are where deals actually turn.

What is the difference between pre-qualified and pre-approved?

Pre-qualified means a lender looked at what you told them. Pre-approved means a lender verified it, pulled credit, and reviewed income and asset documentation. In a market where a seller is choosing between offers, those two documents are not treated as equivalent, and in a competitive situation a pre-qualification is close to worthless. Some lenders go further and underwrite the file up front so the only thing outstanding is the property itself. That version is the strongest paper a buyer can carry into an offer, and it is worth asking your lender whether they do it.

Is now a good time to buy?

I will not answer that with a yes or a no, because the honest answer depends on things I do not know about you. What I can do is show the arithmetic. The cost of waiting section above runs the math on price and payment at this neighborhood's own appreciation rate, and then runs it again at the more conservative citywide rate so you can see how much of the result is the assumption. It also shows a rates-fall and a rates-rise case, because rates have moved more than prices here over the past year and a calculation that only moves in one direction is a sales tool. The questions that actually decide it are how long you expect to hold, how stable your income is, and whether the payment works at today's rate without assuming you refinance later.

What is my home actually worth?

Not what an automated estimate says, and not what the index in this report says either. Both of those describe a typical home, and no one owns a typical home. Value comes from comparable sales: recent, nearby, similar in size and condition, with adjustments for what differs. In a neighborhood with housing stock as varied as this one, condition and updates move the number more than square footage does. I will put that analysis together in writing, with the comparables shown rather than asserted, and there is no charge and no obligation attached to it.

Should I fix things before I sell?

Some things, not everything. The repairs that reliably return more than they cost are the ones that remove a buyer's objection or a lender's condition: deferred maintenance that shows up in an inspection report, anything that affects the roof or the systems, and cosmetic work that is cheap relative to how much it changes the first impression. The ones that rarely return their cost are full remodels done to the seller's taste immediately before a sale. The sequence I would use is to get the inspection first, then decide what to fix with the actual report in hand, rather than guessing and spending money on the wrong items.

Do I pay tax when I sell my home?

Often not, because of the primary residence exclusion under Internal Revenue Code section 121. It excludes up to 250,000 dollars of gain for a single filer and 500,000 for a married couple filing jointly, where the ownership and use tests are met. In a market at these values that exclusion is frequently the single largest number in the transaction, and the tests around it are specific. Gain is calculated from your basis. That basis includes capital improvements you made over the years, so the records matter. I do not advise on the exclusion or on your basis. That belongs with your CPA. What I do is flag the question early and provide the sale figures they need to model it, well before anyone is committed to a timeline.

Can I buy before I sell?

It is possible and it is harder than it was when rates were low. The underwriting question is whether you qualify carrying both payments, and at 7.4 percent that is a higher bar than most people expect. There are three usual routes. A sale contingency weakens an offer. A bridge loan costs money, or using equity from the current home before it sells. Each of those trades money for certainty in a different place. What I would not do is start touring before the lender has confirmed which of those routes is actually open, because the answer changes which houses are realistic.

What is a supplemental tax bill?

It is the bill that arrives months after closing and surprises almost every first-time buyer. When a property changes hands it is reassessed at the purchase price, and the difference between the seller's old assessed value and the new one is billed separately from the regular installments. It frequently arrives after the escrow impound account was already set up using the seller's old number. The impound comes up short. It is routine and it is also real money, so it belongs in the first-year budget rather than in the surprise column.

What happens to the house in a divorce?

It is usually the largest asset and the only one that cannot be divided without either a sale or a buyout. It tends to set the pace of the whole settlement. Two things decide most outcomes. First, authority: listing a jointly held property generally needs both signatures. Second, the refinance, because removing one spouse from the loan is underwritten on the remaining spouse's income alone at today's rate and not the rate on the existing loan. A buyout that works on a spreadsheet often does not survive that. I do not give legal advice and I do not interpret the settlement agreement. What I provide is the valuation and net-proceeds analysis both sides and both attorneys can work from, with the holding-cost math shown. The best financial outcomes I see come from that analysis being done before the terms are fixed rather than after.

What happens to the house when someone dies?

It depends entirely on how title was held. That is the first question, not a detail. A property in a properly funded trust passes under the trust without probate. Property held in joint tenancy passes to the survivor. Property held in the decedent's name alone generally goes through probate, and California calculates statutory fees on the gross value of the asset rather than on the family's equity, so a large mortgage does not reduce them. The probate section above runs that calculation at this neighborhood's typical value. I do not advise on whether probate is required or on a trustee's duties. That belongs with the probate attorney. What I do is provide the date-of-death and current valuations the attorney and the CPA both need, flag the title, condition and vacant-property insurance issues early, and give the family a real monthly carrying cost so the choice between selling and holding is made against a number.
