How these reports are built

Every report in this series is rebuilt from the current month's data. Nothing is carried over from last month except the comparison against it.

Where the numbers come from

Typical home values are the Zillow Home Value Index at neighborhood and city level, smoothed and seasonally adjusted. Listing activity, inventory, days on market and price per square foot come from the Realtor.com residential listings database at ZIP level. Rent is the Zillow Observed Rent Index. Mortgage rate averages are the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey, published weekly. Loan limits are the Federal Housing Finance Agency county file and HUD's published FHA limits. Transfer tax rates come from the California City Finance compilation of city and county documentary transfer tax rates. School listings come from the California Department of Education public directory. Housing and affordability figures come from the Census Bureau American Community Survey five-year estimates.

Every figure in a report carries its own source and its own date in the sources table at the bottom of that report.

The two calculations

Cost of waiting is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period. The appreciation rate is set to that neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value, and it is labelled as an extrapolation of one observed year rather than as a forecast. Every report also runs the same math at the more conservative citywide rate, and shows a rates-fall and a rates-rise case, so the result is never presented as moving in only one direction. Rent paid while waiting is reported separately rather than folded into the headline figure.

Cost of holding is the loan payment plus property tax plus insurance plus utilities plus a stated maintenance budget. HOA dues are excluded and the report says so. Where a figure is a stated convention instead of a measurement, such as budgeting maintenance at one percent of value a year, the report says so at the point of use.

What these reports will not do

These reports cover the housing: the stock, the market measurements, the money and the transaction. They do not describe who lives in a neighborhood, and they do not rank or compare schools. School listings are published facts only, with the district office as the authority on which school serves a specific address. Those are Fair Housing matters, and they are also not things that tell you anything useful about a house.

They do not carry invented figures. Where data is unavailable the report says so rather than estimating, and the build refuses to publish a report containing an unresolved source marker.

Corrections

If a figure here looks wrong, it is worth telling me, because the sources update and the compilations occasionally disagree with each other. Where two sources disagree, the reports say so rather than picking the flattering one.
