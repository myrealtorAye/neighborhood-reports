Mission Valley, Fremont: the October 2026 read

Mission Valley is the ground between the old mission and the valley floor, and it was built as housing tracts in the late 1960s.

The clearest documentary trace of that is a school. John Gomes Elementary, a short way off, was built specifically to serve what the district at the time called the Way Out West and Mission Valley housing, took its first students in September 1969 and was dedicated in March 1970. Mission Valley Elementary sits in the neighborhood at 41700 Denise Street. When a school district builds schools for a named housing project, that is the development record telling you when the neighborhood arrived.

The anchor to the north is Old Mission San Jose at 43300 Mission Boulevard, founded on June 11, 1797 and holding the oldest standing adobe building in Alameda County, put up in 1809. This neighborhood is named for its position relative to that, not for any mission era settlement of its own.

The housing is accordingly postwar tract: single family, laid out to a plan, on a street pattern that follows the rising grade toward the hills. Across Fremont, 81,579 housing units and only about 2 percent of them predate 1950. The city median listing runs around 1,489 square feet and the ACS median owner occupied value for Fremont is 1,403,800 dollars, with owner occupancy at 60.8 percent citywide.

What to do with that. Because the stock here went up inside a narrow window from a small number of builders, the comparable sales are genuinely comparable and pricing reads cleanly. Use that. Pull the year built and the tract, then compare only against the same era.

Check the lot dimensions and the setbacks if an accessory dwelling unit is any part of your plan, because single story houses on regular lots are the best ADU candidates in Fremont and the limit is usually the setback, not the floor plan.

Then drive Mission Boulevard at five on a weekday. The commute is what people get wrong about the east side of this city.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Fremont I cover, and what each one is:

  • Ardenwood. A Pennsylvania farmer named George Washington Patterson came to California for gold in 1849, found almost none, and in 1857 built a redwood farmhouse here instead.
  • Baylands. This is the edge of Fremont where the city stops and the bay starts, and most of it is federally protected.
  • Brookvale. Brookvale is one of the Fremont neighborhoods where the published history is genuinely thin, so this is the development record and the practical read, not a story somebody made up.
  • Cabrillo. Cabrillo is a 1960s tract neighborhood in north Fremont, and the useful facts about it are in the development record instead of in any story.
  • Centerville. There is a cemetery at Post Street and Bonde Way that is older than the city around it by a century.
  • Cherry Guardino. Cherry-Guardino takes its name from its two main streets, and like several Fremont tract neighborhoods it has almost no published history.
  • City Center. On September 6, 1960 the Fremont City Council picked the Fudenna ranch as the site for its civic center.
  • Glenmoor. Glenmoor has a homeowners association that was incorporated in March 1953, when there were no more than 75 houses standing in the whole subdivision.

What it costs to own here

Owning that typical home runs about $22,013 a month, $264,158 a year, once the loan, the property tax, insurance, utilities and a maintenance budget are all counted. HOA dues are not in it, because they vary by building.

Rates sit at 7.4 percent on the 30-year fixed as of 2026-10-15. A year earlier the same survey read 6.27 percent. The conforming and FHA ceiling for a one-unit property in Alameda County is $1,249,125 in 2026.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $2,434,564 here, transfer tax runs about $2,678 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Convention puts about $1,339 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Emeryville at 26.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $60,864.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about $164,482 here. The five year figure is $518,334.

Treat it as a scenario, not a prediction. The appreciation input is one year of observed change carried forward, and five years of compounding on one year of data flatters the result.
