Crestmont, Oakland: the October 2026 read

Crestmont is a hillside pocket on the Oakland ridge with little published history of its own, so this is the development record and the practical read.

What places it is geology and fire. These hills carried the San Antonio redwood forest until loggers took it down inside about fifteen years from the late 1840s, and the first steam sawmill went up in 1850 at the head of Dimond Canyon. What grew back is the mixed woodland the whole ridge now carries.

Then 1991. On the morning of Sunday, October 20, 1991 a grass fire that had been knocked down the day before near the Caldecott Tunnel came back to life shortly before eleven. Winds gusting to 65 miles an hour drove it down the south ridge of Claremont Canyon, and inside thirty minutes it had jumped Highway 24 and Highway 13 and set hundreds of houses alight. By the time it was fully controlled on October 23 it had killed 25 people, injured 150, and destroyed 2,843 single family homes and 437 apartment and condominium units. Losses ran to about 1.5 billion dollars. Cal Fire lists it as the Tunnel Fire and the cause was never determined. The firestorm defines how this entire side of Oakland is insured and priced, whether or not a specific street burned.

The housing is hillside single family on sloped lots, later in era than the Oakland average. Across the city 46.6 percent of the 191,713 housing units predate 1950, and 41.5 percent of units are single family detached with owner occupancy at 42.3 percent. On ridge ground both of the latter figures run considerably higher.

So the order of work here is fixed and it does not start with the kitchen.

Fire insurance first. A written quote on the exact address before your contingencies expire, the risk rating explained, the defensible space requirement confirmed, and a straight answer on which carriers will write this zone.

Second, the hillside list: soils and drainage history for the parcel, the condition and ownership of any retaining structures, and where water leaves the property in a wet month.

Third, the house matched to its year built. Pre war stock gets foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines and the sewer lateral Oakland certifies at sale. Later stock gets the roof, the mechanical systems and the windows.

Then drive in and out once after dark and once in the rain, because on these streets that is information you cannot get any other way.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Oakland I cover, and what each one is:

  • Acorn. Fifty blocks of this neighborhood were demolished.
  • Adams Point. Adams Point is named for Edson Adams, one of the men who got hold of Oakland's land early, and in 1906 it filled up with San Franciscans who had nowhere else to go.
  • Allendale. The Allendale tract map was recorded in 1892, and there was a cinema operating here by 1913.
  • Brookfield Village. Twelve hundred houses went up here during the Second World War and they sold for four thousand dollars each.
  • Bushrod. Bushrod was annexed by Oakland in 1897, in the same batch as Temescal, Golden Gate and Piedmont Avenue, and that date is the most solid thing in its published record.
  • Caballo Hills. Caballo Hills is a hillside pocket at the southeastern edge of Oakland with very little published history, so this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Castlemont. Castlemont takes its name from the high school at its centre, and its published history is thin enough that this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Chabot Park. Anthony Chabot dammed a creek in 1868 and built Oakland its first water supply at municipal scale, and his name is on the parkland at the edge of this neighborhood.

What it costs to own here

Put the minimum down on the typical Crestmont home and the monthly carry lands near $12,343. That is $148,116 across a year, counting loan, tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance. HOA dues are not in it, since no two are alike.

The 30-year fixed average is 7.4 percent as of 2026-10-15, against 6.27 percent in the same week last year. In Alameda County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Crestmont charges 16.10 per thousand, so the typical $1,287,058 home owes roughly $20,722 at transfer. Custom is to split it, so about $10,361 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $2,000,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 18.60 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $2,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $19,306.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about $269,877 here. The five year figure is $928,201.

This rests on one observed year carried forward. The two year figure is the defensible one; the five year figure is arithmetic, not a forecast.
