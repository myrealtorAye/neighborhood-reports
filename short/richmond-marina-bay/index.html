Marina Bay, Richmond: the October 2026 read

The shipyards were here. Marina Bay is built on them.

Henry Kaiser opened his first Richmond shipyard in December 1940. The city had fewer than 24,000 people that year and nearly 100,000 by 1943. Four yards here built 747 ships, more than any other shipyard complex in the country. The Rosie the Riveter World War II Home Front National Historical Park was dedicated on October 25, 2000 and was the first national park created to commemorate the home front. The Rosie the Riveter Memorial stands on this waterfront, and the SS Red Oak Victory, one of the ships built in these yards, is moored nearby.

What went up on the yard ground afterward is the newest housing in Richmond: planned waterfront development, much of it attached, most of it inside homeowner associations, with the bay trail running along the shoreline.

Richmond has 42,509 housing units and 28.5 percent of them predate 1950. 59.9 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 54.7 percent, and the ACS median value is 668,200 dollars. Marina Bay sits at the opposite end of that range from the rest of the city. Almost nothing here is pre war.

So the pre war inspection list does not apply, and three different questions do.

Get the environmental record for the specific parcel in writing before your contingencies expire. This is a former industrial shipyard and the record exists. Read the record itself. Do not take a summary of it.

Get the association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, and ask specifically what the association maintains on the shoreline side. On made ground at the water that can include levee and bulkhead obligations, and those become assessments.

Get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote, and ask the inspector about settlement, because this is fill at the bay margin.

Then walk the bay trail from the address at dusk. The shoreline access is the reason to be here and it is worth confirming on foot.

What it costs to own here

The all-in monthly number for the typical home here is about $5,229, $62,747 over twelve months. It counts the payment, the tax bill, insurance, utilities and a maintenance allowance, and leaves out HOA dues.

As of 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed averages 7.4 percent, up or down from 6.27 percent twelve months before. Contra Costa County's 2026 one-unit loan limit sits at $1,249,125. That is the cap for conforming conventional and for FHA alike.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 8.10 dollars per thousand, a $510,301 sale in Marina Bay carries about $4,133 in transfer tax. Custom is to split it, so about $2,067 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $1,000,000 the rate steps from 8.10 to 13.50 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,400 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $1,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $6,124.

What waiting costs

Hold off two years and the combined price and payment cost is near -$57,687. At five years it reaches -$161,600.

That rate is one observed year extrapolated forward, not a forecast, and compounding a single strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Weight the two year figure more heavily.
