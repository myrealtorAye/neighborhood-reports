Clayton: the October 2026 read

Clayton would have been called Rhinesville if Charles Rhine had won the argument.

Clayton was nearly called Rhinesville. Joel Clayton and Charles Rhine cofounded the town and each wanted it named after himself. Clayton won, and he and his wife Margaret are buried in Live Oak Cemetery in what is now Concord. The city incorporated in 1964 after a failed attempt in 1960, pushed along by a Concord annexation move in 1963 and by Pleasant Hill's own 1961 incorporation on the other side. The vote on March 3, 1964 carried 251 to 61.

Clayton has 3,990 housing units and 2.1 percent of them predate 1950. 85.8 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 89.2 percent, and the ACS median value is 1,055,200 dollars. ZHVI is about 1,027,000 dollars, up 1.8 percent over the year.

89.2 percent owner occupancy across 3,990 units is among the highest in the county, and only 2.1 percent of the stock predates 1950. This is a small city of owner occupied postwar and newer houses at the foot of Mount Diablo.

So pull the year built and the permit history. On the newer subdivisions and the golf course communities, the association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, plus a written answer on whether club membership transfers with the property. On the hill addresses, soils, drainage, retaining structures and a written fire insurance quote on the exact address.

On the eastern edge, confirm whether the parcel is inside the city or in unincorporated county territory. And get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list and it is the single most useful number on a property profile. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. Those two documents cost nothing, they take a day, and between them they settle most of what a buyer actually needs to know before writing an offer.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Clayton is $1,026,888 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.47 percent from the prior month and up 1.8 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Listing activity in Clayton for 2026-09. Active inventory is 31, down 18.4 percent from the prior month and up 17.0 percent from a year earlier. 14 new listings came on, down 12.5 percent month over month, and 13 went pending. Median days on market is 50, up 35.8 percent month over month and up 28.8 percent year over year.

Median asking price is $1,074,250 at $490 per square foot, on a median listing of 2,030 square feet. Typical rent in the same area is $4,985.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Clayton is up 1.8 percent year over year. The median asking price of what is actively listed in Clayton is up 5.8 percent year over year, and price per square foot is down 1.3 percent.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What it costs to own here, month to month

Sellers underestimate this number, consistently, and it is the number that should set the pricing decision. Owning the typical home in Clayton runs about $333,130 a month, or $3,997,554 a year. That figure is the loan payment at 3.5 percent down and 400 percent, property tax at 1.31 percent, insurance at the California average, utilities for a home of roughly 1,800 square feet, and maintenance budgeted at 1 percent of value a year. HOA dues are not included, because they vary by building and subdivision.

The 15-year average is 400 percent. These are the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey averages, published weekly, and they are survey averages and not a quote.

On a loan of $990,947, the typical home here with the minimum down payment, that year of rate movement is the difference between a payment of $330,316 and $330,316, a change of $0 a month on the same house at the same price. Rate movement has been the larger force on affordability here than price movement.

The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling. At the typical value here of $1,026,888, a 3.5 percent down payment leaves a loan of $990,947, comfortably inside the limit. Both FHA and conforming conventional are live options at this price point. That is not true in every neighborhood in this county, and it widens the program list considerably.

The ceiling still sets a practical top end. The most expensive home a minimum-down loan reaches in this county is about $1,294,430. Above that, the options narrow to a larger down payment or a jumbo loan.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $1,026,888 here, transfer tax runs about $1,130 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $565 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $12,733.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at 1.8 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 400 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $1,026,888 today becomes $1,064,261 in two years and $1,122,887 in five. The monthly payment moves from $330,316 to $342,337 and then $361,195. The cost of waiting comes to $325,893 over two years and $1,948,766 over five. Rent over those periods adds $119,640 and $299,100 on top, at a typical area rent of $4,985.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating 1.8 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
