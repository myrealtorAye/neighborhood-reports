Clayton: the October 2026 read

Clayton would have been called Rhinesville if Charles Rhine had won the argument.

Clayton was nearly called Rhinesville. Joel Clayton and Charles Rhine cofounded the town and each wanted it named after himself. Clayton won, and he and his wife Margaret are buried in Live Oak Cemetery in what is now Concord. The city incorporated in 1964 after a failed attempt in 1960, pushed along by a Concord annexation move in 1963 and by Pleasant Hill's own 1961 incorporation on the other side. The vote on March 3, 1964 carried 251 to 61.

Clayton has 3,990 housing units and 2.1 percent of them predate 1950. 85.8 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 89.2 percent, and the ACS median value is 1,055,200 dollars. ZHVI is about 1,027,000 dollars, up 1.8 percent over the year.

89.2 percent owner occupancy across 3,990 units is among the highest in the county, and only 2.1 percent of the stock predates 1950. This is a small city of owner occupied postwar and newer houses at the foot of Mount Diablo.

So pull the year built and the permit history. On the newer subdivisions and the golf course communities, the association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, plus a written answer on whether club membership transfers with the property. On the hill addresses, soils, drainage, retaining structures and a written fire insurance quote on the exact address.

On the eastern edge, confirm whether the parcel is inside the city or in unincorporated county territory. And get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list and it is the single most useful number on a property profile. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. Those two documents cost nothing, they take a day, and between them they settle most of what a buyer actually needs to know before writing an offer.

What it costs to own here

Put the minimum down on the typical Clayton home and the monthly carry lands near $9,675. That is $116,099 across a year, counting loan, tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance. HOA dues are not in it, since no two are alike.

The 30-year fixed average is 7.4 percent as of 2026-10-15, against 6.27 percent in the same week last year. The conforming and FHA ceiling for a one-unit property in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125 in 2026.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Transfer tax on the typical Clayton home, $1,026,888, comes to roughly $1,130 at a rate of 1.10 per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $565 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $12,733.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about $43,366. Stretch it to five and the figure is $134,483.

Treat it as a scenario, not a prediction. The appreciation input is one year of observed change carried forward, and five years of compounding on one year of data flatters the result.
