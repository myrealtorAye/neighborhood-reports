Lakeshore, Newark: the October 2026 read

Lakeshore is one of Newark's planned residential neighborhoods, laid out around water features on the flat bay side of the city.

Newark is named after a castle in Scotland. J. Barr Robertson gave it the name, taken from Newark Castle at Port Glasgow. A company bought the land from Washington Township in 1878 to lay the town out, and before that, in 1853, Mayhew's Landing here held warehouses for wheat, hay and coal. The city came much later and it came by refusal: when the five townships around it were merging into Fremont, Newark's Chamber of Commerce objected to being zoned mainly for industry inside that new city and pushed to incorporate on its own instead. Newark became a city on September 22, 1955, the first new city in Alameda County in 47 years.

Newark has 16,015 housing units and 4.5 percent of them predate 1950. 67.9 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 69.4 percent, the ACS median value is 1,142,800 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,776.

So this is a postwar single family market and the consistency is the thing to use. Where the housing went up inside a narrow window, comparable sales genuinely compare and the whole price spread is condition and updates.

Pull the year built and the square footage, compare only within the same cohort, and put your effort into the inspection report.

The list for this era: the roof and its age, the supply lines, the electrical panel capacity if you intend to add load, the sewer lateral, the furnace and the water heater.

Then the water. It matters on this side of the city. Newark sits on flat ground at the bay margin, so get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire and ask the inspector about settlement and about the foundation specifically.

If the property sits inside a homeowners association, get the budget and the reserve study, not the dues number, and ask what it maintains on any water features or shoreline, because that can become an assessment.

Then check the zoning on the parcel and its neighbours, because industrial ground sits closer to housing in this city than in most, and that is the reason Newark incorporated in the first place.

Then drive your commute at your real hour.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Lakeshore is $1,185,129 as of 2026-09-30, down 0.08 percent from the prior month and down 2.4 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Listing activity in Newark for 2026-09. Active inventory is 83. 52 new listings came on, and 35 went pending. Median days on market is 29.

Median asking price is $1,198,000 at $692 per square foot.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Lakeshore is down 2.4 percent year over year. Realtor.com does not publish year over year listing figures at this geography, so the comparison below rests on the value index and the current month's listings.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What it costs to own here, month to month

Sellers underestimate this number, consistently, and it is the number that should set the pricing decision. Owning the typical home in Lakeshore runs about $11,145 a month, or $133,745 a year. That figure is the loan payment at 3.5 percent down and 7.4 percent, property tax at 1.42 percent, insurance at the California average, utilities for a home of roughly 1,800 square feet, and maintenance budgeted at 1 percent of value a year. HOA dues are not included, because they vary by building and subdivision.

The 30-year fixed average is 7.4 percent as of 2026-10-15, against 6.27 percent in the same week a year earlier, a move of up 1.13 percentage points. The 15-year average is 6.71 percent. These are the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey averages, published weekly, and they are survey averages and not a quote.

On a loan of $1,143,649, the typical home here with the minimum down payment, that year of rate movement is the difference between a payment of $7,057 and $7,918, a change of $862 a month on the same house at the same price. Rate movement has been the larger force on affordability here than price movement.

The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling. At the typical value here of $1,185,129, a 3.5 percent down payment leaves a loan of $1,143,649, comfortably inside the limit. Both FHA and conforming conventional are live options at this price point. That is not true in every neighborhood in this county, and it widens the program list considerably.

The ceiling still sets a practical top end. The most expensive home a minimum-down loan reaches in this county is about $1,294,430. Above that, the options narrow to a larger down payment or a jumbo loan.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $1,185,129 here, transfer tax runs about $1,304 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $652 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $17,777.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at -2.4 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 7.4 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $1,185,129 today becomes $1,129,832 in two years and $1,051,686 in five. The monthly payment moves from $7,918 to $7,549 and then $7,027. The cost of waiting comes to -$64,164 over two years and -$186,938 over five.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating -2.4 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
