Pleasanton: the October 2026 read

Pleasanton is spelled wrong, and it has been since 1894.

The city incorporated on June 18, 1894 and took its name from Major General Alfred Pleasonton of the Union Army. His name has an o in the middle. The town's does not. Nobody fixed it.

Before the name there was a rancho. Rancho el Valle de San Jose was granted in 1839 to the Bernal brothers and their brothers-in-law, and the settlement here was called Alisal in the 1850s when it worked as a mercantile stop on the Gold Rush road. In 1858 the Bernal family built a one-mile racetrack that is still the oldest in the country. John Kottinger is credited by most historians with founding the town: the railroad was announced in 1862, he began selling plots along the planned route the next year, and by 1869 there was a town with the transcontinental line through it. Kottinger is also credited with establishing the first commercial vineyards and winery in Alameda County here in the 1850s. At one point this valley was among the largest hop producers in the world.

Kottinger Ranch carries his name today, up on the hillside above the town he sold the first lots in.

What the city is now is new. Pleasanton has 28,975 housing units and only 2.5 percent of them predate 1950, so almost everything here is postwar or later. 61.1 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy is 67.0 percent, the ACS median value is 1,537,100 dollars and the median gross rent is 3,060.

So treat Pleasanton as two markets. Downtown, where the old street and the old housing are and where the walkability premium sits. And the tracts and hillside developments, where the stock is consistent, the comparable sales actually compare, and the variables are the homeowners association, the tax bill with its district assessments, and on the slopes the soils and the fire insurance.

Pull the year built, then the tax bill, then the association documents. In that order.

Inside it

This report covers 12 areas of Pleasanton. What each one actually is:

  • Birdland. Birdland takes its name from its streets.
  • Del Prado. Del Prado is a postwar Pleasanton tract and its published history is the city's, so this is that plus the development record.
  • Downtown Pleasanton. The town is spelled wrong on purpose, or at least it has been since 1894.
  • Golden Eagle. Golden Eagle sits on the hillside ground at the edge of Pleasanton, built late and built large.
  • Kottinger Ranch. This neighborhood is named for the man who sold the first lots in Pleasanton.
  • Laguna Oaks. Laguna Oaks is one of Pleasanton's later hillside developments, built large on big lots.
  • Mohr Martin. Mohr and Martin are the two roads this neighborhood is named for, on the flatter western side of Pleasanton.
  • Pleasanton Meadows. Pleasanton Meadows is a postwar tract on the valley floor and the name is as literal as it sounds.
  • Ruby Hill. Ruby Hill is a gated development on vineyard ground, and the vineyard part is older than almost anything else in this valley.
  • Val Vista. Val Vista is a postwar Pleasanton neighborhood on the valley floor, and its history belongs to the city, not the pocket.
  • Valley Trails. Valley Trails sits on the western side of Pleasanton near the arroyo, and its history is the city's.
  • Vintage Hills. Vintage Hills is named for the vineyards, and the vineyards are the oldest commercial thing in this valley.

What it costs to own here

Put the minimum down on the typical Pleasanton home and the monthly carry lands near $13,888. That is $166,662 across a year, counting loan, tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance. HOA dues are not in it, since no two are alike.

As of 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed averages 7.4 percent, up or down from 6.27 percent twelve months before. The conforming and FHA ceiling for a one-unit property in Alameda County is $1,249,125 in 2026.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Pleasanton charges 1.10 per thousand, so the typical $1,500,493 home owes roughly $1,651 at transfer. Convention puts about $825 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Emeryville at 26.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $37,512.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about -$102,628. Stretch it to five and the figure is -$296,196.

Treat it as a scenario, not a prediction. The appreciation input is one year of observed change carried forward, and five years of compounding on one year of data flatters the result.
