San Pablo Avenue corridor, Albany: the October 2026 read

San Pablo Avenue is Albany's other commercial street and it is a very different animal from Solano.

Albany is a small city wedged between Berkeley and the Contra Costa line, and almost half of its housing is older than the Second World War. Solano Avenue is the commercial spine and it runs straight through into Berkeley. That is the practical fact about living here: the shopping street does not stop at the city line. Albany Hill rises at the western end, and the Albany waterfront and the Bulb sit beyond it on the bay.

Albany has 8,070 housing units and 48.7 percent of them predate 1950, one of the highest pre war shares in this series. 50.3 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 53.1 percent, the ACS median value is 1,202,200 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,445. Nearly half the stock being pre war is the single most important thing to know before you make an offer.

So run the pre war list properly. Foundation bolting first. Then knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, the permit history on every addition, and the condition of original windows and siding.

Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing, because conversions are common in stock this age and the permits often are not.

Then the lot. Check the dimensions and setbacks early if an accessory dwelling unit is part of your plan, because Albany lots are small and the setback decides it.

Then walk Solano Avenue from the front door and time it. That walk, and the fact that it carries on into Berkeley without a break, is what the Albany premium actually buys.

San Pablo Avenue is the other kind of commercial street: wider, faster, more mixed in use, and carrying through traffic instead of foot traffic. That changes two things for a buyer. Pull the zoning for the parcel and for everything touching it, and ask the city what is permitted next door instead of reading what operates there now, because a corridor like this carries uses a residential block does not. And go stand at the address in the evening and listen, because proximity to an arterial is the single most common thing buyers underestimate and the easiest to check.

What it costs to own here

Carry on the typical home here is roughly $11,602 monthly, or $139,219 annually. That covers the loan payment, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance at a stated budget. HOA dues are excluded, because they differ building to building.

On 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed average was 7.4 percent, where the year-ago figure was 6.27 percent. In Alameda County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $1,237,577 here, transfer tax runs about $19,925 at 16.10 dollars per thousand. Convention puts about $9,962 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $18,564.

What waiting costs

On the same trailing rate, two years of waiting adds up to roughly $183,865 in price and payment combined, and five years to $609,356.

Treat it as a scenario, not a prediction. The appreciation input is one year of observed change carried forward, and five years of compounding on one year of data flatters the result.
