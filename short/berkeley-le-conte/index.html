Le Conte, Berkeley: the October 2026 read

The neighborhood is still called Le Conte. The school at the center of it is not.

LeConte Elementary stood at 2241 Russell Street under that name for generations. Joseph LeConte was a respected member of the UC Berkeley faculty, and he was also a slave owner and a committed supporter of the Confederacy. An effort to change the school's name got going around 2012, when the district was moving the school toward a two way Spanish and English immersion program, and in May 2018 the Berkeley school board voted unanimously to rename it for Sylvia Mendez. The Mendez family's court case ended legal segregation in California schools in 1947. Separately, in November 2020, UC Berkeley took the LeConte name off one of its own buildings along with Barrows Hall.

That sequence is a useful thing to know here, because the neighborhood name and the school name no longer match, and a buyer searching one will not always find the other.

The neighborhood itself is South Berkeley flatland between the campus area and Ashby, on a regular grid. The housing is early twentieth century for the most part, modest in scale, on small lots, with a significant share of it in small multi unit buildings and converted flats.

So unit count is the first question on any purchase here. Confirm with the city how many legal units a building has, because the number on a listing and the number the city recognizes are not always the same in this part of Berkeley. If it is more than one unit, read Berkeley's rent stabilization rules before you model income, since they are specific and they apply.

Then the age questions. Pull the year built and the permit history, and on anything pre war ask directly about foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring and galvanized supply lines.

And walk to the school at 2241 Russell before you decide. Most of what people like about this neighborhood is that it is flat, central and quiet, and you confirm that on foot.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Berkeley I cover, and what each one is:

  • Bateman. Nobody is entirely sure who Bateman was, and the neighborhood is named after him anyway.
  • Berkeley Hills. On September 17, 1923, a fire came over the ridge out of Wildcat Canyon and burned a large part of this area to the ground in a single afternoon.
  • Berkeley Marina. There is a pier here that used to run three and a half miles out into the bay.
  • Claremont. The Claremont Hotel could not legally serve a drink for decades, and the reason was a state law measured with a ruler.
  • Cragmont. Cragmont is named for the rock, and the rock is why the neighborhood looks the way it does.
  • Downtown Berkeley. Shattuck Avenue is wide and straight through downtown because it was a railroad before it was a street.
  • Elmwood. The oldest building in Elmwood's shopping district went up in 1907, one year after the San Francisco earthquake, and that timing explains how the neighborhood filled in.
  • Gilman District. Green Day's original graffiti is on a beam above the stage at 924 Gilman Street, from back when they were called Sweet Children.

What it costs to own here

The all-in monthly number for the typical home here is about $13,384, $160,606 over twelve months. It counts the payment, the tax bill, insurance, utilities and a maintenance allowance, and leaves out HOA dues.

Rates sit at 7.4 percent on the 30-year fixed as of 2026-10-15. A year earlier the same survey read 6.27 percent. The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming ceiling and the FHA ceiling.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Transfer tax on the typical Le Conte home, $1,442,475, comes to roughly $23,224 at a rate of 16.10 per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $11,612 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $1,600,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 26.10 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $16,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $1,600,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $21,637.

What waiting costs

Waiting two years at this place's own trailing rate costs about $278,021, counting the price increase and the payment increase together. Over five years it is $946,506.

That rate is one observed year extrapolated forward, not a forecast, and compounding a single strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Weight the two year figure more heavily.
