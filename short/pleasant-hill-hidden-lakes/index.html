Hidden Lakes, Pleasant Hill: the October 2026 read

Hidden Lakes is a Pleasant Hill neighborhood on the rising ground, around the lakes it is named for.

Pleasant Hill became a city to stop a neighbour. Concord was expanding west, and the voters here approved incorporation on November 7, 1961, with one city history giving November 14 and the sources differing by a week. That 1961 vote blocked Concord's westward expansion, and it is part of why Clayton on the far side kept pushing for its own city after a 1963 Concord annexation move. Diablo Valley College is here. The town had a post office from 1948, thirteen years before it had a city hall, and at least one account mistakes that post office year for the incorporation date.

Pleasant Hill has 13,939 housing units and 9.6 percent of them predate 1950. 60.0 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 62.6 percent, and the ACS median value is 1,077,100 dollars. ZHVI is about 973,000 dollars, up 1.3 percent over the year.

So the buying questions are mostly postwar questions.

Pull the year built and the permit history for the parcel. On a tract house of this era, ask about the electrical panel and its capacity, the supply lines, the sewer lateral, the roof, and whether the foundation has ever been bolted.

An original kitchen and original aluminium windows are either the discount or the asset. The inspection report tells you which.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Then the part specific to this address. On the hill ground, ask the inspector about soils and drainage on the parcel and about where water leaves it in a wet month. Ask about the condition and the ownership of any retaining structure. Get a written fire insurance quote on the exact address with the risk rating explained. Pull the tract map for the subdivision from the county recorder. The recording date, the original lot dimensions and the approved street layout are all on it, and that record beats anything a listing will tell you about when and how this neighborhood was built.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Hidden Lakes is $973,036 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.38 percent from the prior month and up 1.3 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Listing activity in Pleasant Hill for 2026-09. Active inventory is 57. 36 new listings came on, and 32 went pending. Median days on market is 27.

Median asking price is $912,000 at $584 per square foot.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Hidden Lakes is up 1.3 percent year over year. Realtor.com does not publish year over year listing figures at this geography, so the comparison below rests on the value index and the current month's listings.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What it costs to own here, month to month

Sellers underestimate this number, consistently, and it is the number that should set the pricing decision. Owning the typical home in Hidden Lakes runs about $9,211 a month, or $110,537 a year. That figure is the loan payment at 3.5 percent down and 7.4 percent, property tax at 1.31 percent, insurance at the California average, utilities for a home of roughly 1,800 square feet, and maintenance budgeted at 1 percent of value a year. HOA dues are not included, because they vary by building and subdivision.

The 30-year fixed average is 7.4 percent as of 2026-10-15, against 6.27 percent in the same week a year earlier, a move of up 1.13 percentage points. The 15-year average is 6.71 percent. These are the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey averages, published weekly, and they are survey averages and not a quote.

On a loan of $938,980, the typical home here with the minimum down payment, that year of rate movement is the difference between a payment of $5,794 and $6,501, a change of $708 a month on the same house at the same price. Rate movement has been the larger force on affordability here than price movement.

The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling. At the typical value here of $973,036, a 3.5 percent down payment leaves a loan of $938,980, comfortably inside the limit. Both FHA and conforming conventional are live options at this price point. That is not true in every neighborhood in this county, and it widens the program list considerably.

The ceiling still sets a practical top end. The most expensive home a minimum-down loan reaches in this county is about $1,294,430. Above that, the options narrow to a larger down payment or a jumbo loan.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $973,036 here, transfer tax runs about $1,070 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $535 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $11,676.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at 1.3 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 7.4 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $973,036 today becomes $997,965 in two years and $1,036,562 in five. The monthly payment moves from $6,501 to $6,668 and then $6,926. The cost of waiting comes to $28,927 over two years and $88,993 over five.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating 1.3 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
