Downtown Martinez, Martinez: the October 2026 read

Downtown Martinez is a county seat downtown, and it has been the county seat since 1850.

Martinez was laid out in 1849 by Col. William M. Smith and named for Ygnacio Martinez. It became the county seat in 1850 and it has been ever since. It could not incorporate at the time, because it did not have the 200 registered voters the law required, and it did not become a city until 1876. Rumours that the oil companies were coming set off a real estate rush in 1912, and Shell located here in 1915.

The John Muir National Historic Site is here. The house was built in 1883 by Dr. John Strentzel, Muir's father in law. Muir came to Martinez in 1880, lived in this house from 1890, and died in it in 1914. Martinez also claims the martini, from a local bartender's Martinez Special in 1849. San Francisco and several other cities claim it too, so the claim is disputed and should be told that way. Joe DiMaggio was born in this city.

Martinez has 15,178 housing units and 14.0 percent of them predate 1950, a high pre war share for this county, behind El Cerrito, Richmond and San Pablo. 63.7 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 70.8 percent, the highest of the six, and the ACS median value is 859,800 dollars. Citywide ZHVI is about 750,000 dollars, down 1.3 percent over the year.

So the first questions here are the old housing questions.

Pull the year built. On pre war stock, ask about foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, and the condition of the original windows and siding. Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing, and get the permit history.

Ask whether the building is nonconforming, because that limits what you can rebuild after a loss and it affects financing and insurance.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Then the part specific to this address. Downtown adds three. Pull the zoning for the parcel and its neighbours, because a county seat downtown mixes civic, commercial and residential use on adjacent lots. Ask the city whether the address falls in any historic district or specific plan area, since that governs what you may change on the outside of the building. And ask the inspector specifically about the ground floor, because downtown buildings of this age frequently have had commercial use in them.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $749,729 here, transfer tax runs about $825 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $412 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $8,997.
