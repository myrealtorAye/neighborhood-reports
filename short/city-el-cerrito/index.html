El Cerrito: the October 2026 read

El Cerrito was settled by people running from an earthquake, and it was called Rust.

El Cerrito was built by refugees and it was called Rust first. People leaving San Francisco after the 1906 earthquake settled on what was then Don Victor Castro's Rancho San Pablo. The post office opened in 1909 with Rust as the first postmaster on March 1 of that year. The residents disliked the name and changed it to El Cerrito, Little Hill. The village incorporated on August 23, 1917 with about 1,500 people, on a vote of 158 to 131.

The Cerrito Theatre at 10070 San Pablo Avenue opened in 1937 in Art Deco, closed in 1966, and was restored forty years later by the El Cerrito Redevelopment Agency. Victor Castro's adobe stood in the El Cerrito Plaza area until it burned in 1956.

El Cerrito has 11,247 housing units and 32.8 percent of them predate 1950, one of the highest pre war shares in the county. 66.6 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 58.6 percent, and the ACS median value is 1,124,400 dollars. ZHVI is about 1,122,000 dollars, up 3.4 percent over the year.

That 32.8 percent pre war share is the highest of any city in this county, ahead of Richmond, so the inspection list here is a pre war list more often than not.

So pull the year built first. Foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, the original windows and siding. On the hill addresses, soils, drainage, retaining structures and a written fire insurance quote on the exact address.

And note the two BART stations, Plaza and del Norte. Walk to whichever one serves the address and time it yourself, then ask the city about any station area specific plan covering the parcel.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list and it is the single most useful number on a property profile. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. Those two documents cost nothing, they take a day, and between them they settle most of what a buyer actually needs to know before writing an offer.

Inside it

This report covers 6 areas of El Cerrito. What each one actually is:

  • Cerrito Vista. Cerrito Vista is an El Cerrito neighborhood on the rising ground, near the park of the same name.
  • El Cerrito Del Norte. El Cerrito del Norte is the northern end of the city, around the BART station of that name.
  • El Cerrito Hills. The El Cerrito hills are the upper side of the city, on the slope above San Pablo Avenue.
  • El Cerrito Plaza. El Cerrito Plaza is the southern commercial centre of the city, and Victor Castro's adobe stood on this ground until 1956.
  • Fairmont. Fairmont is a central El Cerrito neighborhood, named like the school.
  • Mira Vista. Mira Vista is a hill neighborhood in El Cerrito, and the name needs care.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $1,122,098 here, transfer tax runs about $14,699 at 13.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $7,350 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $13,914.
