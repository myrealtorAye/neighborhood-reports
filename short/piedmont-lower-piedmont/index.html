Lower Piedmont, Piedmont: the October 2026 read

Lower Piedmont is the edge where the city meets Oakland, and in places the boundary runs down the middle of a block.

Piedmont exists because Oakland was about to swallow it. When it became clear that Oakland, wanting the tax base, was preparing to annex this hill, a charter was drawn up in a hurry and Piedmont incorporated on January 30, 1907. The boundaries were taken off a sewer line map, and that is why the city's edges weave in and out of Oakland the way they do. It became a charter city on December 18, 1922, and voters adopted the charter on February 27, 1923.

Piedmont has 4,073 housing units and 71.7 percent of them predate 1950, the highest pre war share of any city in this series. 94.7 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 90.9 percent, both also the highest. The ACS median value tops out at the Census ceiling of 2,000,001 dollars and the city ZHVI is 2,549,073. Those three figures together describe something unusual: a city that is almost entirely owner occupied detached houses, most of them a century old.

So this is a pre war single family market with essentially no other product in it, and the inspection is the whole job.

Get a specialist inspection on anything pre 1930 and ask specifically about foundation bolting, because a large house on a hill that has never been bolted is both a structural and an insurance problem.

Then knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, the sewer lateral, and the permit history on every addition. Houses of this size and age have usually been altered repeatedly and not all of that work was permitted.

Ask whether any local historic designation applies before you plan an exterior change.

On the sloped parcels, add the soils and drainage history, the condition and ownership of any retaining structures, and where water leaves the lot in a wet month.

Then confirm which city the parcel is actually in. Piedmont's boundary weaves through Oakland block by block, and city determines your school district, your transfer tax and your services. Take it from the assessor's parcel record, never from the mailing address.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $2,549,073 here, transfer tax runs about $35,942 at 14.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $17,971 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Emeryville at 26.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $63,727.
