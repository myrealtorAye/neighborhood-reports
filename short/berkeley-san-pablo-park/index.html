San Pablo Park, Berkeley: the October 2026 read

Berkeley bought the land for this park in 1907 for thirty five thousand dollars, and it is the oldest park the city owns.

The park came first and the neighborhood was built around it. San Pablo Park was developed in 1914, and the playground opened to the public that September, a month after Berkeley appointed its first general superintendent of playgrounds. The park was the centerpiece of the San Pablo Park development, a tract of modest houses laid out by the real estate firm of Duncan McDuffie.

That name is worth pausing on. McDuffie's firm also laid out Northbrae and Thousand Oaks up in the north Berkeley hills, donated the land around five volcanic outcrops to the city for parks in 1917, and he went on to serve as president of the Sierra Club and is regarded as one of the fathers of the California state park system. The same developer built the expensive hill subdivisions and this tract of small houses on the flats, and in both cases he put a park at the middle of it as the selling point.

The Frances Albrier Community Center was added to the park and dedicated on October 18, 1964, fifty years after the playground opened.

So the housing here is early twentieth century, modest in scale, on small flat lots on a tight grid, all of it oriented around a park at the center.

Four things to check. Year built and permit history, since small houses get added onto and in this part of Berkeley a lot of that work predates permitting. The pre war list: foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines. Whether the building has been split into more than one unit and whether the city agrees that is legal. And the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote, because this is flat ground west of the rail corridor.

Then go stand in the park on a Saturday morning. A neighborhood designed around a park either still works that way or it does not, and you can tell in fifteen minutes.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Berkeley I cover, and what each one is:

  • Bateman. Nobody is entirely sure who Bateman was, and the neighborhood is named after him anyway.
  • Berkeley Hills. On September 17, 1923, a fire came over the ridge out of Wildcat Canyon and burned a large part of this area to the ground in a single afternoon.
  • Berkeley Marina. There is a pier here that used to run three and a half miles out into the bay.
  • Claremont. The Claremont Hotel could not legally serve a drink for decades, and the reason was a state law measured with a ruler.
  • Cragmont. Cragmont is named for the rock, and the rock is why the neighborhood looks the way it does.
  • Downtown Berkeley. Shattuck Avenue is wide and straight through downtown because it was a railroad before it was a street.
  • Elmwood. The oldest building in Elmwood's shopping district went up in 1907, one year after the San Francisco earthquake, and that timing explains how the neighborhood filled in.
  • Gilman District. Green Day's original graffiti is on a beam above the stage at 924 Gilman Street, from back when they were called Sweet Children.

What it costs to own here

Figure about $13,384 a month to own the typical home in San Pablo Park, $160,606 a year. Loan, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance are in that figure. HOA dues are not, and they vary too much to average.

The survey average for a 30-year fixed is 7.4 percent at 2026-10-15, compared with 6.27 percent a year back. For 2026, one-unit financing in Alameda County tops out at $1,249,125 on both the conforming and the FHA side.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

San Pablo Park charges 16.10 per thousand, so the typical $1,442,475 home owes roughly $23,224 at transfer. Local practice splits it down the middle, roughly $11,612 each, though the contract decides.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $1,600,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 26.10 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $16,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $1,600,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $21,637.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about $278,021 here. The five year figure is $946,506.

One year of data, extended. That is worth saying plainly, because compounding it out to five years makes the total look more certain than it is.
