Bal, San Leandro: the October 2026 read

Bal is one of the smaller named pockets in San Leandro and its published history belongs to the city, so this is that plus the development record.

Rancho San Leandro went to Jose Joaquin Estudillo, born in Monterey in 1800, who served a year as alcalde of Yerba Buena before bringing his wife and ten children across the bay. They settled on San Leandro Creek just outside the Peraltas' Rancho San Antonio and were the first settlers in what became Eden Township. The grant ran from San Leandro Creek south to San Lorenzo Creek. Estudillo died in 1852. Casa Peralta at 384 West Estudillo Avenue, built 1901, has been on the National Register since January 4, 1982.

The orchards came next, cherries most of all, and the Cherry City name with them. Daniel Best Agricultural Works gave the town its first large sustained payroll. After the Second World War housing replaced the orchards.

San Leandro has 31,954 housing units and 26.2 percent of them predate 1950. 57.3 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 58.1 percent, the ACS median value is 844,200 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,186. Bal sits in that mix, with single family housing and some multi unit stock close to the arterials.

So pull the year built first and confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing, because conversions happen in this part of the East Bay and the permits often do not.

Then match the inspection to the era. Pre war: foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines. Postwar: the roof, the supply lines, the electrical panel capacity, the sewer lateral and the furnace.

Then the fault. It applies across this city. Ask whether the house has ever been bolted to its foundation and ask the title company whether the parcel sits inside an Alquist-Priolo earthquake fault zone.

Then check the lot and setbacks if an accessory dwelling unit is in your plan, and walk to the nearest BART access to see the real distance.

What it costs to own here

Figure about $7,779 a month to own the typical home in Bal, $93,344 a year. Loan, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance are in that figure. HOA dues are not, and they vary too much to average.

Rates sit at 7.4 percent on the 30-year fixed as of 2026-10-15. A year earlier the same survey read 6.27 percent. In Alameda County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $798,068 here, transfer tax runs about $9,657 at 12.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $4,828 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $11,971.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about -$6,634. Stretch it to five and the figure is -$19,916.

That rate is one observed year extrapolated forward, not a forecast, and compounding a single strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Weight the two year figure more heavily.
