Jack London, Oakland: the October 2026 read

Jack London drank in a bar down here that is still open, and he put it in his books seventeen times.

Johnny Heinold opened the First and Last Chance Saloon on this waterfront in 1883, though one city history gives the date as June 1, 1884. London was a local kid and the saloon became his base. It is the setting behind scenes in The Call of the Wild and The Sea Wolf, it gets referenced seventeen times in John Barleycorn, and it has been nicknamed Jack London's Rendezvous ever since. The floor still slopes from the 1906 earthquake.

Everything else about this district follows from the water. It was working port and warehouse ground, and the Amtrak station is here. That makes it the one Oakland neighborhood where you can walk from your door to a long distance train.

The housing is almost entirely converted warehouses and newer construction: lofts, condominiums and apartment buildings put into or built beside industrial shells. That is unusual for Oakland, where 46.6 percent of the 191,713 housing units predate 1950 and most of the older stock is houses instead of warehouses.

So shop it as a loft and condominium market with industrial neighbours, and ask the industrial questions.

Get the association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, and ask what the association insures. Ask whether the building has had a seismic retrofit and whether it is soft story or unreinforced masonry, because converted industrial buildings are frequently both and it changes your financing.

Ask the city what the surrounding parcels permit, because this is a working port district and the use next door can change in ways a residential neighborhood's cannot. Then ask about noise and about freight and train movement at the specific address, at night.

And on anything near the estuary, get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire.

Then go sit in Heinold's for twenty minutes. The building is a listed piece of Oakland and it costs you the price of one drink to understand the neighborhood's whole history.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Oakland I cover, and what each one is:

  • Acorn. Fifty blocks of this neighborhood were demolished.
  • Adams Point. Adams Point is named for Edson Adams, one of the men who got hold of Oakland's land early, and in 1906 it filled up with San Franciscans who had nowhere else to go.
  • Allendale. The Allendale tract map was recorded in 1892, and there was a cinema operating here by 1913.
  • Brookfield Village. Twelve hundred houses went up here during the Second World War and they sold for four thousand dollars each.
  • Bushrod. Bushrod was annexed by Oakland in 1897, in the same batch as Temescal, Golden Gate and Piedmont Avenue, and that date is the most solid thing in its published record.
  • Caballo Hills. Caballo Hills is a hillside pocket at the southeastern edge of Oakland with very little published history, so this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Castlemont. Castlemont takes its name from the high school at its centre, and its published history is thin enough that this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Chabot Park. Anthony Chabot dammed a creek in 1868 and built Oakland its first water supply at municipal scale, and his name is on the parkland at the edge of this neighborhood.

What it costs to own here

Figure about $7,190 a month to own the typical home in Jack London, $86,280 a year. Loan, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance are in that figure. HOA dues are not, and they vary too much to average.

Rates sit at 7.4 percent on the 30-year fixed as of 2026-10-15. A year earlier the same survey read 6.27 percent. The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming ceiling and the FHA ceiling.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Jack London charges 16.10 per thousand, so the typical $710,647 home owes roughly $11,441 at transfer. Convention puts about $5,721 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $2,000,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 18.60 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $2,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $10,660.

What waiting costs

Waiting two years at this place's own trailing rate costs about $9,161, counting the price increase and the payment increase together. Over five years it is $27,880.

This rests on one observed year carried forward. The two year figure is the defensible one; the five year figure is arithmetic, not a forecast.
