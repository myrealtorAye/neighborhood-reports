Fairview Hills, Hayward: the October 2026 read

Fairview Hills is on the unincorporated slope above Hayward, and that word unincorporated is the practical headline.

Parts of this area sit outside the city limits in county territory. That changes who issues your permits, who provides your services and what you are allowed to build. It is the first thing to establish on a specific address and it is not always obvious from a listing.

The ground itself is the Hayward Fault's work. The fault runs along the base of these hills the length of the East Bay, and the rise these houses sit on is a product of it.

Below, the city grew on fruit. Hayward incorporated in 1876, Hunt Brothers opened a cannery in 1895, and the district shipped cherries, apricots and peaches until postwar housing took the orchards.

Hayward has 52,243 housing units and only 9.1 percent of them predate 1950, so this is overwhelmingly a postwar city. 51.2 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 58.0 percent, the ACS median value is 854,400 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,391. On this slope the stock is later, larger lotted and predominantly single family.

So establish jurisdiction first. Confirm with both the City of Hayward and Alameda County which one governs the parcel, and get your permit and zoning answers from whichever it is.

Then the fault. Ask the title company whether the property is inside an Alquist-Priolo earthquake fault zone, request any seismic and soils report, and ask whether the house has ever been bolted to its foundation.

Then the hillside list: drainage history, retaining structures and their ownership, and where water leaves the property in a wet month.

Then a written fire insurance quote on the exact address, with the risk rating explained, because this is hill ground against open space and carriers price it accordingly.

And confirm what services come with the address. On unincorporated ground, refuse, sewer, water and road maintenance are not always the arrangement a buyer assumes.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Hayward I cover, and what each one is:

  • Burbank. Burbank is one of Hayward's older central neighborhoods, close in to downtown, and the published history belongs to the city, not the pocket, so this is that history plus the development record.
  • Downtown Hayward. The city was incorporated on March 11, 1876 under the name Haywards, with the s, and it was named after a hotel.
  • Eden Shores. Eden Shores is the newest ground in Hayward and it sits out on the bay edge.
  • Fairway Park. Fairway Park is postwar Hayward out toward the bay side, laid out around a golf course, and the name is literal.
  • Glen Eden. Eden is the old name for this whole area.
  • Harder Tennyson. Harder and Tennyson are the two roads this neighborhood is named for, in the southern part of Hayward, and the history here is the city's.
  • Hayward Highland. Hayward Highland sits on the slope above the city with the Hayward Fault running along the foot of it.
  • Hayward Hills. The Hayward Fault runs along the base of these hills, and it is the reason there are hills here at all.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Fairview Hills charges 9.60 per thousand, so the typical $816,424 home owes roughly $7,838 at transfer. Expect it split, near enough $3,919 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $12,246.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about -$44,655. Stretch it to five and the figure is -$130,051.

Treat it as a scenario, not a prediction. The appreciation input is one year of observed change carried forward, and five years of compounding on one year of data flatters the result.
