Del Prado, Pleasanton: the October 2026 read

Del Prado is a postwar Pleasanton tract and its published history is the city's, so this is that plus the development record.

The valley's record is deep even where a particular tract's is not. The ground was Rancho el Valle de San Jose, granted in 1839 to the Bernal brothers and their brothers-in-law. The Bernal family built a one-mile racetrack in 1858 and it remains the oldest in the country. John Kottinger is credited with founding the town after the railroad was announced in 1862, selling plots the following year, and he is also credited with the first commercial vineyards and winery in Alameda County here in the 1850s. The town incorporated on June 18, 1894, named for Major General Alfred Pleasonton, whose name has an o the town's does not. At one point Pleasanton was among the world's largest hop producers.

Pleasanton has 28,975 housing units and only 2.5 percent of them predate 1950. 61.1 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 67.0 percent, the ACS median value is 1,537,100 dollars and the median gross rent is 3,060.

So this is a postwar tract and the consistency is what to use. Where the housing went up inside a narrow window, comparable sales genuinely compare and the price spread is condition and updates, not vintage.

Pull the year built and the square footage, compare only within the same cohort, and put your effort into the inspection report.

The list for this era: the roof and its age, the supply lines, the electrical panel capacity if you intend to add load, the sewer lateral, the furnace and the water heater.

If the property sits inside a homeowners association, get the budget and the reserve study, not the dues number.

Then check the lot dimensions and the setbacks early if an accessory dwelling unit is part of your plan, because single storey houses on regular lots are the best candidates and the setback usually decides it.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Pleasanton I cover, and what each one is:

  • Birdland. Birdland takes its name from its streets.
  • Downtown Pleasanton. The town is spelled wrong on purpose, or at least it has been since 1894.
  • Golden Eagle. Golden Eagle sits on the hillside ground at the edge of Pleasanton, built late and built large.
  • Kottinger Ranch. This neighborhood is named for the man who sold the first lots in Pleasanton.
  • Laguna Oaks. Laguna Oaks is one of Pleasanton's later hillside developments, built large on big lots.
  • Mohr Martin. Mohr and Martin are the two roads this neighborhood is named for, on the flatter western side of Pleasanton.
  • Pleasanton Meadows. Pleasanton Meadows is a postwar tract on the valley floor and the name is as literal as it sounds.
  • Ruby Hill. Ruby Hill is a gated development on vineyard ground, and the vineyard part is older than almost anything else in this valley.

What it costs to own here

Figure about $13,888 a month to own the typical home in Del Prado, $166,662 a year. Loan, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance are in that figure. HOA dues are not, and they vary too much to average.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 1.10 dollars per thousand, a $1,500,493 sale in Del Prado carries about $1,651 in transfer tax. Local practice splits it down the middle, roughly $825 each, though the contract decides.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Emeryville at 26.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $37,512.

What waiting costs

Waiting two years at this place's own trailing rate costs about -$102,628, counting the price increase and the payment increase together. Over five years it is -$296,196.

Treat it as a scenario, not a prediction. The appreciation input is one year of observed change carried forward, and five years of compounding on one year of data flatters the result.
