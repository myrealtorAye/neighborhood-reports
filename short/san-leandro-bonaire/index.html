Bonaire, San Leandro: the October 2026 read

Bonaire is a postwar San Leandro neighborhood whose published history is the city's, so this is that plus the development record and the practical read.

The land was Rancho San Leandro, granted to Jose Joaquin Estudillo, born in Monterey in 1800 and once alcalde of Yerba Buena, who moved his wife and ten children across the bay and settled on San Leandro Creek. The grant ran from San Leandro Creek south to San Lorenzo Creek and Estudillo died in 1852. After the Gold Rush brought squatters, the ground went into orchards and cherries became the crop the city was known for. Daniel Best Agricultural Works brought the first large sustained payroll and industry followed the farming. Postwar, the orchards and rhubarb fields gave way to housing tracts.

San Leandro has 31,954 housing units and 26.2 percent of them predate 1950. 57.3 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 58.1 percent, the ACS median value is 844,200 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,186. Bonaire is part of that postwar cohort: single family on regular lots, consistent in era.

So use the consistency. Pull the year built and the square footage and compare only within the same cohort, because where the housing is this uniform the whole price spread is condition and updates.

Then put your effort into the inspection report. The postwar list is the roof and its age, the supply lines, the electrical panel capacity if you intend to add load, the sewer lateral, the furnace and the water heater.

Then the fault. The Hayward Fault runs along the hills immediately east of this city, so ask whether the house has ever been bolted to its foundation and ask the title company whether the parcel falls inside an Alquist-Priolo earthquake fault zone.

Then check the lot dimensions and the setbacks if an accessory dwelling unit is part of your plan, because single storey houses on regular lots are the best candidates here.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it.

What it costs to own here

The all-in monthly number for the typical home here is about $8,172, $98,064 over twelve months. It counts the payment, the tax bill, insurance, utilities and a maintenance allowance, and leaves out HOA dues.

Rates sit at 7.4 percent on the 30-year fixed as of 2026-10-15. A year earlier the same survey read 6.27 percent. The conforming and FHA ceiling for a one-unit property in Alameda County is $1,249,125 in 2026.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Transfer tax on the typical Bonaire home, $843,288, comes to roughly $10,204 at a rate of 12.10 per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $5,102 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $12,649.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about -$17,383. Stretch it to five and the figure is -$51,770.

That rate is one observed year extrapolated forward, not a forecast, and compounding a single strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Weight the two year figure more heavily.
