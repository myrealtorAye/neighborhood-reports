College Park, Pleasant Hill: the October 2026 read

College Park is the Pleasant Hill neighborhood next to Diablo Valley College.

Pleasant Hill became a city to stop a neighbour. Concord was expanding west, and the voters here approved incorporation on November 7, 1961, with one city history giving November 14 and the sources differing by a week. That 1961 vote blocked Concord's westward expansion, and it is part of why Clayton on the far side kept pushing for its own city after a 1963 Concord annexation move. Diablo Valley College is here. The town had a post office from 1948, thirteen years before it had a city hall, and at least one account mistakes that post office year for the incorporation date.

Pleasant Hill has 13,939 housing units and 9.6 percent of them predate 1950. 60.0 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 62.6 percent, and the ACS median value is 1,077,100 dollars. ZHVI is about 973,000 dollars, up 1.3 percent over the year.

So the buying questions are mostly postwar questions.

Pull the year built and the permit history for the parcel. On a tract house of this era, ask about the electrical panel and its capacity, the supply lines, the sewer lateral, the roof, and whether the foundation has ever been bolted.

An original kitchen and original aluminium windows are either the discount or the asset. The inspection report tells you which.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Then the part specific to this address. A college next door changes the street. Ask the city about parking restrictions on the block and look at the address on a weekday in term, not in July. Then pull the zoning for the parcel and its neighbours.

And pull the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll before you write anything, not the listing estimate. Read which districts and assessments appear on it and what each one charges. That document is free, it takes a day to get, and it is the difference between the payment a buyer expects and the payment they get.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What it costs to own here

The all-in monthly number for the typical home here is about $8,062, $96,742 over twelve months. It counts the payment, the tax bill, insurance, utilities and a maintenance allowance, and leaves out HOA dues.

The survey average for a 30-year fixed is 7.4 percent at 2026-10-15, compared with 6.27 percent a year back. The conforming and FHA ceiling for a one-unit property in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125 in 2026.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 1.10 dollars per thousand, a $839,461 sale in College Park carries about $923 in transfer tax. Expect it split, near enough $462 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $10,074.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about $36,166. Stretch it to five and the figure is $112,215.

One year of data, extended. That is worth saying plainly, because compounding it out to five years makes the total look more certain than it is.
