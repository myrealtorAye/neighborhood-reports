Highland Park, Oakland: the October 2026 read

Highland Park has thin published history of its own, so this is the development record and the practical read instead of a story somebody invented.

What places it is the 1909 annexation. Oakland's largest expansion took in Fruitvale, Elmhurst, Melrose and Fitchburg that year, carrying the city from 22.9 square miles to roughly 60 and doubling its population to 150,174 by 1910, most of that from people who left San Francisco after the 1906 earthquake. The East Oakland districts, this one included, are the product of that decade.

Geographically it sits in East Oakland on ground that rises away from the flats, and that rise is where the Highland name comes from. The Interstate 580 corridor was cut through this part of the city in the 1960s and it is the hard line between hill and flatland.

The sourced figures are the city's. Oakland has 191,713 housing units. 71,234 were built in 1939 or earlier and a further 18,124 between 1940 and 1949, so 46.6 percent of the city predates 1950. 41.5 percent of units are single family detached, owner occupancy is 42.3 percent, the ACS median value for an owner occupied home is 929,900 dollars and the median gross rent is 1,979. Zillow tracks 121 separate neighborhoods inside Oakland, and this is one of them.

So the work here is the standard older East Oakland work, and the first question is legal instead of cosmetic.

Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not from the listing, because conversions across these districts are common and the permits often are not. Ask whether the building is nonconforming, since that limits rebuilding after a loss and affects financing and insurance. If it holds more than one unit, read Oakland's rent adjustment program and the just cause for eviction rules before you model a dollar of income.

Then the pre war list: foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, and the sewer lateral Oakland certifies at sale.

On any sloped parcel, ask for soils and drainage history and get a written insurance quote on the exact address.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Oakland I cover, and what each one is:

  • Acorn. Fifty blocks of this neighborhood were demolished.
  • Adams Point. Adams Point is named for Edson Adams, one of the men who got hold of Oakland's land early, and in 1906 it filled up with San Franciscans who had nowhere else to go.
  • Allendale. The Allendale tract map was recorded in 1892, and there was a cinema operating here by 1913.
  • Brookfield Village. Twelve hundred houses went up here during the Second World War and they sold for four thousand dollars each.
  • Bushrod. Bushrod was annexed by Oakland in 1897, in the same batch as Temescal, Golden Gate and Piedmont Avenue, and that date is the most solid thing in its published record.
  • Caballo Hills. Caballo Hills is a hillside pocket at the southeastern edge of Oakland with very little published history, so this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Castlemont. Castlemont takes its name from the high school at its centre, and its published history is thin enough that this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Chabot Park. Anthony Chabot dammed a creek in 1868 and built Oakland its first water supply at municipal scale, and his name is on the parkland at the edge of this neighborhood.

What it costs to own here

Owning that typical home runs about $6,572 a month, $78,867 a year, once the loan, the property tax, insurance, utilities and a maintenance budget are all counted. HOA dues are not in it, because they vary by building.

The 30-year fixed average is 7.4 percent as of 2026-10-15, against 6.27 percent in the same week last year. In Alameda County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Transfer tax on the typical Highland Park home, $641,539, comes to roughly $10,329 at a rate of 16.10 per thousand. Convention puts about $5,164 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $2,000,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 18.60 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $2,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $9,623.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about $118,466. Stretch it to five and the figure is $401,343.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
