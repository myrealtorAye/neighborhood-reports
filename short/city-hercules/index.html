Hercules: the October 2026 read

Hercules is named after dynamite and 0.5 percent of its housing predates 1950.

Hercules is named after dynamite. The California Powder Works developed this site in 1881 to make its patented explosive, Hercules powder, and the town took the product's name. The company's managers incorporated the town itself on December 15, 1900, because they could not get the county supervisors to pass the laws they wanted for the plant. The corporate history is tangled: an earlier Hercules Powder Company was incorporated in 1882 by DuPont with Laflin and Rand and DuPont dissolved it in 1904, and a later one was incorporated on October 18, 1912 and began business in January 1913.

Hercules has 9,581 housing units and 0.5 percent of them predate 1950. For a town incorporated in 1900 that is the whole modern history of the place in one number: almost everything standing here went up long after the powder works. 57.9 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 79.2 percent, and the ACS median value is 769,400 dollars. ZHVI is about 736,000 dollars, down 0.7 percent over the year.

So this is a town with a nineteenth century industrial history and almost entirely modern housing, and those two facts shape different questions.

On the housing, the questions are newer-construction questions. Get the association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure. Get the tax bill for the address and ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in and when each expires.

On the ground, ask for the site history in writing, because this shoreline manufactured explosives for roughly a century and the record is public. On the shoreline developments, get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote and ask who maintains any bulkhead or levee the property sits behind.

And in the historic district, read the district rules before planning any exterior change.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Hercules is $735,539 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.41 percent from the prior month and down 0.7 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Listing activity in Hercules for 2026-09. Active inventory is 36, up 1.4 percent from the prior month and up 0.0 percent from a year earlier. 14 new listings came on, up 16.7 percent month over month, and 15 went pending. Median days on market is 39, up 14.7 percent month over month and up 8.3 percent year over year.

Median asking price is $618,500 at $442 per square foot, on a median listing of 1,428 square feet. Typical rent in the same area is $3,209.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Hercules is down 0.7 percent year over year. The median asking price of what is actively listed in Hercules is down 7.5 percent year over year, and price per square foot is up 4.9 percent.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at -0.7 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 400 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $735,539 today becomes $725,955 in two years and $711,813 in five. The monthly payment moves from $236,598 to $233,515 and then $228,966. The cost of waiting comes to -$83,572 over two years and -$481,642 over five. Rent over those periods adds $77,027 and $192,568 on top, at a typical area rent of $3,209.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating -0.7 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
