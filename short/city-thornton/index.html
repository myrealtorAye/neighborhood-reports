Thornton: the October 2026 read

Thornton is unincorporated San Joaquin County territory up on the Delta edge north of Stockton, and a quarter of its housing is pre war. Lodi to the east began as Mokelumne Station in 1869 and its renaming is given variously as 1873, 1874 and 1878.

Thornton has 291 housing units and 25.4 percent of them predate 1950, the highest pre war share of any place in this county. 71.5 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 60.4 percent, and the ACS median value is 435,400 dollars. ZHVI is about 451,000 dollars, flat over the year.

So the first questions here are the old housing questions.

Pull the year built. On pre war stock, ask about foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, and the condition of the original windows and siding. Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing, and get the permit history for the parcel.

Ask whether the building is nonconforming, because that limits what you can rebuild after a loss and it affects financing and insurance.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Confirm first that the address is unincorporated San Joaquin County territory, because it changes who issues your permits, who provides your services, who polices it and how your tax bill is built. Take it from the county, not from a listing. Then ask which school district serves the parcel. Get the flood zone determination for the parcel with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire, and ask the inspector about drainage and about where water leaves the property in a wet month. This is Delta and valley floor ground and levee and flood mapping drive the insurance. Thornton sits in Delta country and the levee question is the central one, so ask the county and the reclamation district which levee protects the parcel and what its certification status is. Ask whether the parcel is on sewer or on septic, and if septic, get the most recent inspection and the pumping record. Ask whether water comes from a district or a well, and if a well, get the yield and the water quality tests in writing. On valley agricultural ground, also ask what is farmed on the adjoining parcels and when it is sprayed.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. In an unincorporated place there is no city hall between you and the county, so your permits, your inspections and your service districts all come from the county and each district levies on the parcel. Ask the county's Local Agency Formation Commission whether the parcel sits in any city's sphere of influence, because that is where future annexation would start and it is a public record.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Thornton is $451,446 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.06 percent from the prior month and down 0.0 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Listing activity in Thornton for 2026-09. Active inventory is 2, up 100.0 percent from the prior month and up 100.0 percent from a year earlier. 0 new listings came on, and 1 went pending. Median days on market is 15, down 87.5 percent month over month and up 130.8 percent year over year.

Median asking price is $1,161,250 at $743 per square foot, on a median listing of 2,015 square feet.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Thornton is down 0.0 percent year over year. The median asking price of what is actively listed in Thornton is up 183.2 percent year over year, and price per square foot is up 110.9 percent.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at -0.0 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 400 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $451,446 today becomes $451,426 in two years and $451,397 in five. The monthly payment moves from $145,215 to $145,209 and then $145,199. The cost of waiting comes to -$172 over two years and -$1,002 over five.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating -0.0 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
