Marina Village, Alameda: the October 2026 read

Bethlehem Steel built ships on this ground from 1916 to 1948. Joseph Seiger and Michael Humphreys put the Marina Village complex up on it afterward.

That is the short version of Alameda's north shore. The strip along Clement Avenue facing the Oakland and Alameda estuary was industrial waterfront for most of the twentieth century, and the adjacent Alameda Marina site, roughly 44 acres of it, has run as an industrial boating and marine craft maintenance and storage operation since 1950. Some of that work is still there, mixed in with light industrial and professional tenants.

The site has been in play for two decades. In 2005 the owner, Pacific Shops Inc., wanted to sell. The Svendsen family formed a partnership to pursue it, and Bay West Development Group was later brought in to seek entitlements for mixed use and multifamily redevelopment. The city has designated the Alameda Marina site for mixed use redevelopment after a public process.

So this is the part of Alameda where the next large scale change is most likely to happen, and that is the single most important thing to understand before you buy here.

That is also the practical instruction. Go to the city and read the current entitlements and plans for the Alameda Marina site and for the parcels around any address you are considering. These are public documents, the process has been running for years, and what gets approved will change your view, your traffic and your value. That matters more here than the condition of any individual kitchen.

Then the ordinary work. Much of the housing on this side is attached or inside an association, so get the budget and the reserve study, not just the dues. Ask what the association insures. This is low ground on the estuary, so get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire, and ask the inspector about settlement and about the condition of any seawall or bulkhead the property depends on.

And remember the city charter. Alameda voters banned new multifamily construction in 1973 and capped density in 1991, and a 2020 repeal failed, so what can be built here is a live legal question and not a settled one.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Alameda I cover, and what each one is:

  • Alameda Point. This is 1,560 acres of the island that belonged to the United States Navy until 1997, and the Secretary of the Interior made the heart of it a National Historic Landmark District in January 2013.
  • Ballena Bay. Ballena Bay is an island off the side of an island, and a good part of it runs on a lease from the city.
  • Bay Farm. A man named Amos Mecartney tried to farm this ground in the 1870s and it did not work out.
  • Bronze Coast. The Bronze Coast sits between the Gold Coast and the rest of the island, and the name tells you exactly what it is: the same era, a step down in scale.
  • Central Alameda. Central Alameda is the part of the island everybody drives through and nobody can draw the boundary of.
  • Fernside. There was a seventy room mansion on Fernside Boulevard and it burned to the ground in 1897 with nobody in it.
  • Gold Coast. The name is not marketing.
  • Harbor Bay Isle. Harbor Bay Isle was supposed to be ten thousand homes.

What it costs to own here

Owning that typical home runs about $9,135 a month, $109,624 a year, once the loan, the property tax, insurance, utilities and a maintenance budget are all counted. HOA dues are not in it, because they vary by building.

The survey average for a 30-year fixed is 7.4 percent at 2026-10-15, compared with 6.27 percent a year back. For 2026, one-unit financing in Alameda County tops out at $1,249,125 on both the conforming and the FHA side.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Transfer tax on the typical Marina Village home, $954,035, comes to roughly $12,498 at a rate of 13.10 per thousand. It is normally split down the middle, about $6,249 on each party. The contract governs, not the custom.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $14,311.

What waiting costs

Waiting two years at this place's own trailing rate costs about $26,449, counting the price increase and the payment increase together. Over five years it is $81,265.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
