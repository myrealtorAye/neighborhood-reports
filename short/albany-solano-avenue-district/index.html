Solano Avenue district, Albany: the October 2026 read

Solano Avenue is the reason people move to Albany, and it does not stop at the city line.

Albany is a small city wedged between Berkeley and the Contra Costa line, and almost half of its housing is older than the Second World War. Solano Avenue is the commercial spine and it runs straight through into Berkeley. That is the practical fact about living here: the shopping street does not stop at the city line. Albany Hill rises at the western end, and the Albany waterfront and the Bulb sit beyond it on the bay.

Albany has 8,070 housing units and 48.7 percent of them predate 1950, one of the highest pre war shares in this series. 50.3 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 53.1 percent, the ACS median value is 1,202,200 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,445. Nearly half the stock being pre war is the single most important thing to know before you make an offer.

So run the pre war list properly. Foundation bolting first. Then knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, the permit history on every addition, and the condition of original windows and siding.

Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing, because conversions are common in stock this age and the permits often are not.

Then the lot. Check the dimensions and setbacks early if an accessory dwelling unit is part of your plan, because Albany lots are small and the setback decides it.

Then walk Solano Avenue from the front door and time it. That walk, and the fact that it carries on into Berkeley without a break, is what the Albany premium actually buys.

What to test before you pay the Solano premium. Walk the avenue from the front door of the specific address and do a real errand: groceries, a coffee, a haircut. Then keep walking east past the city line and see how far the usable stretch actually runs, because the commercial street continues into Berkeley and the useful part is longer than the Albany portion alone. Then come back at nine on a weeknight. A shopping street is a different neighbour after dark, and on a block this close to one you should know which version you are buying.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Solano Avenue district is $1,237,577 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.59 percent from the prior month and up 6.2 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Listing activity in Albany for 2026-09. Active inventory is 25. 18 new listings came on, and 9 went pending. Median days on market is 25.

Median asking price is $669,000 at $602 per square foot.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Solano Avenue district is up 6.2 percent year over year. Realtor.com does not publish year over year listing figures at this geography, so the comparison below rests on the value index and the current month's listings.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What it costs to own here, month to month

Sellers underestimate this number, consistently, and it is the number that should set the pricing decision. Owning the typical home in Solano Avenue district runs about $11,602 a month, or $139,219 a year. That figure is the loan payment at 3.5 percent down and 7.4 percent, property tax at 1.42 percent, insurance at the California average, utilities for a home of roughly 1,800 square feet, and maintenance budgeted at 1 percent of value a year. HOA dues are not included, because they vary by building and subdivision.

The 30-year fixed average is 7.4 percent as of 2026-10-15, against 6.27 percent in the same week a year earlier, a move of up 1.13 percentage points. The 15-year average is 6.71 percent. These are the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey averages, published weekly, and they are survey averages and not a quote.

On a loan of $1,194,262, the typical home here with the minimum down payment, that year of rate movement is the difference between a payment of $7,369 and $8,269, a change of $900 a month on the same house at the same price. Rate movement has been the larger force on affordability here than price movement.

The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling. At the typical value here of $1,237,577, a 3.5 percent down payment leaves a loan of $1,194,262, comfortably inside the limit. Both FHA and conforming conventional are live options at this price point. That is not true in every neighborhood in this county, and it widens the program list considerably.

The ceiling still sets a practical top end. The most expensive home a minimum-down loan reaches in this county is about $1,294,430. Above that, the options narrow to a larger down payment or a jumbo loan.

On a home at $1,237,577 here, transfer tax runs about $19,925 at 16.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $9,962 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at 6.2 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 7.4 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $1,237,577 today becomes $1,396,032 in two years and $1,672,555 in five. The monthly payment moves from $8,269 to $9,328 and then $11,175. The cost of waiting comes to $183,865 over two years and $609,356 over five.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating 6.2 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
