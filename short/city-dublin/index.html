Dublin: the October 2026 read

Dublin has three nineteenth century buildings and they are all on the same corner.

The Murray Schoolhouse opened in 1856 with fifty pupils. The Dublin Pioneer Cemetery was formally established in 1859. Green's Store opened in 1860. All three sit at what is now Dublin Boulevard and Donlon Way, and together they are effectively the city's entire pre modern built history.

How they got there starts with a grant. Jose Maria Amador received over 16,000 acres in 1835 for his service as a Mexican soldier and as administrator at Mission San Jose, and the Amador Valley carries his name. Michael Murray and Jeremiah Fallon arrived in 1846 and bought 982 acres from him in the 1850s with money made in the Gold Rush. Murray Township was created in June 1853 and named for Murray, a few months after Alameda County itself was formed. James Witt Dougherty bought 10,000 acres from Amador and became the largest landowner in the area. Two of those names are on the map today: Fallon Village, and Dougherty Road.

Then the valley sat. Dublin did not incorporate as a city until February 1982. That makes it the newest city in Alameda County, and almost everything you can see was built after that.

The figures say it plainly. Dublin has 25,157 housing units and 0.8 percent of them predate 1950, the lowest share anywhere in these three counties. 54.2 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy is 65.7 percent, the ACS median value is 1,317,100 dollars and the median gross rent is 3,191.

So the questions here are not the questions anywhere else in this series. There is no knob and tube wiring to find and no sewer lateral ordinance to budget for. What there is instead is the homeowners association and the tax bill.

Get the association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure. Then pull the actual tax bill for the address, because community facilities district assessments ride on top of the base rate across this city and they are easy to miss until the first bill arrives.

$1,227,704 is the typical Dublin home as of 2026-09-30. Month over month that is down 0.36 percent, and over the year down 5.5 percent.

Inside it

This report covers 11 areas of Dublin. What each one actually is:

  • Boulevard. Boulevard is one of the newest master planned communities in east Dublin, built on the last of the open ground.
  • Downtown Dublin. The oldest things in Dublin sit at the corner of Dublin Boulevard and Donlon Way, and there are three of them.
  • Dublin Ranch. Dublin Ranch is the largest of the master planned communities that turned east Dublin from open land into a suburb.
  • East Dublin. East Dublin is the reason this city grew from a crossroads to a suburb inside one generation.
  • Fallon Village. Fallon Village carries the name of Jeremiah Fallon, who arrived in this valley in 1846 and bought 982 acres from Jose Maria Amador in the 1850s with Gold Rush money.
  • Jordan Ranch. Jordan Ranch is one of the named communities in east Dublin, developed as part of the city's continuing eastern build out.
  • Positano. Positano is one of the master planned villages on Dublin's eastern side, built as part of the city's recent expansion.
  • Schaefer Ranch. Schaefer Ranch sits on the western hillside edge of Dublin, above the freeway, and it is one of the city's later developments.
  • Tassajara Hills. Tassajara Hills sits on the rising ground at Dublin's northeastern edge, where the city runs up against open hills.
  • Wallis Ranch. Wallis Ranch is one of the later master planned communities on Dublin's eastern side.
  • West Dublin. West Dublin is the older half of a city that is itself only incorporated since February 1982.

What it costs to own here

Put the minimum down on the typical Dublin home and the monthly carry lands near $11,516. That is $138,189 across a year, counting loan, tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance. HOA dues are not in it, since no two are alike.

As of 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed averages 7.4 percent, up or down from 6.27 percent twelve months before. In Alameda County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand, so the typical $1,227,704 home owes roughly $1,350 at transfer. It is normally split down the middle, about $675 on each party. The contract governs, not the custom.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $18,416.

What waiting costs

Hold off two years and the combined price and payment cost is near -$151,741. At five years it reaches -$422,060.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
