Rossmoor, Walnut Creek: the October 2026 read

Rossmoor is a 55 and over community and it was a cattle ranch first.

Walnut Creek was called The Corners until the post office renamed it in 1862. The Southern Pacific arrived in 1891. It became the eighth city in Contra Costa County on October 21, 1914, by a vote of 127 to 67, with a population of about 500 and Harry Spencer as its first board president. One account says the voters wanted incorporation partly so the town could tax itself to pave Main Street. BART arrived in 1973.

Stanley Dollar built an estate in Tice Valley in the 1930s, where the family bred Hereford cattle and raised show horses. In 1960 Dollar's son sold it to Ross W. Cortese of the Rossmoor Corporation. Cortese started out intending a Leisure World style community and dropped that plan shortly after breaking ground. Rossmoor opened in 1964. One warning on your own research: Cortese built a second, separate Rossmoor in Orange County between 1955 and 1961, and a search on the name returns both. Confirm that anything you read is about this one.

Walnut Creek has 34,389 housing units and only 5.3 percent of them predate 1950. 36.8 percent are single family detached, the lowest detached share of any large city in this county. Owner occupancy runs 64.4 percent and the ACS median value is 1,057,300 dollars. Citywide ZHVI is about 990,000 dollars, up 1.3 percent over the year.

So the buying questions here are newer-construction questions.

Get the homeowners association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, and ask specifically what the association maintains and what it does not. A thin reserve on a young association becomes a special assessment.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate, and ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in. Newer East County subdivisions frequently carry Mello Roos, and that line can run thousands a year and does not come off with the mortgage.

Then ask when the district is scheduled to expire, in writing.

Then the part specific to this address. This one is different from any other address in the city and three things come before the house. Confirm the age restriction and exactly how it applies to your household, in writing, because an age qualified community limits who may live there and who may inherit. Get the governing documents and read the transfer requirements. And ask your lender early whether it will finance in this community at all, since some will not.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What it costs to own here

Carry on the typical home here is roughly $9,361 monthly, or $112,338 annually. That covers the loan payment, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance at a stated budget. HOA dues are excluded, because they differ building to building.

The 30-year fixed average is 7.4 percent as of 2026-10-15, against 6.27 percent in the same week last year. Contra Costa County's 2026 one-unit loan limit sits at $1,249,125. That is the cap for conforming conventional and for FHA alike.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Rossmoor charges 1.10 per thousand, so the typical $990,472 home owes roughly $1,090 at transfer. Expect it split, near enough $545 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $11,886.

What waiting costs

Hold off two years and the combined price and payment cost is near $29,864. At five years it reaches $91,901.

This rests on one observed year carried forward. The two year figure is the defensible one; the five year figure is arithmetic, not a forecast.
