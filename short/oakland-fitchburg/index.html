Fitchburg, Oakland: the October 2026 read

Fitchburg was a town, Oakland annexed it in 1909, and the name has largely been replaced by Lockwood.

That is worth knowing before you search for it, because a buyer looking for Fitchburg and a buyer looking for Lockwood are frequently looking at the same blocks. The 1909 annexation was the biggest in Oakland's history. It brought in Fitchburg, Elmhurst, Fruitvale and Melrose together, took the city from 22.9 square miles to roughly 60, and followed a population that doubled to 150,174 by 1910 as people resettled from San Francisco after the 1906 earthquake.

Past the annexation and the name change, the published record on this district is thin, so the rest is the development record and the buying read.

The housing is East Oakland flats: older stock along the rail corridor, much of it divided into flats, with multi unit buildings and commercial frontage on the arterials and postwar infill mixed through. Oakland has 191,713 housing units, 46.6 percent of them built before 1950, 41.5 percent single family detached, with owner occupancy at 42.3 percent citywide and an ACS median value of 929,900 dollars.

So pull the year built first and do not reason from the house next door, because the eras sit side by side through here.

Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing. Get the permit history. Ask whether the building is nonconforming. If it holds more than one unit, read Oakland's rent adjustment program and the just cause for eviction rules before you model income.

On pre war stock run the full list: foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, and the sewer lateral Oakland certifies at sale. On postwar stock: the roof, the supply lines, the panel capacity and the furnace.

And on any parcel near the tracks, ask for the site history in writing. Industry followed the rail line through East Oakland for a hundred years and the record is public.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Oakland I cover, and what each one is:

  • Acorn. Fifty blocks of this neighborhood were demolished.
  • Adams Point. Adams Point is named for Edson Adams, one of the men who got hold of Oakland's land early, and in 1906 it filled up with San Franciscans who had nowhere else to go.
  • Allendale. The Allendale tract map was recorded in 1892, and there was a cinema operating here by 1913.
  • Brookfield Village. Twelve hundred houses went up here during the Second World War and they sold for four thousand dollars each.
  • Bushrod. Bushrod was annexed by Oakland in 1897, in the same batch as Temescal, Golden Gate and Piedmont Avenue, and that date is the most solid thing in its published record.
  • Caballo Hills. Caballo Hills is a hillside pocket at the southeastern edge of Oakland with very little published history, so this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Castlemont. Castlemont takes its name from the high school at its centre, and its published history is thin enough that this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Chabot Park. Anthony Chabot dammed a creek in 1868 and built Oakland its first water supply at municipal scale, and his name is on the parkland at the edge of this neighborhood.

What it costs to own here

Carry on the typical home here is roughly $4,699 monthly, or $56,388 annually. That covers the loan payment, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance at a stated budget. HOA dues are excluded, because they differ building to building.

As of 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed averages 7.4 percent, up or down from 6.27 percent twelve months before. For 2026, one-unit financing in Alameda County tops out at $1,249,125 on both the conforming and the FHA side.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Fitchburg charges 16.10 per thousand, so the typical $431,998 home owes roughly $6,955 at transfer. Expect it split, near enough $3,478 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $2,000,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 18.60 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $2,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $6,480.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about -$20,263 here. The five year figure is -$59,316.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
