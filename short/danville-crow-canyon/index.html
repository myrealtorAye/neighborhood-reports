Crow Canyon, Danville: the October 2026 read

Crow Canyon is the corridor on the southern side of Danville, along the road toward San Ramon.

Danville took four defeats to become a town. Valley wide incorporation went to a vote in 1964, 1967, 1973 and 1976 and failed every time. A home rule campaign started in 1980 and the voters approved it on June 8, 1982.

Eugene O'Neill won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1936 and spent the money building a house above Danville. Tao House is a Monterey Colonial by the architect Frederick Confer, and O'Neill and his wife lived in it from 1937 to 1944. In 1965 three local women, among them Darlene Blair and Lois Sizoo, formed an association to save it, and the place became a National Historic Landmark in 1971, a National Historic Site in 1976, and a National Park Service property in 1980. Getting in is unusual: the site is reached through a private gated road owned by neighbouring residents and visitors cannot drive their own cars to it.

Danville has 16,004 housing units and 1.8 percent of them predate 1950. 77.4 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 85.5 percent. The ACS median value is 1,681,700 dollars and ZHVI is about 1,821,000, up 0.2 percent over the year.

So the buying questions here are newer-construction questions.

Get the homeowners association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, and ask exactly what the association maintains and what it does not.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate, and ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in and when each one expires, in writing. A newly built valley subdivision can carry a community facilities district on top of the base rate, and that line runs for decades and does not come off with the mortgage.

Then ask the inspector about the grading and the drainage, because a young subdivision on engineered fill shows its settlement in the first fifteen years.

Then the part specific to this address. Drive Crow Canyon Road at your real commute hour in both directions. This is the route that carries the valley. Pull the tract map for the subdivision from the county recorder. The recording date, the original lot dimensions and the approved street layout are all on it, and that record beats anything a listing will tell you about when and how this neighborhood was built.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $1,821,427 here, transfer tax runs about $2,004 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Expect it split, near enough $1,002 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $22,586.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about $10,047 here. The five year figure is $30,434.
