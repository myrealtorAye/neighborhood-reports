Venetian Bridges, Stockton: the October 2026 read

Venetian Bridges is built on the waterways in north Stockton.

Stockton exists because a man gave up on gold. Captain Charles Maria Weber, who came from Germany, acquired Rancho Campo de los Franceses and founded the town in 1849. He tried mining first and worked out that selling supplies to the people going to the mines paid better. The site sat at the head of navigation on the San Joaquin River, so the goods came by water and the miners came to him. He laid the place out as Tuleburg and then settled on Stockton, for Commodore Robert F. Stockton. The city incorporated on July 23, 1850 and took its charter from the state in 1851.

The other thing every buyer here should know is the bankruptcy, because it is finished and the facts are public. Stockton filed Chapter 9 on July 28, 2012 against more than 2 billion dollars in labour agreements and long term debt, the largest bankrupt city in the country until Detroit filed the next year. A judge found it eligible on April 1, 2013. Chief Bankruptcy Judge Christopher Klein confirmed the plan of adjustment on October 30, 2014 and signed the confirmation order on February 4, 2015, and the city exited effective February 25, 2015. Every major creditor settled except Franklin Templeton Investments. The payment schedule under that plan runs to 2053.

Stockton has 105,162 housing units and 13.8 percent of them predate 1950. 66.8 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 53.9 percent, and the ACS median value is 440,900 dollars. ZHVI is about 422,000 dollars, down 1.7 percent over the year, the lowest level of any city in the three counties covered here.

So the buying questions are mostly postwar questions.

Pull the year built and the permit history for the parcel. On a tract house of this era, ask about the electrical panel and its capacity, the supply lines, the sewer lateral, the roof, and whether the foundation has ever been bolted.

An original kitchen and original aluminium windows are either the discount or the asset. The inspection report tells you which.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Then the part specific to this address. Get the flood zone determination for the parcel with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire, and ask the inspector about drainage and about where water leaves the property in a wet month. This is Delta and valley floor ground and levee and flood mapping drive the insurance. Ask who owns and who maintains the channel and the bridges, and whether either is an association obligation. Those answers are the purchase out here.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Venetian Bridges is $422,451 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.10 percent from the prior month and down 1.7 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Median asking price is $423,750 at $279 per square foot.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Venetian Bridges is down 1.7 percent year over year. Realtor.com does not publish year over year listing figures at this geography, so the comparison below rests on the value index and the current month's listings.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at -1.7 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 400 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $422,451 today becomes $408,322 in two years and $388,008 in five. The monthly payment moves from $135,889 to $131,343 and then $124,809. The cost of waiting comes to -$123,213 over two years and -$699,208 over five.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating -1.7 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
