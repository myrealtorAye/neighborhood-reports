Alvarado, Union City: the October 2026 read

Alvarado was the county seat of Alameda County, and it held that job from 1853.

The Horner brothers made the first non-native settlement here in 1850. Henry C. Smith founded New Haven in 1851, and in 1853 New Haven combined with the nearby town of Alvarado and became the county seat of the newly created Alameda County. Alvarado carries the name of Governor Juan Alvarado, the same governor who granted Guillermo Castro the rancho that became Castro Valley. In 1870 the California Beet Sugar Company opened at Alvarado and ran until 1873, the first successful sugar beet factory in the United States. Ezra Decoto sold land to the railroad, in 1867 by one account and 1870 by another, and the settlement that grew there took his name.

The county seat moved away long ago and Alvarado became a district of a city that did not exist yet. Union City itself was created when Alvarado and Decoto voted to incorporate together, on January 13, 1959 by one account and formally on January 26 by another. The petition was filed to preclude annexation by Hayward. Tom Kitayama, who sat on the citizens committee that pushed it, became the first mayor.

Union City has 21,901 housing units and 4.0 percent of them predate 1950. 57.8 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 64.7 percent, the ACS median value is 1,104,900 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,713. Old Alvarado holds the oldest stock in the city.

So pull the year built, because this is one of the few parts of Union City where genuinely old buildings exist.

Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing, get the permit history, and ask whether the building is nonconforming.

On pre war stock: foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, and the permit history on every addition. Ask whether the property carries any local historic designation before planning an exterior change.

Then the ground. This is the flat western side of the county near the bay, so get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire and ask the inspector about settlement.

Then walk the old Alvarado street grid. A nineteenth century county seat inside a 1959 city is an odd thing to find and most people drive straight past it.

What it costs to own here

Put the minimum down on the typical Alvarado home and the monthly carry lands near $11,237. That is $134,845 across a year, counting loan, tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance. HOA dues are not in it, since no two are alike.

Rates sit at 7.4 percent on the 30-year fixed as of 2026-10-15. A year earlier the same survey read 6.27 percent. For 2026, one-unit financing in Alameda County tops out at $1,249,125 on both the conforming and the FHA side.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Alvarado charges 1.10 per thousand, so the typical $1,195,668 home owes roughly $1,315 at transfer. Custom is to split it, so about $658 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $17,935.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about -$52,718. Stretch it to five and the figure is -$154,610.

This rests on one observed year carried forward. The two year figure is the defensible one; the five year figure is arithmetic, not a forecast.
