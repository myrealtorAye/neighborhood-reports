Discovery Bay: the October 2026 read

Discovery Bay is a Delta community with navigable water running into the neighborhoods, and 90.1 percent of it is owner occupied.

Discovery Bay is unincorporated Contra Costa County territory out on the San Joaquin River Delta east of Brentwood, built as a planned waterfront community. Brentwood nearby takes its name from John Marsh's ancestral home in Essex, England, was bought up by the British investment house Balfour, Guthrie and Co. in 1910, and incorporated on January 21, 1948. Oakley to the north became the county's newest city on July 1, 1999. Discovery Bay stayed county ground.

Discovery Bay has 6,053 housing units and 2.8 percent of them predate 1950. 90.8 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 90.1 percent, among the highest in the county. The ACS median value is 832,000 dollars and ZHVI is about 775,000 dollars, down 0.9 percent over the year.

So the water is most of the purchase and it carries the questions. Get the flood zone determination for the parcel with a written insurance quote, and ask the inspector about settlement and about the condition and ownership of any bulkhead, levee or revetment the property sits behind. On the water that is the most expensive thing that can go wrong and it is frequently shared. Ask who owns and who maintains the channel and the dock, get the dock permit and its condition in writing, and ask whether dredging is an association obligation and when it was last done.

A Delta levee is an insurance question too, so get the flood determination with a written quote before your contingencies expire. Then confirm the parcel is unincorporated and get the association budget and the reserve study.

One thing worth spelling out, because it catches buyers. In an unincorporated place there is no city hall standing between you and the county. Your building permits and inspections come from the county, code enforcement is the county's, the Sheriff answers calls unless a special district provides policing, and fire, water and sewer frequently come from separate districts that each levy on the parcel. So get the full list of districts for the address off the county's tax roll, not the listing, and read what each one charges. Then ask the county's Local Agency Formation Commission whether the parcel sits in any city's sphere of influence, because that is where future annexation would start and it is a public record.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list and it is the single most useful number on a property profile. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. Those two documents cost nothing, they take a day, and between them they settle most of what a buyer actually needs to know before writing an offer.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $775,031 here, transfer tax runs about $853 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $426 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $9,300.
