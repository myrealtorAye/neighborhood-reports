Prescott, Oakland: the October 2026 read

The transcontinental railroad ended here. Not near here. Here.

The 16th Street Station opened in 1912 as the western terminus of the transcontinental line, a Beaux-Arts building by the architect Jarvis Hunt and once the largest Southern Pacific station in Northern California. It also became the western headquarters of the Brotherhood of Sleeping Car Porters, the union the porters organised in 1925 and the first Black union in the country. Its headquarters were in Chicago, but its vice president was based in Oakland. C. L. Dellums worked as a Pullman porter from 1924 to 1927 and was let go partly for openly supporting the union. He was elected a vice president in 1929 and later became its president, in 1968 by one account and 1966 by another. He was the uncle of Ron Dellums, later a congressman and mayor of Oakland.

The station took damage in the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake and closed soon after. Amtrak had already moved its trains to Jack London Square and Emeryville during the 1980s. The building is on the National Register and it is still standing, still empty, and still the subject of a restoration campaign.

Prescott runs from Mandela Parkway east to Frontage Road west, between West Grand Avenue and 7th Street, and it takes its name from the Prescott School. Seventh Street was the music district. In the 1940s and 1950s it was lined with jazz clubs and bars and people called it the Harlem of the West.

Then the Cypress Freeway went through in the 1950s, cut West Oakland in two and isolated Oakland Point from the rest, and that is where the nickname Lower Bottoms comes from.

The housing is Victorian and early twentieth century, and a lot of it holds more than one household. Oakland has 191,713 units with 46.6 percent built before 1950, and here that share is near total.

So confirm the legal unit count with the city before anything else, get the permit history, and read Oakland's rent adjustment and just cause rules before you model income. Then the pre war list: foundation bolting, knob and tube, galvanized lines, and the sewer lateral Oakland certifies at sale.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Oakland I cover, and what each one is:

  • Acorn. Fifty blocks of this neighborhood were demolished.
  • Adams Point. Adams Point is named for Edson Adams, one of the men who got hold of Oakland's land early, and in 1906 it filled up with San Franciscans who had nowhere else to go.
  • Allendale. The Allendale tract map was recorded in 1892, and there was a cinema operating here by 1913.
  • Brookfield Village. Twelve hundred houses went up here during the Second World War and they sold for four thousand dollars each.
  • Bushrod. Bushrod was annexed by Oakland in 1897, in the same batch as Temescal, Golden Gate and Piedmont Avenue, and that date is the most solid thing in its published record.
  • Caballo Hills. Caballo Hills is a hillside pocket at the southeastern edge of Oakland with very little published history, so this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Castlemont. Castlemont takes its name from the high school at its centre, and its published history is thin enough that this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Chabot Park. Anthony Chabot dammed a creek in 1868 and built Oakland its first water supply at municipal scale, and his name is on the parkland at the edge of this neighborhood.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 16.10 dollars per thousand, a $587,799 sale in Prescott carries about $9,464 in transfer tax. Custom is to split it, so about $4,732 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $2,000,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 18.60 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $2,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $8,817.

What waiting costs

Waiting two years at this place's own trailing rate costs about -$31,478, counting the price increase and the payment increase together. Over five years it is -$91,746.

That rate is one observed year extrapolated forward, not a forecast, and compounding a single strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Weight the two year figure more heavily.
