Glen Eden, Hayward: the October 2026 read

Eden is the old name for this whole area. Hayward sat in Eden Township, and the name survives on neighborhoods like this one long after the township stopped meaning anything administratively.

What the township was is the agricultural district that made the city. From the later nineteenth century this was one of California's leading fruit growing areas, shipping cherries, apricots, peaches, tomatoes and potatoes out by rail. Hunt Brothers opened a cannery in 1895 that anchored the local economy through each season. By 1950 Hayward had about 14,000 residents and the nickname Apricot City. Then the war ended and the orchards went under housing.

Glen Eden is part of that postwar build out, in the southern part of the city.

Hayward has 52,243 housing units and only 9.1 percent of them predate 1950, so this is overwhelmingly a postwar city. 51.2 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 58.0 percent, the ACS median value is 854,400 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,391. Here the stock is postwar single family on regular lots, and the consistency is the useful part: where most houses went up inside a narrow window, the comparable sales genuinely compare and the price spread is condition, not era.

So pull the year built and compare only within it, then put the effort into the inspection, because that is where the money is in a uniform tract.

The postwar list: the roof and its age, the supply lines, the electrical panel capacity if you plan to add load, the sewer lateral, the furnace and the water heater.

Then the fault. It applies across this city. Ask whether the house has ever been bolted to its foundation and ask the title company whether the parcel sits inside an Alquist-Priolo earthquake fault zone.

Then check the lot dimensions and setbacks if an accessory dwelling unit is any part of your plan, because single storey houses on regular lots are the best candidates and the setback is usually the binding constraint.

Then drive your commute at your real hour.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Hayward I cover, and what each one is:

  • Burbank. Burbank is one of Hayward's older central neighborhoods, close in to downtown, and the published history belongs to the city, not the pocket, so this is that history plus the development record.
  • Downtown Hayward. The city was incorporated on March 11, 1876 under the name Haywards, with the s, and it was named after a hotel.
  • Eden Shores. Eden Shores is the newest ground in Hayward and it sits out on the bay edge.
  • Fairview Hills. Fairview Hills is on the unincorporated slope above Hayward, and that word unincorporated is the practical headline.
  • Fairway Park. Fairway Park is postwar Hayward out toward the bay side, laid out around a golf course, and the name is literal.
  • Harder Tennyson. Harder and Tennyson are the two roads this neighborhood is named for, in the southern part of Hayward, and the history here is the city's.
  • Hayward Highland. Hayward Highland sits on the slope above the city with the Hayward Fault running along the foot of it.
  • Hayward Hills. The Hayward Fault runs along the base of these hills, and it is the reason there are hills here at all.

What it costs to own here

Put the minimum down on the typical Glen Eden home and the monthly carry lands near $8,081. That is $96,968 across a year, counting loan, tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance. HOA dues are not in it, since no two are alike.

As of 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed averages 7.4 percent, up or down from 6.27 percent twelve months before. Alameda County's 2026 one-unit loan limit sits at $1,249,125. That is the cap for conforming conventional and for FHA alike.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Figure $7,995 in transfer tax on a $832,781 sale here, at 9.60 dollars per thousand. Local practice splits it down the middle, roughly $3,997 each, though the contract decides.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $12,492.

What waiting costs

Hold off two years and the combined price and payment cost is near -$33,452. At five years it reaches -$98,359.

One year of data, extended. That is worth saying plainly, because compounding it out to five years makes the total look more certain than it is.
