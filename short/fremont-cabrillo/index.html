Cabrillo, Fremont: the October 2026 read

Cabrillo is a 1960s tract neighborhood in north Fremont, and the useful facts about it are in the development record instead of in any story.

Cabrillo Elementary sits at 36700 San Pedro Drive, and the street names around it run Spanish. That is where the neighborhood's name comes from. Local guides put the typical house here at three bedrooms, two baths and somewhere between 1,100 and 1,500 square feet, built in the 1960s, mostly traditional ranch. Those figures come from real estate sources instead of from the assessor, so treat them as the shape of the stock and not as gospel.

The sourced picture for the city around it is firmer. Fremont has 81,579 housing units. 977 of them were built in 1939 or earlier and 749 between 1940 and 1949, so about 2 percent of the city predates 1950. 45,927 units, or 56.3 percent, are single family detached. Owner occupancy citywide is 60.8 percent. The ACS median value for an owner occupied home in Fremont is 1,403,800 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,933. The median listing on the market runs about 1,489 square feet, putting the typical Cabrillo house at or a little under the citywide middle.

That matters more than it sounds. A neighborhood of near identical houses from one decade is the easiest kind of market to price and the hardest kind to overpay in, provided you do the comparison properly.

So pull the year built and the square footage and compare only against the same era and size. Then look at what has been done to the house. In a tract where the bones are all the same, the entire price spread is condition and updates, and that is a negotiation you can win with an inspection report.

Check the lot dimensions and the setbacks if an ADU is part of the plan. Single story on a regular lot is the best case in this city.

Then walk the block in the evening. In a neighborhood this uniform, the street is the variable.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Fremont I cover, and what each one is:

  • Ardenwood. A Pennsylvania farmer named George Washington Patterson came to California for gold in 1849, found almost none, and in 1857 built a redwood farmhouse here instead.
  • Baylands. This is the edge of Fremont where the city stops and the bay starts, and most of it is federally protected.
  • Brookvale. Brookvale is one of the Fremont neighborhoods where the published history is genuinely thin, so this is the development record and the practical read, not a story somebody made up.
  • Centerville. There is a cemetery at Post Street and Bonde Way that is older than the city around it by a century.
  • Cherry Guardino. Cherry-Guardino takes its name from its two main streets, and like several Fremont tract neighborhoods it has almost no published history.
  • City Center. On September 6, 1960 the Fremont City Council picked the Fudenna ranch as the site for its civic center.
  • Glenmoor. Glenmoor has a homeowners association that was incorporated in March 1953, when there were no more than 75 houses standing in the whole subdivision.
  • Grimmer. Grimmer is named for a family nobody has documented properly, and the honest thing is to say so.

What it costs to own here

The all-in monthly number for the typical home here is about $12,424, $149,082 over twelve months. It counts the payment, the tax bill, insurance, utilities and a maintenance allowance, and leaves out HOA dues.

On 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed average was 7.4 percent, where the year-ago figure was 6.27 percent. The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming ceiling and the FHA ceiling.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Figure $1,465 in transfer tax on a $1,332,071 sale here, at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Local practice splits it down the middle, roughly $733 each, though the contract decides.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $19,981.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about $2,347 here. The five year figure is $7,093.

Treat it as a scenario, not a prediction. The appreciation input is one year of observed change carried forward, and five years of compounding on one year of data flatters the result.
