Bay Point: the October 2026 read

Bay Point is unincorporated, and its ACS median value of 525,000 dollars is among the lowest of any place covered in this county.

Bay Point is unincorporated Contra Costa County territory on the Suisun Bay shoreline between Concord and Pittsburg. The national memorial at Port Chicago is on this water. On July 17, 1944 at 10:08 at night an explosion there killed 320 men, the worst stateside disaster of World War II. The trial that followed was the largest naval mutiny case in United States history and it led to the desegregation of the Navy. The Port Chicago Naval Magazine National Memorial was established in 1992 and became a unit of the national park system in 2009. It sits on an active military base and can only be visited by reservation.

Bay Point has 6,914 housing units and 7.9 percent of them predate 1950. 68.6 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 55.6 percent.

A scope note on price. Neither Zillow nor Realtor.com publishes a separate value series for this community, so there is no citywide figure to quote for it. Work from closed comparable sales on the specific streets and treat any citywide number a listing shows you as belonging to somewhere else.

So confirm the parcel is unincorporated county territory first, because it changes your permits, your services, your policing and your tax bill. Then pull the zoning for the parcel and everything touching it and ask for the site history in writing, because this shoreline has a century of industry and military use in it.

Ask your insurance broker how the industrial and military proximity is rated for the exact address, and ask the county what notification and emergency systems cover it. Then pull the year built and the permit history.

One thing worth spelling out, because it catches buyers. In an unincorporated place there is no city hall standing between you and the county. Your building permits and inspections come from the county, code enforcement is the county's, the Sheriff answers calls unless a special district provides policing, and fire, water and sewer frequently come from separate districts that each levy on the parcel. So get the full list of districts for the address off the county's tax roll, not the listing, and read what each one charges. Then ask the county's Local Agency Formation Commission whether the parcel sits in any city's sphere of influence, because that is where future annexation would start and it is a public record.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list and it is the single most useful number on a property profile. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. Those two documents cost nothing, they take a day, and between them they settle most of what a buyer actually needs to know before writing an offer.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $559,194 here, transfer tax runs about $615 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $308 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $6,710.
