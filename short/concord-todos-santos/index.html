Todos Santos, Concord: the October 2026 read

Concord's town square is older than the city's name.

Concord started as a Mexican land grant. Rancho Monte del Diablo went to Salvio Pacheco in 1834. In 1868 the Pacheco family and Francisco Galindo laid out a town on it called Todos Santos, and the Contra Costa Gazette announced the new name Concord on April 17, 1869. The plaza at the centre of it covers 2.5 acres, bounded by Salvio Street, Willow Pass Road, Grant Street and Mount Diablo Street. Sources put its dedication at 1868 and at 1869, so the exact year is contested. In 2018 the American Planning Association named it one of three Great Places in California.

A detail most buyers never hear: a number of the historic houses standing around the plaza were not built where they sit. They were moved here from other parts of Concord to keep them from being demolished.

The farmers market runs on Tuesdays year round, with a second market on Thursday evenings in the warm months. Published hours conflict across three sources, so check the city's current schedule and do not trust a listing on it.

Concord has 47,703 housing units and 6.7 percent of them predate 1950. 58.7 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 61.6 percent, and the ACS median value is 797,600 dollars. The city's own figure is that 73 percent of its existing homes went up between 1950 and 1979. Around the plaza the pre war share runs above that citywide 6.7 percent.

So the buying questions in Concord are mostly postwar questions.

Pull the year built and the permit history for the parcel. On a 1950s or 1960s tract house, ask about the electrical panel and its capacity, the supply lines, the sewer lateral, the roof, and whether the foundation has ever been bolted.

Original aluminium windows and an original kitchen are either the discount or the asset here. The inspection report tells you which.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate, and ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in.

Downtown adds two more. Pull the zoning for the parcel and its neighbours, because the blocks off the plaza mix commercial and residential and what is permitted next door is not always what operates there now. And ask the city whether the address falls in any downtown specific plan area, since that governs what can be built across the street from you.

One thing hangs over every address in this city. The former Concord Naval Weapons Station covers roughly 2,225 acres and the reuse plan calls for more than 12,000 homes, 6 million square feet of commercial space and more than 800 acres of parks, with Brookfield Properties as master developer. The city announced on September 24, 2026 that the Navy had approved the term sheet, and the governor signed SB 328, exempting the project from the Surplus Land Act and streamlining its environmental review. The site is a federal Superfund site with lead and arsenic in it and the Navy has to finish the cleanup before anything gets built. Nobody can tell you the delivery date.

And ask your agent to put the base reuse timeline in writing as a range, not a date. A buyer three miles from 12,000 future homes is making a bet on traffic, schools and supply, and nobody honest will give you a single year for it.

Then stand in the plaza on a Tuesday and again on a Sunday. It is the same two and a half acres and it is a different place.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Todos Santos is $720,907 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.37 percent from the prior month and up 0.2 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Listing activity in Concord for 2026-09. Active inventory is 203. 86 new listings came on, and 70 went pending. Median days on market is 40.

Median asking price is $751,719 at $467 per square foot.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Todos Santos is up 0.2 percent year over year. Realtor.com does not publish year over year listing figures at this geography, so the comparison below rests on the value index and the current month's listings.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What it costs to own here, month to month

Sellers underestimate this number, consistently, and it is the number that should set the pricing decision. Owning the typical home in Todos Santos runs about $234,117 a month, or $2,809,398 a year. That figure is the loan payment at 3.5 percent down and 400 percent, property tax at 1.31 percent, insurance at the California average, utilities for a home of roughly 1,800 square feet, and maintenance budgeted at 1 percent of value a year. Rate movement has been the larger force on affordability here than price movement.

The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling. At the typical value here of $720,907, a 3.5 percent down payment leaves a loan of $695,675, comfortably inside the limit. Both FHA and conforming conventional are live options at this price point. That is not true in every neighborhood in this county, and it widens the program list considerably.

The ceiling still sets a practical top end. The most expensive home a minimum-down loan reaches in this county is about $1,294,430. Above that, the options narrow to a larger down payment or a jumbo loan.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $720,907 here, transfer tax runs about $793 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $396 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $8,651.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at 0.2 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 400 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $720,907 today becomes $723,516 in two years and $727,448 in five. The monthly payment moves from $231,892 to $232,731 and then $233,996. The cost of waiting comes to $22,754 over two years and $132,786 over five.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating 0.2 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
