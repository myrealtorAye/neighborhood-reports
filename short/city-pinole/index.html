Pinole: the October 2026 read

Pinole has an 1824 rancho name and almost no nineteenth century housing.

Pinole takes its name from Rancho El Pinole. The Mexican government granted over 17,000 acres including Pinole Creek to Don Ignacio Martinez, commandant of the San Francisco Presidio, in 1823 by one account and 1824 by another, and the same grant covered Alhambra Valley over in Martinez. A Spanish expedition under Don Pedro Fages had come through this part of Contra Costa well before that. Dr. Samuel J. Tennent married Rafaela, Martinez's daughter, and through her inheritance came to control much of the land here. Bernardo Fernandez, who came from Portugal, made a success of a bayside trading operation in the 1850s and built a mansion that still stands at the end of Tennent Avenue.

The railroad arrived in 1878 and opened the waterfront next door to the California Powder Works, at the place they named Hercules. During World War I that plant made more TNT than any other plant in the country. Pinole itself incorporated in 1903. Interstate 80 came through in 1958 and turned the town into a bedroom community, and the downtown still holds much of its turn of the century building stock.

Pinole has 7,056 housing units and only 4.5 percent of them predate 1950, so despite the old name this is largely a postwar city. 74.0 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 79.4 percent, and the ACS median value is 718,600 dollars. ZHVI is about 716,000 dollars, up 0.1 percent over the year.

That 4.5 percent pre war figure is the one to hold onto, because the Old Town name misleads buyers into expecting an older city than this is. Old Town is where the pre war stock is and the rest of Pinole is postwar.

So pull the year built and let it decide the inspection list. On the postwar tracts, the panel, the supply lines, the sewer lateral, the roof and the foundation bolting. In Old Town, the full pre war list plus the zoning and any historic district rules.

On the shoreline side, get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote. And get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list and it is the single most useful number on a property profile. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. Those two documents cost nothing, they take a day, and between them they settle most of what a buyer actually needs to know before writing an offer.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $716,435 here, transfer tax runs about $788 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $394 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $8,597.
