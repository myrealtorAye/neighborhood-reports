Hayward Highland, Hayward: the October 2026 read

Hayward Highland sits on the slope above the city with the Hayward Fault running along the foot of it.

The fault is the governing physical fact on this side of Hayward. It created the rise these houses are built on, it runs the length of the East Bay, and it is among the most studied faults in the state. Anything you buy on this ground should be looked at with that in mind.

The city below grew on orchards. Hayward incorporated on March 11, 1876 as Haywards, named for a hotel, and spent the next seventy years as one of California's leading fruit districts, with Hunt Brothers running a cannery from 1895 and the city calling itself the Apricot City by 1950. The hillside neighborhoods came after the Second World War, when housing tracts replaced the farms.

Hayward has 52,243 housing units and only 9.1 percent of them predate 1950, so this is overwhelmingly a postwar city. 51.2 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 58.0 percent, the ACS median value is 854,400 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,391. On the Highland the stock runs later and more consistently single family than the citywide figure suggests, on lots cut into grade with views west across the bay.

So the order of work is the hillside order, with the fault first.

Ask the title company whether the parcel sits inside an Alquist-Priolo earthquake fault zone, because that is a recorded designation and it carries disclosure and construction consequences. Ask for any seismic and soils report on the property and whether the foundation has ever been bolted.

Then drainage: the history for the parcel, the condition and ownership of any retaining structures, and where water actually leaves the lot in a wet month. On a slope the ground is the expensive part.

Then a written fire insurance quote on the exact address before your contingencies expire, with the risk rating explained.

Then the house itself, matched to its year built, and a drive down to the freeway at the hour you would really be making it.

Inside it

What it costs to own here

Carry on the typical home here is roughly $10,976 monthly, or $131,716 annually. That covers the loan payment, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance at a stated budget. HOA dues are excluded, because they differ building to building.

As of 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed averages 7.4 percent, up or down from 6.27 percent twelve months before. The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming ceiling and the FHA ceiling.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Transfer tax on the typical Hayward Highland home, $1,165,687, comes to roughly $11,191 at a rate of 9.60 per thousand. It is normally split down the middle, about $5,595 on each party. The contract governs, not the custom.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $17,485.

What waiting costs

Hold off two years and the combined price and payment cost is near $19,417. At five years it reaches $59,238.
