Sundale, Fremont: the October 2026 read

Sundale has very little published history, so what follows is the development record and the buying read. That is more useful than invented colour anyway.

The housing here is described as a mix of mid century single family houses and newer construction, in central Fremont. That mix is the operative fact, because it means two addresses a few streets apart can be thirty or forty years apart in construction type, and the inspection list and the insurance question change with it.

The sourced context comes from the 2024 American Community Survey and this city's own market data. Fremont has 81,579 housing units. 977 of them were built in 1939 or earlier and 749 between 1940 and 1949, putting about 2 percent of the city before 1950. 45,927 units, 56.3 percent, are single family detached. Owner occupancy runs 60.8 percent. The median value of an owner occupied home is 1,403,800 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,933. The median listing currently on the market is about 1,489 square feet.

So the first job on any Sundale address is to establish which product you are looking at. Pull the year built, then compare only against houses of the same era and size, because a mid century ranch and a 1990s two storey in the same neighborhood are different markets wearing the same zip code.

On the mid century stock, ask about the supply lines, the electrical panel capacity, the sewer lateral and the roof. On the newer stock, ask about the roof, the water heater, the furnace and whether the house sits in an association, and if it does, read the reserve study, not the dues number.

Either way, check the lot dimensions and the setbacks if an accessory dwelling unit is in your plan. Single storey houses on regular lots are the best ADU candidates in Fremont and the binding constraint is almost always the setback.

Then drive the commute at your real hour. In central Fremont that answer decides more than the floor plan does.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Fremont I cover, and what each one is:

  • Ardenwood. A Pennsylvania farmer named George Washington Patterson came to California for gold in 1849, found almost none, and in 1857 built a redwood farmhouse here instead.
  • Baylands. This is the edge of Fremont where the city stops and the bay starts, and most of it is federally protected.
  • Brookvale. Brookvale is one of the Fremont neighborhoods where the published history is genuinely thin, so this is the development record and the practical read, not a story somebody made up.
  • Cabrillo. Cabrillo is a 1960s tract neighborhood in north Fremont, and the useful facts about it are in the development record instead of in any story.
  • Centerville. There is a cemetery at Post Street and Bonde Way that is older than the city around it by a century.
  • Cherry Guardino. Cherry-Guardino takes its name from its two main streets, and like several Fremont tract neighborhoods it has almost no published history.
  • City Center. On September 6, 1960 the Fremont City Council picked the Fudenna ranch as the site for its civic center.
  • Glenmoor. Glenmoor has a homeowners association that was incorporated in March 1953, when there were no more than 75 houses standing in the whole subdivision.

What it costs to own here

The all-in monthly number for the typical home here is about $11,892, $142,707 over twelve months. It counts the payment, the tax bill, insurance, utilities and a maintenance allowance, and leaves out HOA dues.

The survey average for a 30-year fixed is 7.4 percent at 2026-10-15, compared with 6.27 percent a year back. The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming ceiling and the FHA ceiling.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Sundale charges 1.10 per thousand, so the typical $1,270,987 home owes roughly $1,398 at transfer. Local practice splits it down the middle, roughly $699 each, though the contract decides.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $19,065.

What waiting costs

On the same trailing rate, two years of waiting adds up to roughly -$48,439 in price and payment combined, and five years to -$142,619.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
