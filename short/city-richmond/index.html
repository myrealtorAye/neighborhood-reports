Richmond: the October 2026 read

Richmond was a refinery before it was a city.

Standard Oil built here in 1901, with one account giving 1902, and by 1904 the plant was reported as the second largest oil refinery in the world. Chevron runs it today. The city incorporated in 1905, four years after the refinery.

The railroad came first of all. The Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe put its western terminus at Point Richmond, in 1899 by one source and on July 4, 1900 by another, and ran ferries across to San Francisco. The settlement there was called East Yards before it was called Point Richmond, and it served as the city's downtown into the early 1900s. Ford opened an assembly plant in 1930.

Then the war. This is what Richmond is known for. Henry Kaiser opened his first shipyard here in December 1940. The city had fewer than 24,000 people that year and nearly 100,000 by 1943. Four yards built 747 ships, more than any other shipyard complex in the country. The Rosie the Riveter World War II Home Front National Historical Park was dedicated on October 25, 2000, the first national park created to commemorate the home front, and the SS Red Oak Victory is moored on the waterfront where the yards stood.

Richmond has 42,509 housing units and 28.5 percent of them predate 1950, the highest share of any city in this county with more than 20,000 housing units. 59.9 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy is 54.7 percent, and the ACS median value is 668,200 dollars.

So this is really two markets. The flats and the older neighborhoods, where the housing is pre war, the inspection list is long, and the zoning question matters because industry runs through them. And the waterfront, built on former shipyard ground, where the stock is new, the associations are the asset you are buying alongside the house, and the environmental record for the parcel is the first document to ask for.

Pull the year built. It tells you which Richmond you are in.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $600,691 here, transfer tax runs about $4,866 at 8.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $2,433 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $1,000,000 the rate steps from 8.10 to 13.50 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,400 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $1,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $7,208.
