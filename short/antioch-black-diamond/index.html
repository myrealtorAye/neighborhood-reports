Black Diamond, Antioch: the October 2026 read

Black Diamond is named for the coal.

Antioch started as a river landing. John Marsh, who owned Rancho Los Meganos, built a landing on the San Joaquin in 1848. The Smith twins founded a town just west of it in 1849 and called it Smith's Landing. The place also went by Marsh's Landing and East Antioch before it took the name of the biblical city. The year of that rename is not settled in the sources, so treat any single date for it with suspicion.

Then coal. It was found in 1859 near Horse Haven and the Black Diamond Mine opened in 1860. The boom founded four towns in these hills, Nortonville, Somersville, Stewartville and Black Diamond, and that last one is now the city of Pittsburg. Nortonville was the biggest and the oldest, with a peak population of about 1,000. Sand mining carried on after the coal and ended by the late 1940s, beaten by Belgian glass sand and the closing of the steel foundry. The ground is now Black Diamond Mines Regional Preserve, 6,000 acres under the East Bay Regional Park District.

Antioch has 38,629 housing units and 7.4 percent of them predate 1950. 78.3 percent are single family detached, second only to Brentwood among this county's larger cities. Owner occupancy runs 63.6 percent and the ACS median value is 629,700 dollars. Citywide ZHVI is about 590,000 dollars, down 0.7 percent over the year.

So the buying questions are mostly postwar questions.

Pull the year built and the permit history for the parcel. On a tract house of this era, ask about the electrical panel and its capacity, the supply lines, the sewer lateral, the roof, and whether the foundation has ever been bolted.

An original kitchen and original aluminium windows are either the discount or the asset. The inspection report tells you which.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate, and ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in.

Then the part specific to this address. This location adds the open space questions. Get a written fire insurance quote on the exact address with the risk rating explained, because housing against 6,000 acres of park is rated differently from housing in the middle of a tract. On the higher ground, ask the inspector about soils and drainage on the parcel and about where water leaves it in a wet month. Ask about the condition and the ownership of any retaining structure, because a shared retaining wall on a slope is the most expensive thing that can go wrong here. Get a written fire insurance quote on the exact address with the risk rating explained. And confirm what sits between the lot line and the preserve: a trail easement, a fuel management zone and a neighbour's fence are three different things.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What it costs to own here

Owning that typical home runs about $5,918 a month, $71,019 a year, once the loan, the property tax, insurance, utilities and a maintenance budget are all counted. HOA dues are not in it, because they vary by building.

The survey average for a 30-year fixed is 7.4 percent at 2026-10-15, compared with 6.27 percent a year back. For 2026, one-unit financing in Contra Costa County tops out at $1,249,125 on both the conforming and the FHA side.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Black Diamond charges 1.10 per thousand, so the typical $590,401 home owes roughly $649 at transfer. Expect it split, near enough $325 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $7,085.

What waiting costs

Hold off two years and the combined price and payment cost is near -$9,592. At five years it reaches -$28,647.

This rests on one observed year carried forward. The two year figure is the defensible one; the five year figure is arithmetic, not a forecast.
