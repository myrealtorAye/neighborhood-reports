Newark: the October 2026 read

Newark is a separate city because its chamber of commerce refused to be somebody else's industrial zone.

Newark is named after a castle in Scotland. J. Barr Robertson gave it the name, taken from Newark Castle at Port Glasgow. A company bought the land from Washington Township in 1878 to lay the town out, and before that, in 1853, Mayhew's Landing here held warehouses for wheat, hay and coal. The city came much later and it came by refusal: when the five townships around it were merging into Fremont, Newark's Chamber of Commerce objected to being zoned mainly for industry inside that new city and pushed to incorporate on its own instead. Newark became a city on September 22, 1955, the first new city in Alameda County in 47 years. Fremont incorporated four months later without it. That is why a city of 16,015 housing units sits inside the shape of a much larger one.

That refusal is not a quirk. It is the organising fact of the place. Newark kept its own zoning authority specifically so it could decide how much of itself would be industrial, and the result is a city where industrial ground and housing sit closer together than in most of this county.

Newark has 16,015 housing units and 4.5 percent of them predate 1950. 67.9 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 69.4 percent, the ACS median value is 1,142,800 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,776. 67.9 percent single family detached and 69.4 percent owner occupancy are both high, so this is a city of houses.

So three things to do here, in order.

Pull the zoning for the parcel and for everything touching it, and ask the city what is permitted next door instead of looking at what operates there now. In Newark that question has more weight than almost anywhere, for the reason above.

Get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire, because this is flat ground at the bay margin and the premium is a monthly number instead of a closing detail.

Then the house, matched to its year built. Outside Old Town almost everything is postwar, so the list is the roof, the supply lines, the electrical panel capacity, the sewer lateral, the furnace and the water heater, plus the lot dimensions and setbacks if an accessory dwelling unit is in your plan.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Newark is $1,185,129 as of 2026-09-30, down 0.08 percent from the prior month and down 2.4 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Listing activity in Newark for 2026-09. Active inventory is 83, up 13.0 percent from the prior month and up 24.1 percent from a year earlier. 52 new listings came on, up 30.0 percent month over month, and 35 went pending. Median days on market is 29, down 5.0 percent month over month and down 8.1 percent year over year.

Median asking price is $1,198,000 at $692 per square foot, on a median listing of 1,632 square feet. Typical rent in the same area is $3,670.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Newark is down 2.4 percent year over year. The median asking price of what is actively listed in Newark is up 0.3 percent year over year, and price per square foot is down 4.0 percent.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What it costs to own here, month to month

Sellers underestimate this number, consistently, and it is the number that should set the pricing decision. Owning the typical home in Newark runs about $11,145 a month, or $133,745 a year. That figure is the loan payment at 3.5 percent down and 7.4 percent, property tax at 1.42 percent, insurance at the California average, utilities for a home of roughly 1,800 square feet, and maintenance budgeted at 1 percent of value a year. HOA dues are not included, because they vary by building and subdivision.

The 30-year fixed average is 7.4 percent as of 2026-10-15, against 6.27 percent in the same week a year earlier, a move of up 1.13 percentage points. The 15-year average is 6.71 percent. These are the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey averages, published weekly, and they are survey averages and not a quote.

On a loan of $1,143,649, the typical home here with the minimum down payment, that year of rate movement is the difference between a payment of $7,057 and $7,918, a change of $862 a month on the same house at the same price. Rate movement has been the larger force on affordability here than price movement.

The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling. At the typical value here of $1,185,129, a 3.5 percent down payment leaves a loan of $1,143,649, comfortably inside the limit. Both FHA and conforming conventional are live options at this price point. That is not true in every neighborhood in this county, and it widens the program list considerably.

The ceiling still sets a practical top end. The most expensive home a minimum-down loan reaches in this county is about $1,294,430. Above that, the options narrow to a larger down payment or a jumbo loan.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $1,185,129 here, transfer tax runs about $1,304 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $652 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $17,777.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at -2.4 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 7.4 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $1,185,129 today becomes $1,129,832 in two years and $1,051,686 in five. The monthly payment moves from $7,918 to $7,549 and then $7,027. The cost of waiting comes to -$64,164 over two years and -$186,938 over five. Rent over those periods adds $88,081 and $220,202 on top, at a typical area rent of $3,670.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating -2.4 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
