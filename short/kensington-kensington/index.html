Kensington, Kensington: the October 2026 read

Kensington is unincorporated, 96.4 percent detached, and 59.7 percent of it was built before 1950.

Kensington is unincorporated Contra Costa County territory on the hill above Berkeley and El Cerrito, and it has never incorporated. El Cerrito below it became a village on August 23, 1917 on a vote of 158 to 131, after people leaving San Francisco following the 1906 earthquake settled the slope on what was Don Victor Castro's Rancho San Pablo. Kensington stayed county ground.

Kensington has 2,207 housing units. 59.7 percent of them predate 1950 and 96.4 percent are single family detached, among the highest shares of any place in this county. Owner occupancy runs 86.8 percent and the ACS median value is 1,539,000 dollars. ZHVI is about 1,490,000 dollars, up 5.3 percent over the year, one of the stronger moves in the county.

So the first questions here are the old housing questions.

Pull the year built. On pre war stock, ask about foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, and the condition of the original windows and siding. Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing, and get the permit history for the parcel.

Ask whether the building is nonconforming, because that limits what you can rebuild after a loss and it affects financing and insurance.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Then the part specific to this address. Confirm first that the address is unincorporated county territory, because it changes who issues your permits, who provides your services, who polices it and how your tax bill is built. Take it from the county, not from a listing. Then ask which school district serves the parcel. On the hill ground, ask the inspector about soils and drainage on the parcel and about where water leaves it in a wet month. Ask about the condition and the ownership of any retaining structure. Get a written fire insurance quote on the exact address with the risk rating explained. In a place where nearly 60 percent of the housing is pre war and nearly all of it sits on a slope, those two lists are the whole purchase.

In an unincorporated place the service districts are the government, and Kensington's are unusually well documented. The Kensington Police Protection and Community Services District was formed in 1946, first as the Kensington Police Protection District, and reorganised under the community services district law in 1953. Accounts of its renaming differ, a community timeline giving 1993 and the county's LAFCO giving 1995. Fire ran separately for most of that time: the Kensington Fire Protection District was formed in 1937 and in 1995 it contracted with the City of El Cerrito for fire and emergency service. Effective October 1, 2025 the fire district consolidated into the community services district.

So ask who answers a call at this address and under which district, and ask what each district levies on the parcel. In a place with no city, those are the questions a city would otherwise answer for you.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Kensington is $1,490,293 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.90 percent from the month before and up 5.3 percent over the year.

Realtor.com does not publish monthly listing metrics for Kensington, so there is no inventory, days on market or asking price figure to report here. The value series above is measured separately and is unaffected.

What it costs to own here

Figure about $13,663 a month to own the typical home in Kensington, $163,958 a year. Loan, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance are in that figure. HOA dues are not, and they vary too much to average.

The survey average for a 30-year fixed is 7.4 percent at 2026-10-15, compared with 6.27 percent a year back. The conforming and FHA ceiling for a one-unit property in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125 in 2026.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 1.10 dollars per thousand, a $1,490,293 sale in Kensington carries about $1,639 in transfer tax. Local practice splits it down the middle, roughly $820 each, though the contract decides.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $18,480.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about $188,280. Stretch it to five and the figure is $615,507.

One year of data, extended. That is worth saying plainly, because compounding it out to five years makes the total look more certain than it is.
