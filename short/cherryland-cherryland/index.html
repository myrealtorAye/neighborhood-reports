Cherryland, Cherryland: the October 2026 read

Cherryland is named for the orchards, and the orchards are the reason this whole side of the bay existed.

This ground was part of Guillermo Castro's Rancho San Lorenzo, granted by Governor Juan Alvarado and covering what is now Hayward, San Lorenzo and Castro Valley. Castro sold pieces of it to cover gambling debts, mortgaged the rest, and in 1864 the sheriff sold the last of it for 400,000 dollars to Faxon Atherton. The whole area was Eden Township, the agricultural district that supplied this side of the bay, and the Eden name still turns up on neighborhoods and schools around here. Cherries were the crop that gave Hayward its Apricot City rival nickname and gave this community its name outright.

Cherryland sits between Hayward and San Lorenzo and it is unincorporated.

One thing worth stating plainly before you search: sources disagree about which ZIP covers Cherryland, with 94541 and 94580 both given. That matters for anything keyed to a postal code, so confirm the ZIP for a specific parcel instead of assuming it.

So jurisdiction is the first thing to establish, and it is not a formality.

This is unincorporated Alameda County. There is no city hall. The county issues the permits, sets the zoning and provides most of the services, so get your answers there, and ask specifically what refuse, sewer, water and road maintenance arrangements come with the address. Buyers routinely assume city services on unincorporated ground and are wrong.

Then the fault. The Hayward Fault runs along the hills immediately east of here. Ask the title company whether the parcel sits inside an Alquist-Priolo earthquake fault zone and ask whether the house has ever been bolted to its foundation.

Then the house, matched to its year built. On pre war stock: foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines and the permit history on every addition. On postwar stock: the roof, the supply lines, the electrical panel capacity, the sewer lateral, the furnace and the water heater.

Confirm the legal unit count with the county, not the listing, because conversions happen here and the permits often do not.

Then check the lot dimensions and setbacks if an accessory dwelling unit is part of your plan.

Then drive the commute at your real hour, and walk to the nearest BART access to see what the distance actually is.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 1.10 dollars per thousand, a $759,862 sale in Cherryland carries about $836 in transfer tax. Local practice splits it down the middle, roughly $418 each, though the contract decides.
