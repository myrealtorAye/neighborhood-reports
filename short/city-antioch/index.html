Antioch: the October 2026 read

Antioch was a river landing, then a coal town, then the fastest growing housing market in the county.

Antioch started as a river landing. John Marsh, who owned Rancho Los Meganos, built a landing on the San Joaquin in 1848. The Smith twins founded a town just west of it in 1849 and called it Smith's Landing. The place also went by Marsh's Landing and East Antioch before it took the name of the biblical city. The year of that rename is not settled in the sources, so treat any single date for it with suspicion.

Then coal. It was found in 1859 near Horse Haven and the Black Diamond Mine opened in 1860. The boom founded four towns in these hills, Nortonville, Somersville, Stewartville and Black Diamond, and that last one is now the city of Pittsburg. Nortonville was the biggest and the oldest, with a peak population of about 1,000. Sand mining carried on after the coal and ended by the late 1940s, beaten by Belgian glass sand and the closing of the steel foundry. The ground is now Black Diamond Mines Regional Preserve, 6,000 acres under the East Bay Regional Park District.

Antioch has 38,629 housing units and 7.4 percent of them predate 1950. 78.3 percent are single family detached, second only to Brentwood among this county's larger cities. Owner occupancy runs 63.6 percent and the ACS median value is 629,700 dollars. Citywide ZHVI is about 590,000 dollars, down 0.7 percent over the year.

That 78.3 percent detached share is second only to Brentwood among this county's larger cities, and the 7.4 percent pre war share is near the bottom. Almost everything in Antioch is a detached house built after 1950, and a large part of it was built after 1990.

So the questions split by era. On the older stock in Rivertown, the pre war inspection list and the downtown zoning question. On the postwar tracts, the year built, the permit history and the panel. On the newer subdivisions along the southern edge, the association reserve study and the assessment districts, because Mello Roos out here runs into thousands a year and does not come off with the mortgage.

Ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in and when each is scheduled to expire, in writing. Then drive Lone Tree Way and the Highway 4 corridor at your real commute hour.

What it costs to own here

The all-in monthly number for the typical home here is about $5,918, $71,019 over twelve months. It counts the payment, the tax bill, insurance, utilities and a maintenance allowance, and leaves out HOA dues.

On 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed average was 7.4 percent, where the year-ago figure was 6.27 percent. The conforming and FHA ceiling for a one-unit property in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125 in 2026.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 1.10 dollars per thousand, a $590,401 sale in Antioch carries about $649 in transfer tax. Convention puts about $325 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $7,085.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about -$9,592. Stretch it to five and the figure is -$28,647.

This rests on one observed year carried forward. The two year figure is the defensible one; the five year figure is arithmetic, not a forecast.
