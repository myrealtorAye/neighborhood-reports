Havenscourt, Oakland: the October 2026 read

Havenscourt has little published history of its own, so this is the development record and the practical read, not invented colour.

What places it is the 1909 annexation, the largest in Oakland's history. It brought Elmhurst, Fruitvale, Melrose and Fitchburg into the city in a single move. Oakland went from 22.9 square miles to roughly 60 and its population doubled to 150,174 by 1910, most of that growth from people who left San Francisco after the 1906 earthquake. Every East Oakland district, this one included, is a product of that decade and the ones that followed.

The other shaping fact nearby is industrial. Chevrolet ran an assembly plant on Foothill Boulevard from the mid 1910s until 1963, General Motors chose to build its replacement seventeen miles south in Fremont, and the Oakland site became Eastmont Mall in the later 1960s before being converted into Eastmont Town Center. Deep East Oakland's twentieth century tracks that plant.

The sourced figures are the city's. Oakland has 191,713 housing units. 71,234 were built in 1939 or earlier and 18,124 between 1940 and 1949, so 46.6 percent of the city predates 1950. 41.5 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy is 42.3 percent, the ACS median value for an owner occupied home is 929,900 dollars and the median gross rent is 1,979.

So the first question on any address is legal, not cosmetic.

Confirm the legal unit count with the city instead of taking the listing's word. Get the permit history. Ask whether the building is nonconforming, since that limits rebuilding after a loss and affects financing and insurance. If it holds more than one unit, read Oakland's rent adjustment program and the just cause for eviction rules before you model income.

Then match the inspection to the era. Pre war: foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, the sewer lateral Oakland certifies at sale. Postwar: roof, supply lines, panel capacity, furnace.

On any former industrial parcel, ask for the site history in writing before your contingencies expire.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Oakland I cover, and what each one is:

  • Acorn. Fifty blocks of this neighborhood were demolished.
  • Adams Point. Adams Point is named for Edson Adams, one of the men who got hold of Oakland's land early, and in 1906 it filled up with San Franciscans who had nowhere else to go.
  • Allendale. The Allendale tract map was recorded in 1892, and there was a cinema operating here by 1913.
  • Brookfield Village. Twelve hundred houses went up here during the Second World War and they sold for four thousand dollars each.
  • Bushrod. Bushrod was annexed by Oakland in 1897, in the same batch as Temescal, Golden Gate and Piedmont Avenue, and that date is the most solid thing in its published record.
  • Caballo Hills. Caballo Hills is a hillside pocket at the southeastern edge of Oakland with very little published history, so this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Castlemont. Castlemont takes its name from the high school at its centre, and its published history is thin enough that this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Chabot Park. Anthony Chabot dammed a creek in 1868 and built Oakland its first water supply at municipal scale, and his name is on the parkland at the edge of this neighborhood.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $478,890 here, transfer tax runs about $7,710 at 16.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $3,855 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $2,000,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 18.60 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $2,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $7,183.
