Pacheco: the October 2026 read

Pacheco has the lowest ACS median value of any place in Contra Costa County, and the housing type behind that number is the same fact.

Pacheco carries the name of Salvio Pacheco, who was granted Rancho Monte del Diablo in 1834 and whose family laid out the town of Todos Santos in 1868, the place the Contra Costa Gazette renamed Concord on April 17, 1869. Pacheco itself never incorporated and it is unincorporated Contra Costa County territory today.

Pacheco has 1,625 housing units. 44.6 percent are single family detached, a low share for a Contra Costa place, and the ACS median value is 343,300 dollars, the lowest in the county. 2.8 percent of the stock predates 1950 and owner occupancy runs 79.2 percent. Nearby and also unincorporated: Vine Hill with 1,296 units, Clyde with 285, and Contra Costa Centre with 3,628 units at 6.5 percent detached and 15.4 percent owner occupied.

A scope note on price. Neither Zillow nor Realtor.com publishes a separate value series for this community, so there is no citywide figure to quote for it. Work from closed comparable sales on the specific streets and treat any citywide number a listing shows you as belonging to somewhere else.

So the first thing to establish is what the housing actually is. A 44.6 percent detached share with a 343,300 dollar median means a large part of this market is not conventional detached housing. If the address is a manufactured or mobile home, ask whether you are buying the land or renting a space, get the park's rules and its rent history, and ask your lender early whether it will finance it at all. Those answers change the whole transaction.

Then confirm the parcel is unincorporated county territory and ask which school district serves it.

One thing worth spelling out, because it catches buyers. In an unincorporated place there is no city hall standing between you and the county. Your building permits and inspections come from the county, code enforcement is the county's, the Sheriff answers calls unless a special district provides policing, and fire, water and sewer frequently come from separate districts that each levy on the parcel. So get the full list of districts for the address off the county's tax roll, not the listing, and read what each one charges. Then ask the county's Local Agency Formation Commission whether the parcel sits in any city's sphere of influence, because that is where future annexation would start and it is a public record.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list and it is the single most useful number on a property profile. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. Those two documents cost nothing, they take a day, and between them they settle most of what a buyer actually needs to know before writing an offer.

What it costs to own here

Carry on the typical home here is roughly $7,287 monthly, or $87,448 annually. That covers the loan payment, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance at a stated budget. HOA dues are excluded, because they differ building to building.

As of 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed averages 7.4 percent, up or down from 6.27 percent twelve months before. In Contra Costa County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $749,473 here, transfer tax runs about $824 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Expect it split, near enough $412 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $8,994.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about -$22,177. Stretch it to five and the figure is -$65,660.

Treat it as a scenario, not a prediction. The appreciation input is one year of observed change carried forward, and five years of compounding on one year of data flatters the result.
