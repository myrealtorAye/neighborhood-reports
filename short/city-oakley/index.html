Oakley: the October 2026 read

Oakley planned its downtown three years before it became a city.

Oakley is the newest city in Contra Costa County. It incorporated on July 1, 1999 and has been reported as the eleventh newest city in the state. The ground was part of Rancho Los Meganos, granted in 1835, and the townsite was settled around 1897 with a post office in 1898. The city planned its downtown before it existed as a city: the work began in the early 1990s while this was still unincorporated, and the county adopted the Old Town Oakley Specific Plan in 1996. It is a general law city with a council manager government and a five member council elected by district, on the San Joaquin River Delta.

Oakley has 13,944 housing units and 3.0 percent of them predate 1950. 90.6 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 78.8 percent, and the ACS median value is 660,200 dollars. ZHVI is about 654,000 dollars, down 0.5 percent over the year.

90.6 percent detached across 13,944 units, with 3.0 percent of the stock pre war. This is a Delta farm town that became a suburb fast, and the city is younger than most of its housing.

So the assessment question comes first. Ask which community facilities districts the parcel sits in, what each levies and the year each expires, in writing from the county. Then the association budget and the reserve study where there is an association, not the dues figure.

Ask the inspector about grading and drainage, since a young subdivision on engineered fill shows settlement in its first fifteen years. In Old Town, read the Old Town Oakley Specific Plan the county adopted in 1996, because it is public and it says what is intended on those streets.

And drive Laurel Road and the Highway 4 corridor at your real commute hour.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list and it is the single most useful number on a property profile. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. Those two documents cost nothing, they take a day, and between them they settle most of what a buyer actually needs to know before writing an offer.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Figure $719 in transfer tax on a $653,563 sale here, at 1.10 dollars per thousand. It is normally split down the middle, about $359 on each party. The contract governs, not the custom.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $7,843.

What waiting costs
