Emeryville: the October 2026 read

Emeryville incorporated to protect a racetrack.

Emeryville incorporated in December 1896, on the second by the city's own account and the eighth by another, and it did it to protect a racetrack. Temescal residents had proposed a town that would have covered this ground too. That alarmed the business owners here, and a large part of the push to form a separate town was keeping the California Jockey Club track and the gambling revenue that came with it. The place is named for Joseph Emery, who arrived in the Gold Rush and bought land here. The city also built a large park around its shellmound in the late 1800s. The boundaries drawn by the founders in 1896 are substantially the ones in force today.

What the city became after the track is an industrial town, and then, more recently, something close to a laboratory for dense housing. The figures are unlike anywhere else in this series.

Emeryville has 8,497 housing units and 12.1 percent of them predate 1950. Only 5.8 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 24.2 percent, both the lowest in this entire series. The ACS median value is 604,800 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,936. Hold those against Piedmont, eight minutes away, at 94.7 percent detached and 90.9 percent owner occupancy. Two cities in the same county could not be further apart in what they are built of.

So shopping Emeryville means shopping buildings, not houses.

Get the association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure. Ask what the association insures and what you insure. Ask whether the building has had a seismic retrofit and whether it is soft story, because a great deal of stock here sits over parking and lenders ask. Ask whether there has been construction defect litigation, and read the association minutes.

Pull the zoning for the parcel and everything touching it. This is a small city with industrial ground, active redevelopment and no spare land, so what is permitted next door frequently differs from what stands there, and a view is not a protected asset.

Then pull the actual tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

And on the bay side, get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote and ask the inspector about settlement, because much of that ground is made land.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Emeryville is $429,369 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.03 percent from the prior month and down 6.3 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Listing activity in Emeryville for 2026-09. Active inventory is 70, up 26.4 percent from the prior month and down 24.9 percent from a year earlier. 38 new listings came on, up 58.3 percent month over month, and 18 went pending. Median days on market is 36, down 25.3 percent month over month and down 29.6 percent year over year.

Median asking price is $585,194 at $530 per square foot, on a median listing of 1,020 square feet. Typical rent in the same area is $2,945.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Emeryville is down 6.3 percent year over year. The median asking price of what is actively listed in Emeryville is down 2.9 percent year over year, and price per square foot is down 3.1 percent.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What it costs to own here, month to month

Sellers underestimate this number, consistently, and it is the number that should set the pricing decision. Owning the typical home in Emeryville runs about $139,817 a month, or $1,677,799 a year. That figure is the loan payment at 3.5 percent down and 400 percent, property tax at 1.42 percent, insurance at the California average, utilities for a home of roughly 1,800 square feet, and maintenance budgeted at 1 percent of value a year. HOA dues are not included, because they vary by building and subdivision.

The 15-year average is 400 percent. These are the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey averages, published weekly, and they are survey averages and not a quote.

On a loan of $414,341, the typical home here with the minimum down payment, that year of rate movement is the difference between a payment of $138,114 and $138,114, a change of $0 a month on the same house at the same price. Rate movement has been the larger force on affordability here than price movement.

The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling. At the typical value here of $429,369, a 3.5 percent down payment leaves a loan of $414,341, comfortably inside the limit. Both FHA and conforming conventional are live options at this price point. That is not true in every neighborhood in this county, and it widens the program list considerably.

The ceiling still sets a practical top end. The most expensive home a minimum-down loan reaches in this county is about $1,294,430. Above that, the options narrow to a larger down payment or a jumbo loan.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $429,369 here, transfer tax runs about $5,625 at 13.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $2,812 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $1,000,000 the rate steps from 13.10 to 16.10 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $3,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $1,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $6,441.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at -6.3 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 400 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $429,369 today becomes $377,252 in two years and $310,695 in five. The monthly payment moves from $138,114 to $121,349 and then $99,940. The cost of waiting comes to -$454,457 over two years and -$2,409,086 over five. Rent over those periods adds $70,673 and $176,683 on top, at a typical area rent of $2,945.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating -6.3 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
