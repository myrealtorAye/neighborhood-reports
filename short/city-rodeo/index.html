Rodeo: the October 2026 read

Rodeo is unincorporated, it sits in the refinery belt, and a fifth of its housing is pre war.

Rodeo is unincorporated Contra Costa County territory on the bay shore between Hercules and Crockett, in the industrial corridor along the Carquinez Strait. Hercules next door took its name from the dynamite made there from 1881, and the powder company's managers incorporated that town on December 15, 1900 because they could not get the county supervisors to pass laws supporting the plant. Crockett up the strait grew around the C and H sugar refinery. That plant processed its last shipment of Hawaiian sugar in 2017. Rodeo never incorporated.

Rodeo has 3,190 housing units and 19.9 percent of them predate 1950. 79.4 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 59.0 percent, and the ACS median value is 663,200 dollars. ZHVI is about 646,000 dollars, down 0.3 percent over the year.

So three things come first here. Confirm the parcel is unincorporated county territory, because it changes your permits, your services and your tax bill. Pull the zoning for the parcel and everything touching it, and ask for the site history in writing. And ask your insurance broker how the refinery proximity is rated for the exact address, and the county what notification and emergency systems cover it.

Then pull the year built, because a fifth of this stock carries the pre war inspection list.

One thing worth spelling out, because it catches buyers. In an unincorporated place there is no city hall standing between you and the county. Your building permits and inspections come from the county, code enforcement is the county's, the Sheriff answers calls unless a special district provides policing, and fire, water and sewer frequently come from separate districts that each levy on the parcel. So get the full list of districts for the address off the county's tax roll, not the listing, and read what each one charges. Then ask the county's Local Agency Formation Commission whether the parcel sits in any city's sphere of influence, because that is where future annexation would start and it is a public record.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list and it is the single most useful number on a property profile. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. Those two documents cost nothing, they take a day, and between them they settle most of what a buyer actually needs to know before writing an offer.
