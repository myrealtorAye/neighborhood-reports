Baylands, Fremont: the October 2026 read

This is the edge of Fremont where the city stops and the bay starts, and most of it is federally protected.

The Don Edwards San Francisco Bay National Wildlife Refuge was established in 1972 after years of local campaigning against the filling of the bayfront. Arthur Ogilvie helped organize the committee that pushed for it. Congress named the refuge for Congressman Don Edwards in 1995, and the Fremont visitor center opened in 1979. Mowry Slough, in Fremont, is inside the refuge. One source gives the founding year as 1974, but the Fish and Wildlife Service and the Federal Register both say 1972.

That history is the whole character of the Baylands. The reason there is open tidal marsh at the end of Fremont instead of more subdivision is that people fought to keep it, and then the federal government took it on.

So there is very little housing out here, and that is the first thing to understand if you are searching the Baylands. What people usually mean is the newer development inland of the marsh instead of anything on it. Fremont has 81,579 housing units in total and about 2 percent predate 1950, so what exists on this side is recent, planned, and often inside an association.

Three things to establish on a specific address. Get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire, because this is low flat ground at the bay margin and the premium is a real monthly number, not a closing detail. Ask the inspector about settlement and look hard at foundations and flatwork, since ground near filled marsh behaves differently from ground on the valley floor. And on anything in a homeowners association, read the reserve study, not just the dues.

Then go to the refuge visitor center and walk out on the levee trail. It is the one part of Fremont where you can stand in the middle of a landscape that looks the way the whole bay margin used to look, and it is a short drive from houses that cost well over a million dollars.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Fremont I cover, and what each one is:

  • Ardenwood. A Pennsylvania farmer named George Washington Patterson came to California for gold in 1849, found almost none, and in 1857 built a redwood farmhouse here instead.
  • Brookvale. Brookvale is one of the Fremont neighborhoods where the published history is genuinely thin, so this is the development record and the practical read, not a story somebody made up.
  • Cabrillo. Cabrillo is a 1960s tract neighborhood in north Fremont, and the useful facts about it are in the development record instead of in any story.
  • Centerville. There is a cemetery at Post Street and Bonde Way that is older than the city around it by a century.
  • Cherry Guardino. Cherry-Guardino takes its name from its two main streets, and like several Fremont tract neighborhoods it has almost no published history.
  • City Center. On September 6, 1960 the Fremont City Council picked the Fudenna ranch as the site for its civic center.
  • Glenmoor. Glenmoor has a homeowners association that was incorporated in March 1953, when there were no more than 75 houses standing in the whole subdivision.
  • Grimmer. Grimmer is named for a family nobody has documented properly, and the honest thing is to say so.

What it costs to own here

Owning that typical home runs about $13,361 a month, $160,327 a year, once the loan, the property tax, insurance, utilities and a maintenance budget are all counted. HOA dues are not in it, because they vary by building.

Rates sit at 7.4 percent on the 30-year fixed as of 2026-10-15. A year earlier the same survey read 6.27 percent. In Alameda County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 1.10 dollars per thousand, a $1,439,805 sale in Baylands carries about $1,584 in transfer tax. Expect it split, near enough $792 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $21,597.

What waiting costs

Waiting two years at this place's own trailing rate costs about -$49,092, counting the price increase and the payment increase together. Over five years it is -$144,921.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
