The Vineyards, Antioch: the October 2026 read

The Vineyards is one of Antioch's newer subdivisions, on the southern edge of the city.

Antioch started as a river landing. John Marsh, who owned Rancho Los Meganos, built a landing on the San Joaquin in 1848. The Smith twins founded a town just west of it in 1849 and called it Smith's Landing. The place also went by Marsh's Landing and East Antioch before it took the name of the biblical city. The year of that rename is not settled in the sources, so treat any single date for it with suspicion.

Then coal. It was found in 1859 near Horse Haven and the Black Diamond Mine opened in 1860. The boom founded four towns in these hills, Nortonville, Somersville, Stewartville and Black Diamond, and that last one is now the city of Pittsburg. Nortonville was the biggest and the oldest, with a peak population of about 1,000. Sand mining carried on after the coal and ended by the late 1940s, beaten by Belgian glass sand and the closing of the steel foundry. The ground is now Black Diamond Mines Regional Preserve, 6,000 acres under the East Bay Regional Park District.

Antioch has 38,629 housing units and 7.4 percent of them predate 1950. 78.3 percent are single family detached, second only to Brentwood among this county's larger cities. Owner occupancy runs 63.6 percent and the ACS median value is 629,700 dollars. Citywide ZHVI is about 590,000 dollars, down 0.7 percent over the year.

So the buying questions here are newer-construction questions.

Get the homeowners association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, and ask specifically what the association maintains and what it does not. A thin reserve on a young association becomes a special assessment.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate, and ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in. Newer East County subdivisions frequently carry Mello Roos, and that line can run thousands a year and does not come off with the mortgage.

Then ask when the district is scheduled to expire, in writing.

Then the part specific to this address. Pull the tract map for the subdivision from the county recorder. It carries the recording date, the original lot dimensions and the street layout as approved, and that record beats anything a listing will tell you about when and how this neighborhood was built.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What it costs to own here

Carry on the typical home here is roughly $5,918 monthly, or $71,019 annually. That covers the loan payment, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance at a stated budget. HOA dues are excluded, because they differ building to building.

Rates sit at 7.4 percent on the 30-year fixed as of 2026-10-15. A year earlier the same survey read 6.27 percent. In Contra Costa County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

The Vineyards charges 1.10 per thousand, so the typical $590,401 home owes roughly $649 at transfer. Convention puts about $325 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $7,085.

What waiting costs

On the same trailing rate, two years of waiting adds up to roughly -$9,592 in price and payment combined, and five years to -$28,647.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
