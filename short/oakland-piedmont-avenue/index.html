Piedmont Avenue, Oakland: the October 2026 read

The man who designed Central Park designed the cemetery at the top of this street.

Mountain View Cemetery was founded in 1863 and the commission went to Frederick Law Olmsted. It covers somewhere between 220 and 226 acres depending on the source, and it was dedicated in 1865, with the first burial that same year. Accounts differ on the exact day. The ridge section, where the crypts get grand and the view runs west across the bay to the San Francisco skyline, has been called Millionaires' Row for a century. There is also an unmarked piece of ground called Stranger's Plot holding about 200 unknown burials.

The names in it are the names in this whole project. Julia Morgan is buried there, the first woman licensed to practise architecture in California, who designed Hearst Castle and also the 1917 church at 5951 College Avenue in Rockridge. So is Charles Crocker of the Big Four, the syndicate that bought Alfred Cohen's railroad in 1868 and let him build the Fernside mansion in Alameda. So is Samuel Merritt, the Oakland mayor who proposed protecting the lake that carries his name. And Domingo Ghirardelli.

Piedmont Avenue itself is the commercial street running south from the cemetery gates, annexed by Oakland in 1897 along with Temescal, Golden Gate and Bushrod. It is a walkable strip of small storefronts set at the sidewalk.

The housing is pre war and mixed: houses on the side streets, apartment buildings and small income properties closer to the avenue. Oakland has 191,713 housing units and 46.6 percent of them predate 1950.

So pull the year built and the legal unit count before anything else. Confirm the unit count with the city, not the listing, and if there is more than one, read Oakland's rent adjustment program and the just cause rules before modelling income.

Then the pre war list: foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, and the sewer lateral Oakland certifies at sale.

Then walk up to the cemetery gates and in. It is the best public open space in North Oakland and almost nobody treats it that way.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Oakland I cover, and what each one is:

  • Acorn. Fifty blocks of this neighborhood were demolished.
  • Adams Point. Adams Point is named for Edson Adams, one of the men who got hold of Oakland's land early, and in 1906 it filled up with San Franciscans who had nowhere else to go.
  • Allendale. The Allendale tract map was recorded in 1892, and there was a cinema operating here by 1913.
  • Brookfield Village. Twelve hundred houses went up here during the Second World War and they sold for four thousand dollars each.
  • Bushrod. Bushrod was annexed by Oakland in 1897, in the same batch as Temescal, Golden Gate and Piedmont Avenue, and that date is the most solid thing in its published record.
  • Caballo Hills. Caballo Hills is a hillside pocket at the southeastern edge of Oakland with very little published history, so this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Castlemont. Castlemont takes its name from the high school at its centre, and its published history is thin enough that this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Chabot Park. Anthony Chabot dammed a creek in 1868 and built Oakland its first water supply at municipal scale, and his name is on the parkland at the edge of this neighborhood.

What it costs to own here

Owning that typical home runs about $7,969 a month, $95,625 a year, once the loan, the property tax, insurance, utilities and a maintenance budget are all counted. HOA dues are not in it, because they vary by building.

Rates sit at 7.4 percent on the 30-year fixed as of 2026-10-15. A year earlier the same survey read 6.27 percent. The conforming and FHA ceiling for a one-unit property in Alameda County is $1,249,125 in 2026.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $797,756 here, transfer tax runs about $12,844 at 16.10 dollars per thousand. It is normally split down the middle, about $6,422 on each party. The contract governs, not the custom.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $2,000,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 18.60 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $2,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $11,966.

What waiting costs

Waiting two years at this place's own trailing rate costs about $99,650, counting the price increase and the payment increase together. Over five years it is $325,479.

This rests on one observed year carried forward. The two year figure is the defensible one; the five year figure is arithmetic, not a forecast.
