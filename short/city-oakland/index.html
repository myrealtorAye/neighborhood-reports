Oakland: the October 2026 read

Oakland's chain of title starts with one land grant. In 1820 the last Spanish governor of California gave Sergeant Luis Maria Peralta about 44,800 acres covering what is now Oakland, Berkeley, Alameda, Emeryville, Piedmont and San Leandro. The United States took California, the Land Act of 1851 made the family prove the grant in court, and by the time the Supreme Court confirmed it in 1856 squatters held most of it. Six acres survived as a park.

Then the city ate its neighbours. In 1897 it annexed Temescal, Golden Gate, Bushrod and the Piedmont Avenue area. In 1909 it took Fruitvale, Elmhurst, Melrose and Fitchburg in the largest annexation it ever made, carrying the city from 22.9 square miles to roughly 60 while the population doubled to 150,174 by 1910, most of that from people who left San Francisco after the 1906 earthquake.

A few things here are genuinely first in the country. The state legislature protected Lake Merritt in 1870, making it the first wildlife refuge in North America. Frederick Law Olmsted, who designed Central Park, laid out Mountain View Cemetery in 1863. The 16th Street Station opened in 1912 as the western terminus of the transcontinental railroad and became the western headquarters of the Brotherhood of Sleeping Car Porters.

The hills tell a different story. The San Antonio redwood forest was big enough that ships entering San Francisco Bay steered by two of its trees to clear Blossom Rock, and it was logged level inside about fifteen years. On October 20, 1991 the Tunnel Fire killed 25 people and destroyed 2,843 single family homes across those same hills.

What all of that produces is a city of 191,713 housing units where 46.6 percent predate 1950. Twenty miles south in Fremont that figure is 2 percent. Only 41.5 percent of Oakland units are single family detached and owner occupancy is 42.3 percent, so this is substantially a flats and multi unit market. Zillow tracks 121 separate neighborhoods inside the city.

So Oakland is not one market, it is three. Pre war flats, where the questions are the legal unit count, the rent rules and a long inspection list. The hills, where fire insurance decides affordability before the house does. And downtown, where it is reserve studies and seismic retrofit.

Find out which one you are shopping in before you compare a single price, and pull the year built before anything else.

Inside it

This report covers 22 areas of Oakland. What each one actually is:

  • Acorn. Fifty blocks of this neighborhood were demolished.
  • Adams Point. Adams Point is named for Edson Adams, one of the men who got hold of Oakland's land early, and in 1906 it filled up with San Franciscans who had nowhere else to go.
  • Allendale. The Allendale tract map was recorded in 1892, and there was a cinema operating here by 1913.
  • Brookfield Village. Twelve hundred houses went up here during the Second World War and they sold for four thousand dollars each.
  • Bushrod. Bushrod was annexed by Oakland in 1897, in the same batch as Temescal, Golden Gate and Piedmont Avenue, and that date is the most solid thing in its published record.
  • Caballo Hills. Caballo Hills is a hillside pocket at the southeastern edge of Oakland with very little published history, so this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Castlemont. Castlemont takes its name from the high school at its centre, and its published history is thin enough that this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Chabot Park. Anthony Chabot dammed a creek in 1868 and built Oakland its first water supply at municipal scale, and his name is on the parkland at the edge of this neighborhood.
  • Chinatown. Oakland Chinatown is older than almost everything around it.
  • Clawson. Clawson is the official name, and a good number of people call this ground Dogtown.
  • Cleveland Heights. Cleveland Heights is the slope on the east side of Lake Merritt, and the hook here is the ground itself: it climbs, so the houses look back across the water at downtown.
  • Clinton San Antonio. Clinton and San Antonio sit east of Lake Merritt on ground that was two separate towns before Oakland absorbed them, and the street grid still shows the seam.
  • Columbia Gardens. Columbia Gardens is one of three neighborhoods in the deepest part of East Oakland, with Brookfield Village and Sobrante Park, and one local account calls the three of them independent but intertwined.
  • Crestmont. Crestmont is a hillside pocket on the Oakland ridge with little published history of its own, so this is the development record and the practical read.
  • Dimond. Hugh Dimond came over for the Gold Rush, made money in the Mariposa and Tuolumne mines, and in 1867 bought 267 acres of Oakland foothill along Sausal Creek.
  • Dogtown. Dogtown is a nickname, and the official name of this ground is Clawson.
  • Downtown. Downtown Oakland's commercial centre was fixed at Ninth Street and Broadway by 1877, and Broadway has been the spine ever since.
  • Eastlake. Eastlake is the commercial and residential district immediately east of Lake Merritt, and like several of the older Oakland flats it has very little published history, so this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Eastmont. General Motors built cars here for about fifty years, then left, and the plant became a shopping mall, and the mall became a social services centre.
  • Elmhurst. Elmhurst was called Jones until 1869, and it was a train station before it was a town.
  • Fitchburg. Fitchburg was a town, Oakland annexed it in 1909, and the name has largely been replaced by Lockwood.
  • Glenview. The street you shop on in Glenview was a road for moving dead redwoods.

What it costs to own here

The all-in monthly number for the typical home here is about $7,190, $86,280 over twelve months. It counts the payment, the tax bill, insurance, utilities and a maintenance allowance, and leaves out HOA dues.

The survey average for a 30-year fixed is 7.4 percent at 2026-10-15, compared with 6.27 percent a year back. The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming ceiling and the FHA ceiling.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $710,647 here, transfer tax runs about $11,441 at 16.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $5,721 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $2,000,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 18.60 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $2,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $10,660.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about $9,161 here. The five year figure is $27,880.

This rests on one observed year carried forward. The two year figure is the defensible one; the five year figure is arithmetic, not a forecast.
