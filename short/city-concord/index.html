Concord: the October 2026 read

Concord was a Mexican land grant, then a town square, then a BART suburb, and it is about to become something else again.

Rancho Monte del Diablo went to Salvio Pacheco in 1834. In 1868 the Pacheco family and Francisco Galindo laid out a town on it called Todos Santos, and the Contra Costa Gazette announced the new name Concord on April 17, 1869. The plaza at the centre is 2.5 acres bounded by Salvio Street, Willow Pass Road, Grant Street and Mount Diablo Street. Sources differ on whether it was dedicated in 1868 or 1869. In 2018 the American Planning Association named it one of three Great Places in California. A number of the historic houses around it were moved there from elsewhere in Concord to save them from demolition.

Then the city filled in fast. Park and Shop and Sun Valley Mall opened as regional shopping destinations through the 1950s and 1960s, BART arrived in 1973, and Joseph Eichler built three tracts here, Rancho Del Diablo, Rancho Los Santos and Parkwood Estates, roughly 175 homes in 1963 and 1964.

Concord also holds a national memorial. On July 17, 1944 at 10:08 at night, an explosion at Port Chicago killed 320 men, the worst stateside disaster of World War II. The trial that followed was the largest naval mutiny case in United States history and it led to the desegregation of the Navy. The Port Chicago Naval Magazine National Memorial was established in 1992 and became a unit of the national park system in 2009. It sits on Suisun Bay on an active military base and can only be visited by reservation.

Concord has 47,703 housing units and 6.7 percent of them predate 1950. 58.7 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 61.6 percent, and the ACS median value is 797,600 dollars. The city's own figure is that 73 percent of its existing homes went up between 1950 and 1979. Citywide ZHVI is about 721,000 dollars, up 0.2 percent over the year.

One thing hangs over every address in this city. The former Concord Naval Weapons Station covers roughly 2,225 acres and the reuse plan calls for more than 12,000 homes, 6 million square feet of commercial space and more than 800 acres of parks, with Brookfield Properties as master developer. The city announced on September 24, 2026 that the Navy had approved the term sheet, and the governor signed SB 328, exempting the project from the Surplus Land Act and streamlining its environmental review. The site is a federal Superfund site with lead and arsenic in it and the Navy has to finish the cleanup before anything gets built. Nobody can tell you the delivery date.

So a buyer in Concord is buying a postwar tract house in a city with a 2,225 acre question mark in it. Pull the year built and the permit history. Get the tax bill for the address. And ask for the base timeline as a range.

Inside it

This report covers 13 areas of Concord. What each one actually is:

  • Cambridge. Cambridge is a central Concord neighborhood, and Cambridge Park is the open space the city names inside it.
  • Clayton Valley. Clayton Valley is the eastern side of Concord, running toward the city of Clayton and the foot of Mount Diablo.
  • Colony Park. Colony Park is a postwar residential neighborhood in Concord.
  • Dana Estates. Dana Estates is one of Concord's postwar residential tracts.
  • Holbrook Heights. Holbrook Heights is on the rising ground on Concord's northern side.
  • Limeridge. Lime Ridge is named for a quarry operation.
  • Monument Corridor. This neighborhood is named for a street.
  • Northgate. Northgate sits on Concord's southern edge, on the Walnut Creek side of the city, below the Mount Diablo foothills.
  • Sun Terrace. Sun Terrace is a postwar tract neighborhood in Concord.
  • Todos Santos. Concord's town square is older than the city's name.
  • Turtle Creek. Turtle Creek is a Concord neighborhood named for the watercourse that runs through this side of the city.
  • West Concord. West Concord is the side of the city that faces Pleasant Hill and the Monument corridor.
  • Ygnacio Valley. Ygnacio Valley Road runs the length of this part of Concord and the neighborhood takes its name from the valley.

What it costs to own here

Carry on the typical home here is roughly $7,041 monthly, or $84,498 annually. That covers the loan payment, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance at a stated budget. HOA dues are excluded, because they differ building to building.

The 30-year fixed average is 7.4 percent as of 2026-10-15, against 6.27 percent in the same week last year. In Contra Costa County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $720,907 here, transfer tax runs about $793 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Expect it split, near enough $396 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $8,651.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about $3,028. Stretch it to five and the figure is $9,163.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
