Reliez Valley, Lafayette: the October 2026 read

Reliez Valley runs between Lafayette and Pleasant Hill, and part of it is unincorporated.

Lafayette started as a Mexican land grant. Rancho Acalanes was granted in 1834 to Candelario Valencia, and Elam Brown was its third owner. Brown's party reached the area in 1846 and settled in 1848. Accounts of the purchase differ, one putting it at 1847 for 900 dollars across 3,328.9 acres and another at 1848. Brown built a horse powered grist mill in 1849 and the commerce that gathered around it set the line of what is now Mt. Diablo Boulevard. Brown served as alcalde, sat in the state constitutional convention of 1849, and served in California's first two legislatures. The city itself did not incorporate until 1968.

Lafayette has 9,998 housing units and 16.5 percent of them predate 1950, a high pre war share for this part of the county. 74.3 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 77.1 percent. ZHVI is about 1,907,000 dollars, up 3.5 percent over the year. One number to read carefully. The ACS reports a median owner occupied value of 2,000,001 dollars here, and that is the survey's ceiling, not a measurement. The actual median sits above the highest figure the survey will print.

So the buying questions out here are hill questions and they come before the house.

Ask the inspector about soils and drainage on the parcel and about where water leaves it in a wet month. Ask about the condition and the ownership of every retaining structure, because a shared retaining wall on a slope is the most expensive thing that can go wrong.

Get a written fire insurance quote on the exact address with the risk rating explained, and ask what the insurer wants cleared to write it. In this part of the county that quote decides deals.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Then the part specific to this address. Confirm first that the address is unincorporated county territory, because it changes who issues your permits, who provides your services, who polices it and how your tax bill is built. Take it from the county, not from a listing. Then ask which school district serves the parcel, since district lines out here do not follow the boundaries a buyer assumes. Ask whether the parcel is on sewer or on septic, and if septic, get the most recent inspection and the pumping record. Ask whether water comes from a district or a well, and if a well, get the yield and the water quality tests in writing.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What it costs to own here

Put the minimum down on the typical Reliez Valley home and the monthly carry lands near $17,252. That is $207,026 across a year, counting loan, tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance. HOA dues are not in it, since no two are alike.

On 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed average was 7.4 percent, where the year-ago figure was 6.27 percent. For 2026, one-unit financing in Contra Costa County tops out at $1,249,125 on both the conforming and the FHA side.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 1.10 dollars per thousand, a $1,907,310 sale in Reliez Valley carries about $2,098 in transfer tax. Local practice splits it down the middle, roughly $1,049 each, though the contract decides.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $23,651.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about $155,365 here. The five year figure is $493,904.

Treat it as a scenario, not a prediction. The appreciation input is one year of observed change carried forward, and five years of compounding on one year of data flatters the result.
