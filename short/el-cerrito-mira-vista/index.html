Mira Vista, El Cerrito: the October 2026 read

Mira Vista is a hill neighborhood in El Cerrito, and the name needs care. Richmond Heights also goes by Mira Vista and Antioch has a Mira Vista Hills, so three places in this one county carry the name.

El Cerrito was built by refugees and it was called Rust first. People leaving San Francisco after the 1906 earthquake settled on what was then Don Victor Castro's Rancho San Pablo. The post office opened in 1909 with Rust as the first postmaster on March 1 of that year. The residents disliked the name and changed it to El Cerrito, Little Hill. The village incorporated on August 23, 1917 with about 1,500 people, on a vote of 158 to 131.

The Cerrito Theatre at 10070 San Pablo Avenue opened in 1937 in Art Deco, closed in 1966, and was restored forty years later by the El Cerrito Redevelopment Agency. Victor Castro's adobe stood in the El Cerrito Plaza area until it burned in 1956.

El Cerrito has 11,247 housing units and 32.8 percent of them predate 1950, one of the highest pre war shares in the county. 66.6 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 58.6 percent, and the ACS median value is 1,124,400 dollars. ZHVI is about 1,122,000 dollars, up 3.4 percent over the year.

So the first questions here are the old housing questions.

Pull the year built. On pre war stock, ask about foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, and the condition of the original windows and siding. Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing, and get the permit history for the parcel.

Ask whether the building is nonconforming, because that limits what you can rebuild after a loss and it affects financing and insurance.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Then the part specific to this address. Confirm the city and the parcel before you compare anything, because three Mira Vistas in one county means listing sites and search results get crossed. On the hill ground, ask the inspector about soils and drainage on the parcel and about where water leaves it in a wet month. Ask about the condition and the ownership of any retaining structure. Get a written fire insurance quote on the exact address with the risk rating explained.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

Inside it

What it costs to own here

Figure about $10,494 a month to own the typical home in Mira Vista, $125,932 a year. Loan, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance are in that figure. HOA dues are not, and they vary too much to average.

On 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed average was 7.4 percent, where the year-ago figure was 6.27 percent. In Contra Costa County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Transfer tax on the typical Mira Vista home, $1,122,098, comes to roughly $14,699 at a rate of 13.10 per thousand. Convention puts about $7,350 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $13,914.

What waiting costs

On the same trailing rate, two years of waiting adds up to roughly $89,005 in price and payment combined, and five years to $282,565.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
