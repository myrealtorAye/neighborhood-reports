Proctor, Castro Valley: the October 2026 read

Proctor is one of Castro Valley's established residential neighborhoods, named for the school at its centre.

Guillermo Castro received Rancho San Lorenzo from Governor Juan Alvarado. Most sources date the grant to 1841 and one book on the valley says 1843, and the size is given as either 26,722 acres or about 28,000. It covered what is now Hayward, San Lorenzo and Castro Valley, taking in Crow Canyon, Cull Canyon and Palomares Canyon. Castro gambled. He sold off pieces of the rancho to cover his debts, mortgaged what was left, and fought lawsuits from Gold Rush settlers trying to clear squatters. In 1864 the sheriff, I. A. Mayhew, presided over the sale of the last of it for 400,000 dollars to Faxon Atherton.

The practical consequence of all that today is simpler. Castro Valley is the largest community in unincorporated Alameda County, so there is no city hall. The county handles planning, permits and most services.

Castro Valley has 22,957 housing units and 16.9 percent of them predate 1950. 73.3 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 72.5 percent, both high for this series. The ACS median value is 1,104,600 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,554.

The stock here is consistent in era, and that is useful: where houses went up inside a narrow window, comparable sales genuinely compare and the whole price spread is condition and updates. Pull the year built, compare within the cohort, then put your effort into the inspection report.

The list for this era: the roof and its age, the supply lines, the electrical panel capacity if you intend to add load, the sewer lateral, the furnace and the water heater.

So confirm jurisdiction before anything else. Alameda County, not a city, issues your permits and sets your zoning, and you should ask what services actually come with the address.

Then the fault. The Hayward Fault runs along the western edge of this valley, so ask the title company whether the parcel sits inside an Alquist-Priolo earthquake fault zone and ask whether the house has ever been bolted to its foundation.

Then drive the route to the freeway or to BART at your real commute hour.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Castro Valley I cover, and what each one is:

  • Downtown Castro Valley. A man lost this whole valley at the card table, more or less, and it carries his name anyway.
  • Five Canyons. Five Canyons is a later planned development on the hill ground at the eastern edge of Castro Valley.
  • Jensen Ranch. Jensen Ranch is hillside development on the ridge above Castro Valley, built late and built large.
  • Lake Chabot Area. The Lake Chabot area is the northern edge of Castro Valley, up against the reservoir and the regional parkland around it.
  • Palomares Hills. Palomares Hills sits above the valley in one of the canyons that was part of Guillermo Castro's rancho, and the canyon still carries the Palomares name.
  • Parsons. Parsons is a Castro Valley neighborhood named for the avenue running through it.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $1,088,972 here, transfer tax runs about $1,198 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $599 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $16,335.
