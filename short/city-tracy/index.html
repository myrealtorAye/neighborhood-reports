Tracy: the October 2026 read

Tracy is a railroad town and the date is exact. It was founded on September 8, 1878 and named for Lathrop J. Tracy, a grain merchant and railroad director from Mansfield, Ohio, who as far as the record shows never lived here. The town came out of a junction: Southern Pacific built its line around Suisun Bay in September 1878 and made the connection three miles east of Ellis, with one account calling it a Central Pacific junction. The railroad shut the Ellis coaling station and moved its employees and their families to Lathrop and to the new Tracy station. Southern Pacific moved its operational headquarters from Lathrop to Tracy in 1894. Tracy became a city in 1910.

Tracy has 30,228 housing units and 7.1 percent of them predate 1950. 81.0 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 65.0 percent, and the ACS median value is 699,000 dollars. ZHVI is about 668,000 dollars, down 4.1 percent over the year. That is the steepest twelve month decline of any city in San Joaquin County, and among the steepest in the three counties covered here. A buyer should know it going in.

That 4.1 percent decline deserves its own paragraph, because it is the steepest of any city in San Joaquin County over the same twelve months and among the steepest in the three counties covered here. Tracy is a commuter market at the far edge of the Bay Area's reach, and markets like that move further in both directions than the places they feed.

So the questions are era questions and assessment questions. Pull the year built and the permit history. On the newer southwestern side and in Tracy Hills, ask which community facilities districts the parcel sits in, what each levies and the year each expires, in writing from the county, because that line runs for decades and does not come off with the mortgage.

Get the association budget and the reserve study where there is an association, not the dues figure. Ask the inspector about grading and drainage on new construction. And drive the Altamont at your real commute hour, in both directions, before you decide anything.
