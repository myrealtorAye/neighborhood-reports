Moraga Country Club, Moraga: the October 2026 read

Moraga Country Club is a gated community built around a golf course.

Moraga carries a Californio family name. Joaquin Moraga built the Moraga Adobe in 1841, and it is the oldest of the five surviving adobe houses in Contra Costa County. Saint Mary's College moved here in 1928 and the campus has shaped the town ever since. The town is young as a jurisdiction. An incorporation election carried in November 1974, with November 13 given as the date, and the town was assembled out of three places that had grown separately: Moraga Town, Rheem and Rheem Valley. Rheem carries the name of its developer, Donald Laird Rheem, a co-founder of the Rheem Manufacturing Company. The first inhabitants of this valley were the Saklan people, of the Bay Miwok group, though one account names the Ohlone instead. One correction worth carrying: the Moraga Adobe that Joaquin Moraga built in 1841 stands on Adobe Lane in Orinda, not in this town.

Moraga has 5,719 housing units and only 1.6 percent of them predate 1950. 66.8 percent are single family detached, a lower detached share than its Lamorinda neighbours, and owner occupancy runs 83.1 percent. The ACS median value is 1,663,200 dollars and ZHVI is about 1,607,000, up 3.3 percent over the year.

So the buying questions here are newer-construction questions.

Get the homeowners association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, and ask exactly what the association maintains and what it does not.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate, and ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in and when each one expires, in writing. A newly built valley subdivision can carry a community facilities district on top of the base rate, and that line runs for decades and does not come off with the mortgage.

Then ask the inspector about the grading and the drainage, because a young subdivision on engineered fill shows its settlement in the first fifteen years.

Then the part specific to this address. Three things come before the house. Get the governing documents and read what the association controls, since a gated community governs more than a standard subdivision does. Ask in writing whether club membership transfers with the property and what it costs. And ask your lender early whether it will finance in this community.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Transfer tax on the typical Moraga Country Club home, $1,606,792, comes to roughly $1,767 at a rate of 1.10 per thousand. Expect it split, near enough $884 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $19,924.

What waiting costs

Waiting two years at this place's own trailing rate costs about $125,405, counting the price increase and the payment increase together. Over five years it is $397,806.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
