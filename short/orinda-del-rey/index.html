Del Rey, Orinda: the October 2026 read

Del Rey is a residential neighborhood in Orinda, near the school of the same name.

Orinda was a weekend place before a tunnel made it a town. The Caldecott Tunnel opened in 1937 and is credited with turning the area into a year round community. The Orinda Theatre opened in December 1941, and its dorsal fin marquee is still the most recognisable object in the city. Sources give the opening as December 27 and as December 17, so the exact day is contested. Orinda did not become a city until July 1, 1985, the eighteenth in Contra Costa County, on a March 5 vote of 4,687 to 3,293. Orinda also holds the county's oldest surviving adobe. The Moraga Adobe was built in 1841 by Joaquin Moraga and it is the oldest of the five surviving adobe houses in Contra Costa County. The name misleads people: it stands on Adobe Lane in Orinda, not in the town of Moraga. The land grant behind it is dated 1835.

Orinda has 7,475 housing units and 17.9 percent of them predate 1950, the highest pre war share in Lamorinda. 94.6 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 91.3 percent. The ACS median value is 1,863,200 dollars and ZHVI is about 1,943,000, up 2.6 percent over the year.

So the buying questions out here are hill questions and they come before the house.

Ask the inspector about soils and drainage on the parcel and about where water leaves it in a wet month. Ask about the condition and the ownership of every retaining structure, because a shared retaining wall on a slope is the most expensive thing that can go wrong.

Get a written fire insurance quote on the exact address with the risk rating explained, and ask what the insurer wants cleared to write it. In this part of the county that quote decides deals.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Then the part specific to this address. Confirm which school serves the address directly with the district. Orinda's schools are a large part of what buyers pay for here and attendance areas are the district's to define, not a listing's. Pull the tract map for the subdivision from the county recorder. The recording date, the original lot dimensions and the approved street layout are all on it, and that record beats anything a listing will tell you about when and how this neighborhood was built.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Del Rey is $1,942,797 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.59 percent from the prior month and up 2.6 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Listing activity in Orinda for 2026-09. Active inventory is 33. 26 new listings came on, and 20 went pending. Median days on market is 23.

Median asking price is $1,921,250 at $738 per square foot.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Del Rey is up 2.6 percent year over year. Realtor.com does not publish year over year listing figures at this geography, so the comparison below rests on the value index and the current month's listings.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What it costs to own here, month to month

Sellers underestimate this number, consistently, and it is the number that should set the pricing decision. Owning the typical home in Del Rey runs about $629,510 a month, or $7,554,119 a year. That figure is the loan payment at 3.5 percent down and 400 percent, property tax at 1.31 percent, insurance at the California average, utilities for a home of roughly 1,800 square feet, and maintenance budgeted at 1 percent of value a year. HOA dues are not included, because they vary by building and subdivision.

The 15-year average is 400 percent. These are the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey averages, published weekly, and they are survey averages and not a quote.

On a loan of $1,874,799, the typical home here with the minimum down payment, that year of rate movement is the difference between a payment of $624,933 and $624,933, a change of $0 a month on the same house at the same price. Rate movement has been the larger force on affordability here than price movement.

This is the section most buyers in Del Rey find out about too late. The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125. That single figure is both the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling. Above it, a loan is a jumbo, underwritten to a different and generally stricter standard, with its own reserve and credit requirements and no FHA option at all.

At the typical value here of $1,942,797, a 3.5 percent down payment leaves a loan of $1,874,799. That is $625,674 over the ceiling. To bring the loan down to the limit on a home at this value, the down payment has to be $693,672, or 35.7 percent. Put differently, the most expensive home a minimum-down FHA loan reaches in Contra Costa County is about $1,294,430, and the typical home in Del Rey is $648,367 above that.

There are three honest paths from here, and they are worth knowing before touring anything. Bring the larger down payment and stay conforming. Take a jumbo and qualify under its standards. Or shop at or below $1,294,430. In Orinda that means looking at the neighborhoods further down the ranking table above, where the minimum-down programs still function. Each of those is a real strategy. What does not work is assuming the 3.5 percent down program applies at this price.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $1,942,797 here, transfer tax runs about $2,137 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $1,069 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $24,091.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at 2.6 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 400 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $1,942,797 today becomes $2,043,903 in two years and $2,205,511 in five. The monthly payment moves from $624,933 to $657,455 and then $709,439. The cost of waiting comes to $881,640 over two years and $5,333,101 over five.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating 2.6 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
