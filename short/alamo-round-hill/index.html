Round Hill, Alamo: the October 2026 read

Round Hill is a country club community in Alamo, and it is gated.

Alamo is unincorporated. It has never been a city and that is the first fact about any address here. The valley around it voted on incorporation repeatedly and failed: valley wide attempts went to the ballot in 1964, 1967, 1973 and 1976 and all of them lost. The 1967 proposal would have joined Alamo, Danville and south San Ramon with an 1,800 foot wide cherry stem drawn to leave Bishop Ranch out. Danville went its own way in 1982 and San Ramon in 1983. Alamo stayed county ground.

Alamo has 5,315 housing units and 6.9 percent of them predate 1950. 93.2 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 91.8 percent. ZHVI is about 2,529,000 dollars, up 5.3 percent over the year. One number to read carefully. The ACS reports a median owner occupied value of 2,000,001 dollars here, and that is the survey's ceiling, not a measurement. The actual median sits above the highest figure the survey will print.

So the buying questions out here are hill questions and they come before the house.

Ask the inspector about soils and drainage on the parcel and about where water leaves it in a wet month. Ask about the condition and the ownership of every retaining structure, because a shared retaining wall on a slope is the most expensive thing that can go wrong.

Get a written fire insurance quote on the exact address with the risk rating explained, and ask what the insurer wants cleared to write it. In this part of the county that quote decides deals.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Then the part specific to this address. Confirm first that the address is unincorporated county territory, because it changes who issues your permits, who provides your services, who polices it and how your tax bill is built. Take it from the county, not from a listing. Then ask which school district serves the parcel, since district lines out here do not follow the boundaries a buyer assumes. Then get the governing documents and the reserve study, and ask in writing whether club membership transfers with the property, what it costs and whether there is a waiting list.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Round Hill is $2,529,452 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.94 percent from the prior month and up 5.3 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Listing activity in Alamo for 2026-09. Active inventory is 27. 12 new listings came on, and 9 went pending. Median days on market is 31.

Median asking price is $3,093,750 at $874 per square foot.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Round Hill is up 5.3 percent year over year. Realtor.com does not publish year over year listing figures at this geography, so the comparison below rests on the value index and the current month's listings.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

Rate movement has been the larger force on affordability here than price movement.

This is the section most buyers in Round Hill find out about too late. The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125. That single figure is both the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling. Above it, a loan is a jumbo, underwritten to a different and generally stricter standard, with its own reserve and credit requirements and no FHA option at all.

At the typical value here of $2,529,452, a 3.5 percent down payment leaves a loan of $2,440,921. That is $1,191,796 over the ceiling. To bring the loan down to the limit on a home at this value, the down payment has to be $1,280,327, or 50.6 percent. Put differently, the most expensive home a minimum-down FHA loan reaches in Contra Costa County is about $1,294,430, and the typical home in Round Hill is $1,235,022 above that.

There are three honest paths from here, and they are worth knowing before touring anything. Bring the larger down payment and stay conforming. Take a jumbo and qualify under its standards. Or shop at or below $1,294,430. In Alamo that means looking at the neighborhoods further down the ranking table above, where the minimum-down programs still function. Each of those is a real strategy. What does not work is assuming the 3.5 percent down program applies at this price.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $2,529,452 here, transfer tax runs about $2,782 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $1,391 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at 5.3 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 400 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $2,529,452 today becomes $2,802,531 in two years and $3,268,410 in five. The monthly payment moves from $813,640 to $901,481 and then $1,051,339. The cost of waiting comes to $2,381,253 over two years and $15,000,855 over five.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating 5.3 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
