Coronado, Richmond: the October 2026 read

Coronado is one of Richmond's older flatland neighborhoods, south of the city centre.

Standard Oil built its refinery here in 1901, with one account giving 1902. By 1904 it was reported as the second largest oil refinery in the world. Chevron runs it today. The city incorporated in 1905, after the refinery, not before it.

Henry Kaiser opened his first Richmond shipyard in December 1940. The city had fewer than 24,000 people that year and nearly 100,000 by 1943. Four yards here built 747 ships, more than any other shipyard complex in the country. The Rosie the Riveter World War II Home Front National Historical Park was dedicated on October 25, 2000 and was the first national park created to commemorate the home front.

Richmond has 42,509 housing units and 28.5 percent of them predate 1950. 59.9 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 54.7 percent, and the ACS median value is 668,200 dollars.

So the first questions here are the old housing questions.

Pull the year built. Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing, and get the permit history. Ask whether the building is nonconforming, because that limits what you can rebuild after a loss and it affects financing and insurance.

On pre war stock: foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, and the condition of original windows and siding.

Ask for the site history in writing on any parcel with an industrial past. Richmond has a century of heavy industry in it and the record is public.

Pull the zoning for the parcel and everything touching it. Richmond has heavy industry woven through its flats and a permitted use next door is not always what operates there now.

One Richmond specific worth knowing. The city formally recognises 28 neighborhood councils and Coronado is one of them. The system came out of the upheaval the Kaiser shipyards caused here, with an early pilot in North Richmond in 1956. The Richmond Neighborhood Coordinating Council is the umbrella body for all of them and it is the final arbiter of where one council's boundary ends and the next begins. It meets the second Monday of the month at 7:00 pm.

So take the boundary from the city's own neighborhood council map, not from a listing. Agents draw these lines loosely and the city's map is the one that governs. Then sit in on a council meeting before you write an offer. That hour tells you what the neighborhood is arguing about, and no listing will.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What it costs to own here

Put the minimum down on the typical Coronado home and the monthly carry lands near $5,185. That is $62,225 across a year, counting loan, tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance. HOA dues are not in it, since no two are alike.

On 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed average was 7.4 percent, where the year-ago figure was 6.27 percent. In Contra Costa County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Transfer tax on the typical Coronado home, $505,246, comes to roughly $4,092 at a rate of 8.10 per thousand. Expect it split, near enough $2,046 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $1,000,000 the rate steps from 8.10 to 13.50 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,400 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $1,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $6,063.

What waiting costs

On the same trailing rate, two years of waiting adds up to roughly $2,385 in price and payment combined, and five years to $7,221.

That rate is one observed year extrapolated forward, not a forecast, and compounding a single strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Weight the two year figure more heavily.
