Eden Shores, Hayward: the October 2026 read

Eden Shores is the newest ground in Hayward and it sits out on the bay edge. That is the whole brief for buying here.

The Eden name comes from Eden Township, the agricultural district that made this city. From the later nineteenth century Hayward was one of California's leading fruit growing areas and Hunt Brothers ran a cannery here from 1895, with the city calling itself the Apricot City by 1950. Eden Shores has nothing to do with that era. It is recent master planned development built out toward the shoreline, with the marsh and the bay trail beyond it.

Hayward has 52,243 housing units and only 9.1 percent of them predate 1950, so this is overwhelmingly a postwar city. 51.2 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 58.0 percent, the ACS median value is 854,400 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,391. Out here essentially none of the stock is pre war, a good deal of it sits inside homeowner associations, and it is the most recently built housing in the city.

So the questions are new development questions and bay edge questions.

On the association: get the budget and the reserve study, not the dues number, ask what it insures, and ask specifically what it maintains on the shoreline side, because on made and low ground that can include drainage and levee obligations.

On the tax bill: pull the actual bill for the address instead of the listing's estimate, because newer development here frequently carries district assessments on top of the base rate and they are easy to miss until the first bill arrives.

On the ground: get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire, and ask the inspector about settlement, because this is low flat land at the bay margin.

Then the fault question that applies citywide: ask whether the foundation has been bolted.

Then go walk the bay trail from the edge of the neighborhood at dusk. That shoreline access is the actual amenity you are paying for, and it is worth confirming how you reach it on foot.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Hayward I cover, and what each one is:

  • Burbank. Burbank is one of Hayward's older central neighborhoods, close in to downtown, and the published history belongs to the city, not the pocket, so this is that history plus the development record.
  • Downtown Hayward. The city was incorporated on March 11, 1876 under the name Haywards, with the s, and it was named after a hotel.
  • Fairview Hills. Fairview Hills is on the unincorporated slope above Hayward, and that word unincorporated is the practical headline.
  • Fairway Park. Fairway Park is postwar Hayward out toward the bay side, laid out around a golf course, and the name is literal.
  • Glen Eden. Eden is the old name for this whole area.
  • Harder Tennyson. Harder and Tennyson are the two roads this neighborhood is named for, in the southern part of Hayward, and the history here is the city's.
  • Hayward Highland. Hayward Highland sits on the slope above the city with the Hayward Fault running along the foot of it.
  • Hayward Hills. The Hayward Fault runs along the base of these hills, and it is the reason there are hills here at all.

What it costs to own here

Figure about $7,938 a month to own the typical home in Eden Shores, $95,260 a year. Loan, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance are in that figure. HOA dues are not, and they vary too much to average.

The survey average for a 30-year fixed is 7.4 percent at 2026-10-15, compared with 6.27 percent a year back. Alameda County's 2026 one-unit loan limit sits at $1,249,125. That is the cap for conforming conventional and for FHA alike.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $816,424 here, transfer tax runs about $7,838 at 9.60 dollars per thousand. Expect it split, near enough $3,919 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $12,246.

What waiting costs

Waiting two years at this place's own trailing rate costs about -$44,655, counting the price increase and the payment increase together. Over five years it is -$130,051.

This rests on one observed year carried forward. The two year figure is the defensible one; the five year figure is arithmetic, not a forecast.
