Blackhawk, Blackhawk: the October 2026 read

Blackhawk is a gated community and it is not a city.

Blackhawk is unincorporated Contra Costa County territory on the eastern side of the San Ramon Valley, below Mount Diablo. The valley's towns incorporated around it, Danville in 1982 after voters approved it on June 8 of that year, and San Ramon in 1983, following four failed valley wide attempts in 1964, 1967, 1973 and 1976. Blackhawk remained county ground and it is governed as a private community inside it.

The man who built it left a museum behind. Kenneth Behring developed Blackhawk, and a partnership he began with Don Williams in 1982 opened the Blackhawk Automotive Museum in August 1988, called the Behring Auto Museum in some accounts. It started as a home for his car collection and now runs four more galleries alongside it: The Spirit of the Old West, Art of Africa, Into China and World of Nature. It is a subsidiary of the non profit Behring Global Educational Foundation and an affiliate of the Smithsonian Institution. It sits inside Blackhawk Plaza and is not affiliated with the shopping centre. Behring also owned the Seattle Seahawks and founded the Wheelchair Foundation. He died in 2019 at 91.

Blackhawk has 3,923 housing units and only 0.9 percent of them predate 1950. 91.2 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 89.3 percent. One number to read carefully. The ACS reports a median owner occupied value of 2,000,001 dollars here, and that is the survey's ceiling, not a measurement. The actual median sits above the highest figure the survey will print. On the price level, note the scope: neither Zillow nor Realtor.com publishes a separate value series for Blackhawk, so the nearest published figure is Danville's, about 1,821,000 dollars and up 0.2 percent over the year. Blackhawk trades well above that and the Danville number should not be read as this community's value.

So the buying questions here are newer-construction questions.

Get the homeowners association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, and ask exactly what the association maintains and what it does not.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate, and ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in and when each one expires, in writing. A newly built valley subdivision can carry a community facilities district on top of the base rate, and that line runs for decades and does not come off with the mortgage.

Then ask the inspector about the grading and the drainage, because a young subdivision on engineered fill shows its settlement in the first fifteen years.

Then the part specific to this address. Confirm first that the address is unincorporated county territory, because it changes who issues your permits, who provides your services, who polices it and how your tax bill is built. Take it from the county, not from a listing. Then ask which school district serves the parcel, since district lines out here do not follow the boundaries a buyer assumes. Then the three that are specific to a gated community. Get the governing documents and read what the association controls, because it is more than a standard subdivision governs. Get the budget and the reserve study, since private roads and private gates are the association's to maintain and that is where assessments come from. And ask in writing whether any country club membership transfers with the property and what it costs.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What the market is doing right now

Typical home value in Blackhawk is $1,982,682 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.41 percent from the prior month and down 1.2 percent over the trailing year. Month over month is the number worth watching in a monthly series. A trailing twelve month figure keeps reporting last winter's market long after it has turned.

Realtor.com does not publish monthly listing metrics for Blackhawk, so there is no inventory, days on market or asking price figure to report here. The value series above is measured separately and is unaffected.

Two numbers in this report move in opposite directions. It is worth saying why, and not quietly picking the flattering one. Typical home value in Blackhawk is down 1.2 percent year over year. Realtor.com does not publish year over year listing figures at this geography, so the comparison below rests on the value index and the current month's listings.

They disagree because they measure different populations. The Zillow Home Value Index estimates the typical value of every home in the area, listed or not, and it is smoothed and seasonally adjusted. The median listing price describes only the homes that happen to be on the market this month. Change the mix of what is listed, and the median asking price moves without any individual home changing value. A month heavier in condominiums and smaller units pulls the median asking price down even while the typical value of the housing stock rises.

The practical read: use the value index to understand what the housing stock is worth, and use the listing figures to understand the conditions you are transacting in right now. For a seller, the listing numbers are the ones that describe the competition. For an owner estimating equity, the value index is the better guide. Neither one is the market on its own.

What it costs to own here, month to month

Sellers underestimate this number, consistently, and it is the number that should set the pricing decision. Owning the typical home in Blackhawk runs about $17,901 a month, or $214,811 a year. That figure is the loan payment at 3.5 percent down and 7.4 percent, property tax at 1.31 percent, insurance at the California average, utilities for a home of roughly 1,800 square feet, and maintenance budgeted at 1 percent of value a year. HOA dues are not included, because they vary by building and subdivision.

The 30-year fixed average is 7.4 percent as of 2026-10-15, against 6.27 percent in the same week a year earlier, a move of up 1.13 percentage points. The 15-year average is 6.71 percent. These are the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey averages, published weekly, and they are survey averages and not a quote.

On a loan of $1,913,288, the typical home here with the minimum down payment, that year of rate movement is the difference between a payment of $11,805 and $13,247, a change of $1,442 a month on the same house at the same price. Rate movement has been the larger force on affordability here than price movement.

This is the section most buyers in Blackhawk find out about too late. The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125. That single figure is both the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling. Above it, a loan is a jumbo, underwritten to a different and generally stricter standard, with its own reserve and credit requirements and no FHA option at all.

At the typical value here of $1,982,682, a 3.5 percent down payment leaves a loan of $1,913,288. That is $664,163 over the ceiling. To bring the loan down to the limit on a home at this value, the down payment has to be $733,557, or 37.0 percent. Put differently, the most expensive home a minimum-down FHA loan reaches in Contra Costa County is about $1,294,430, and the typical home in Blackhawk is $688,252 above that.

There are three honest paths from here, and they are worth knowing before touring anything. Bring the larger down payment and stay conforming. Take a jumbo and qualify under its standards. Or shop at or below $1,294,430. In Blackhawk that means looking at the neighborhoods further down the ranking table above, where the minimum-down programs still function. Each of those is a real strategy. What does not work is assuming the 3.5 percent down program applies at this price.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $1,982,682 here, transfer tax runs about $2,181 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $1,090 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $24,585.

What waiting actually costs here

This is the math on waiting, and the assumptions are stated, not buried. Appreciation is set at -1.2 percent, this neighborhood's own trailing twelve month change in typical value as of 2026-09-30. The rate is held at the current 7.4 percent average. The down payment is 3.5 percent. The formula is the price increase plus the payment increase over the waiting period, with rent shown separately and not folded into the headline figure.

At those assumptions, a home at $1,982,682 today becomes $1,936,214 in two years and $1,868,546 in five. The monthly payment moves from $13,247 to $12,937 and then $12,485. The cost of waiting comes to -$53,919 over two years and -$159,891 over five.

Now the part that most versions of this calculation leave out. Extrapolating -1.2 percent for five years is an extrapolation of a single observed year, not a forecast, and compounding one strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Treat the five year figure as a scenario built on one year of data, and weight the two year figure more heavily.

The cost of waiting is also not one-directional. Rates move, and they have moved more than prices here over the past year. Hold the price constant and move only the rate and the rate component shows up on its own. If rates fall, part of the cost of waiting reverses. If they rise, it compounds. Anyone presenting this calculation as a single certain number is selling, not calculating.
