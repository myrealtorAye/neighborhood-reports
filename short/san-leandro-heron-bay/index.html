Heron Bay, San Leandro: the October 2026 read

Heron Bay is the newest ground in San Leandro, out at the shoreline, and that position is the entire brief.

Everything inland of it was Rancho San Leandro, granted to Jose Joaquin Estudillo, born in Monterey in 1800, who served as alcalde of Yerba Buena and then moved his wife and ten children across the bay to settle on San Leandro Creek. The grant ran south to San Lorenzo Creek and he died in 1852. The city that grew from it went orchards, then factories, then postwar tracts, and Heron Bay is the chapter after all of that: recent master planned development on the bay edge, with marsh and shoreline trail beyond it.

San Leandro has 31,954 housing units and 26.2 percent of them predate 1950. 57.3 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 58.1 percent, the ACS median value is 844,200 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,186. Out here essentially none of the stock is pre war, a good deal of it is attached housing, and much of it sits inside a homeowners association.

So the questions are new development questions and shoreline questions.

On the association: get the budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, ask what it insures, and ask specifically what it maintains on the shoreline side, because on low ground that can include drainage and levee obligations that become assessments.

On the tax bill: pull the actual bill for the address, not the listing estimate, since newer development frequently carries district assessments on top of the base rate.

On the ground: get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire, and ask the inspector about settlement, because this is low flat land at the bay margin.

Then the fault question that applies citywide: ask whether the foundation has been bolted.

Then walk the shoreline trail at dusk from the edge of the neighborhood. That access is the amenity you are paying for and it is worth confirming on foot.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $798,068 here, transfer tax runs about $9,657 at 12.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $4,828 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $11,971.
