Tassajara Hills, Dublin: the October 2026 read

Tassajara Hills sits on the rising ground at Dublin's northeastern edge, where the city runs up against open hills.

The valley's record starts with a land grant. Jose Maria Amador received over 16,000 acres here in 1835 for his service as a Mexican soldier and as administrator at Mission San Jose, and the Amador Valley carries his name. Michael Murray and Jeremiah Fallon arrived in 1846 and bought 982 acres from Amador in the 1850s with money they had made in the Gold Rush. Murray Township was created in June 1853 and named for Murray, a few months after Alameda County itself. James Witt Dougherty then bought 10,000 acres from Amador and became the largest landowner in the area. The ground these communities sit on stayed open until the city, incorporated only in February 1982, began approving large scale development on it. The physical remains of the nineteenth century are all on the other side of town at Dublin Boulevard and Donlon Way: the Murray Schoolhouse from 1856, the Pioneer Cemetery from 1859 and Green's Store from 1860.

Dublin has 25,157 housing units and 0.8 percent of them predate 1950, among the lowest shares of any city in this series. 54.2 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 65.7 percent, the ACS median value is 1,317,100 dollars and the median gross rent is 3,191.

So the association and the tax bill come before the house.

Get the association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, because the reserve position is what becomes a special assessment. Ask what it insures, what it maintains, and whether any of that includes slopes, drainage or private roads.

Then pull the actual tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate. Development of this era in Dublin frequently sits inside a community facilities district, and those assessments ride on top of the base rate. They are easy to miss until the first bill lands and they can move a monthly payment materially.

Then the house. On stock this recent the list is the roof, the water heater, the furnace and the air conditioning, plus any original dual pane windows, because those are the systems that reach end of life first.

Ask what is entitled on the parcels around you, because east Dublin has been building continuously and an open field is not a permanent view.

Then drive the commute at your real hour, and check the BART park and ride situation if you intend to use it, because that is the constraint people underestimate here.

On sloped ground add the hillside list: the soils and drainage history for the parcel, the condition and ownership of any retaining structures, and where water leaves the property in a wet month. Get a written fire insurance quote on the exact address before your contingencies expire, with the risk rating explained, because this is hill ground against open space.

What it costs to own here

Carry on the typical home here is roughly $11,516 monthly, or $138,189 annually. That covers the loan payment, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance at a stated budget. HOA dues are excluded, because they differ building to building.

As of 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed averages 7.4 percent, up or down from 6.27 percent twelve months before. In Alameda County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 1.10 dollars per thousand, a $1,227,704 sale in Tassajara Hills carries about $1,350 in transfer tax. Expect it split, near enough $675 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $18,416.

What waiting costs

On the same trailing rate, two years of waiting adds up to roughly -$151,741 in price and payment combined, and five years to -$422,060.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
