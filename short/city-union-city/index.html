Union City: the October 2026 read

Union City is two nineteenth century towns that agreed in 1959 to stop being annexed.

The Horner brothers made the first non-native settlement here in 1850. Henry C. Smith founded New Haven in 1851, and in 1853 New Haven combined with the nearby town of Alvarado and became the county seat of the newly created Alameda County. Alvarado carries the name of Governor Juan Alvarado, the same governor who granted Guillermo Castro the rancho that became Castro Valley. In 1870 the California Beet Sugar Company opened at Alvarado and ran until 1873, the first successful sugar beet factory in the United States. Ezra Decoto sold land to the railroad, in 1867 by one account and 1870 by another, and the settlement that grew there took his name.

By the 1950s both Alvarado and Decoto were facing absorption by Hayward. A citizens committee petitioned to incorporate instead, the two towns voted together in January 1959, with sources giving January 13 for the vote and January 26 for the incorporation, and Tom Kitayama, who had sat on that committee, became the first mayor.

So the city is sixty odd years old and the places inside it are a hundred and seventy. That shows in the housing.

Union City has 21,901 housing units and 4.0 percent of them predate 1950. 57.8 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 64.7 percent, the ACS median value is 1,104,900 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,713. Only 4.0 percent predates 1950, and almost all of that is in Old Alvarado and Decoto.

That gives you a simple rule for shopping here. Pull the year built first. In the old districts you are buying a pre war building with the full inspection list and a legal unit count worth confirming with the city. Everywhere else you are buying consistent postwar tract housing where the comparable sales genuinely compare and the price spread is condition.

Two things apply across the whole city. It is flat ground near the bay, so get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire. And the Union City BART station is the single largest factor in what an address is worth here, so walk to it from the front door and time it at the hour you would really be walking.

Inside it

What it costs to own here

Figure about $11,237 a month to own the typical home in Union City, $134,845 a year. Loan, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance are in that figure. HOA dues are not, and they vary too much to average.

Rates sit at 7.4 percent on the 30-year fixed as of 2026-10-15. A year earlier the same survey read 6.27 percent. In Alameda County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Figure $1,315 in transfer tax on a $1,195,668 sale here, at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Convention puts about $658 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $17,935.

What waiting costs

On the same trailing rate, two years of waiting adds up to roughly -$52,718 in price and payment combined, and five years to -$154,610.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
