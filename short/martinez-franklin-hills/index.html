Franklin Hills, Martinez: the October 2026 read

Franklin Hills is hill ground on the Martinez side of the ridge.

Martinez was laid out in 1849 by Col. William M. Smith and named for Ygnacio Martinez. It became the county seat in 1850 and it has been ever since. It could not incorporate at the time, because it did not have the 200 registered voters the law required, and it did not become a city until 1876. Rumours that the oil companies were coming set off a real estate rush in 1912, and Shell located here in 1915.

Martinez runs from a nineteenth century waterfront downtown up into hill subdivisions built a century later, and this is the later end of that range.

Martinez has 15,178 housing units and 14.0 percent of them predate 1950, a high pre war share for this county, behind El Cerrito, Richmond and San Pablo. 63.7 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 70.8 percent, the highest of the six, and the ACS median value is 859,800 dollars. Citywide ZHVI is about 750,000 dollars, down 1.3 percent over the year.

So the buying questions are mostly postwar questions.

Pull the year built and the permit history for the parcel. On a tract house of this era, ask about the electrical panel and its capacity, the supply lines, the sewer lateral, the roof, and whether the foundation has ever been bolted.

An original kitchen and original aluminium windows are either the discount or the asset. The inspection report tells you which.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate, and ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in.

Then the part specific to this address. On the higher ground, ask the inspector about soils and drainage on the parcel and about where water leaves it in a wet month. Ask about the condition and the ownership of any retaining structure, because a shared retaining wall on a slope is the most expensive thing that can go wrong here. Get a written fire insurance quote on the exact address with the risk rating explained. Pull the tract map for the subdivision from the county recorder. It carries the recording date, the original lot dimensions and the street layout as approved, and that record beats anything a listing will tell you about when and how this neighborhood was built.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Franklin Hills charges 1.10 per thousand, so the typical $749,729 home owes roughly $825 at transfer. Convention puts about $412 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $8,997.

What waiting costs

Waiting two years at this place's own trailing rate costs about -$22,344, counting the price increase and the payment increase together. Over five years it is -$66,145.

That rate is one observed year extrapolated forward, not a forecast, and compounding a single strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Weight the two year figure more heavily.
