Lafayette: the October 2026 read

Lafayette's main street was laid out by a grist mill.

Lafayette started as a Mexican land grant. Rancho Acalanes was granted in 1834 to Candelario Valencia, and Elam Brown was its third owner. Brown's party reached the area in 1846 and settled in 1848. Accounts of the purchase differ, one putting it at 1847 for 900 dollars across 3,328.9 acres and another at 1848. Brown built a horse powered grist mill in 1849 and the commerce that gathered around it set the line of what is now Mt. Diablo Boulevard. Brown served as alcalde, sat in the state constitutional convention of 1849, and served in California's first two legislatures. The city itself did not incorporate until 1968.

Lafayette has 9,998 housing units and 16.5 percent of them predate 1950, a high pre war share for this part of the county. 74.3 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 77.1 percent. ZHVI is about 1,907,000 dollars, up 3.5 percent over the year. One number to read carefully. The ACS reports a median owner occupied value of 2,000,001 dollars here, and that is the survey's ceiling, not a measurement. The actual median sits above the highest figure the survey will print.

That 16.5 percent pre war share is the thing to plan around. Lafayette holds real nineteenth and early twentieth century housing alongside postwar and recent construction, and the inspection list changes completely between them.

So pull the year built first. On the older stock, foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring and galvanized supply lines. On the hill addresses, soils, drainage, retaining walls and a written fire insurance quote on the exact address. In Reliez Valley and on the northern edge, confirm whether the parcel is inside the city or in unincorporated county territory, because it changes your permits and your services.

And get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

What it costs to own here

Figure about $17,252 a month to own the typical home in Lafayette, $207,026 a year. Loan, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance are in that figure. HOA dues are not, and they vary too much to average.

The survey average for a 30-year fixed is 7.4 percent at 2026-10-15, compared with 6.27 percent a year back. The conforming and FHA ceiling for a one-unit property in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125 in 2026.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Lafayette charges 1.10 per thousand, so the typical $1,907,310 home owes roughly $2,098 at transfer. Local practice splits it down the middle, roughly $1,049 each, though the contract decides.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $23,651.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about $155,365 here. The five year figure is $493,904.

That rate is one observed year extrapolated forward, not a forecast, and compounding a single strong year out to five produces a large number by construction. Weight the two year figure more heavily.
