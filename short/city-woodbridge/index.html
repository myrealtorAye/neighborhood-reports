Woodbridge: the October 2026 read

Woodbridge is unincorporated San Joaquin County territory on the Mokelumne River just west of Lodi, in the vineyard country. Lodi next door developed the Flame Tokay and built its arch in 1907 for the first Tokay Carnival, an event that runs today as the Lodi Grape Festival.

Woodbridge has 1,648 housing units and 3.0 percent of them predate 1950. 89.9 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 83.5 percent, and the ACS median value is 559,800 dollars. ZHVI is about 587,000 dollars, up 0.4 percent over the year.

So the buying questions are mostly postwar questions.

Pull the year built and the permit history for the parcel. On a tract house of this era, ask about the electrical panel and its capacity, the supply lines, the sewer lateral, the roof, and whether the foundation has ever been bolted.

An original kitchen and original aluminium windows are either the discount or the asset. The inspection report tells you which.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Confirm first that the address is unincorporated San Joaquin County territory, because it changes who issues your permits, who provides your services, who polices it and how your tax bill is built. Take it from the county, not from a listing. Then ask which school district serves the parcel. Get the flood zone determination for the parcel with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire, and ask the inspector about drainage and about where water leaves the property in a wet month. This is Delta and valley floor ground and levee and flood mapping drive the insurance. Ask whether the parcel is on sewer or on septic, and if septic, get the most recent inspection and the pumping record. Ask whether water comes from a district or a well, and if a well, get the yield and the water quality tests in writing. On valley agricultural ground, also ask what is farmed on the adjoining parcels and when it is sprayed.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. In an unincorporated place there is no city hall between you and the county, so your permits, your inspections and your service districts all come from the county and each district levies on the parcel. Ask the county's Local Agency Formation Commission whether the parcel sits in any city's sphere of influence, because that is where future annexation would start and it is a public record.
