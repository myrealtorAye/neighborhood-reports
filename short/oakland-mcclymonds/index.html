McClymonds, Oakland: the October 2026 read

Bill Russell went to high school here. So did Paul Silas, Joe Ellis, Antonio Davis, Wendell Hayes and Marcus Peters.

McClymonds started in January 1915 in a small building at 12th and Market that Oakland Technical High School had just moved out of, with sixty students. It opened as a vocational school and it was the first public school in California to offer summer school. The name honours John W. McClymonds, who ran the Oakland schools as superintendent from 1889 to 1913 and pushed for the school's creation. The campus moved to 26th and Myrtle in 1924. In its first thirty six years the school changed location five times and operated under eight different names. In 2005 it was split into two small schools, BEST and EXCEL, under Oakland's Small Schools Initiative, and the campus has since been through a three phase modernisation covering building systems and code compliance.

A school that produced that list of athletes is the landmark of this neighborhood, and the neighborhood carries its name.

The housing around it is West Oakland stock: Victorian and early twentieth century, dense, much of it divided into flats. Oakland has 191,713 housing units, 71,234 of them built in 1939 or earlier and 18,124 more in the 1940s, so 46.6 percent of the city predates 1950. Only 41.5 percent of units citywide are single family detached and owner occupancy is 42.3 percent. Both figures run lower here.

So the first questions are regulatory, not cosmetic.

Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing. Get the permit history. Ask whether the building is nonconforming, since that limits rebuilding after a loss and affects both financing and insurance. If it holds more than one unit, read Oakland's rent adjustment program and the just cause for eviction rules before you model income.

Then the pre war list, and it is long on stock this old: foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, the sewer lateral Oakland certifies at sale, and the condition of original siding and windows.

Oakland's transfer tax runs 16.10 dollars per thousand and the bracket applies to the whole price. Check your number against the next threshold.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Oakland I cover, and what each one is:

  • Acorn. Fifty blocks of this neighborhood were demolished.
  • Adams Point. Adams Point is named for Edson Adams, one of the men who got hold of Oakland's land early, and in 1906 it filled up with San Franciscans who had nowhere else to go.
  • Allendale. The Allendale tract map was recorded in 1892, and there was a cinema operating here by 1913.
  • Brookfield Village. Twelve hundred houses went up here during the Second World War and they sold for four thousand dollars each.
  • Bushrod. Bushrod was annexed by Oakland in 1897, in the same batch as Temescal, Golden Gate and Piedmont Avenue, and that date is the most solid thing in its published record.
  • Caballo Hills. Caballo Hills is a hillside pocket at the southeastern edge of Oakland with very little published history, so this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Castlemont. Castlemont takes its name from the high school at its centre, and its published history is thin enough that this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Chabot Park. Anthony Chabot dammed a creek in 1868 and built Oakland its first water supply at municipal scale, and his name is on the parkland at the edge of this neighborhood.

What it costs to own here

The all-in monthly number for the typical home here is about $5,911, $70,929 over twelve months. It counts the payment, the tax bill, insurance, utilities and a maintenance allowance, and leaves out HOA dues.

As of 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed averages 7.4 percent, up or down from 6.27 percent twelve months before. The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming ceiling and the FHA ceiling.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 16.10 dollars per thousand, a $567,543 sale in McClymonds carries about $9,137 in transfer tax. Local practice splits it down the middle, roughly $4,569 each, though the contract decides.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $2,000,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 18.60 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $2,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $8,513.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about -$88,253 here. The five year figure is -$240,203.

This rests on one observed year carried forward. The two year figure is the defensible one; the five year figure is arithmetic, not a forecast.
