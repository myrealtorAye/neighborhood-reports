Downtown Orinda, Orinda: the October 2026 read

Downtown Orinda is small, and the theatre is the landmark.

Orinda was a weekend place before a tunnel made it a town. The Caldecott Tunnel opened in 1937 and is credited with turning the area into a year round community. The Orinda Theatre opened in December 1941, and its dorsal fin marquee is still the most recognisable object in the city. Sources give the opening as December 27 and as December 17, so the exact day is contested. Orinda did not become a city until July 1, 1985, the eighteenth in Contra Costa County, on a March 5 vote of 4,687 to 3,293. Orinda also holds the county's oldest surviving adobe. The Moraga Adobe was built in 1841 by Joaquin Moraga and it is the oldest of the five surviving adobe houses in Contra Costa County. The name misleads people: it stands on Adobe Lane in Orinda, not in the town of Moraga. The land grant behind it is dated 1835.

Orinda has 7,475 housing units and 17.9 percent of them predate 1950, the highest pre war share in Lamorinda. 94.6 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 91.3 percent. The ACS median value is 1,863,200 dollars and ZHVI is about 1,943,000, up 2.6 percent over the year.

So the first questions here are the old housing questions.

Pull the year built. On pre war stock, ask about foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring, galvanized supply lines, and the condition of the original windows and siding. Get the permit history for the parcel.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Then the part specific to this address. The Orinda Theatre's dorsal fin marquee has been standing since December 1941. Downtown adds two. Pull the zoning for the parcel and its neighbours, and ask the city whether the address falls inside a downtown specific plan area. Orinda has argued about downtown development for decades and those documents are where the argument is written down.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What it costs to own here

Carry on the typical home here is roughly $17,558 monthly, or $210,691 annually. That covers the loan payment, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance at a stated budget. HOA dues are excluded, because they differ building to building.

The 30-year fixed average is 7.4 percent as of 2026-10-15, against 6.27 percent in the same week last year. The conforming and FHA ceiling for a one-unit property in Contra Costa County is $1,249,125 in 2026.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Figure $2,137 in transfer tax on a $1,942,797 sale here, at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Expect it split, near enough $1,069 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against Richmond at 13.50. Same county, same price, and the spread is $24,091.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about $117,318 here. The five year figure is $368,033.
