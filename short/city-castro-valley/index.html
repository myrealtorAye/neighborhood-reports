Castro Valley: the October 2026 read

A man named Guillermo Castro gambled away a valley, and the valley kept his name.

Guillermo Castro received Rancho San Lorenzo from Governor Juan Alvarado. Most sources date the grant to 1841 and one book on the valley says 1843, and the size is given as either 26,722 acres or about 28,000. It covered what is now Hayward, San Lorenzo and Castro Valley, taking in Crow Canyon, Cull Canyon and Palomares Canyon. Castro gambled. He sold off pieces of the rancho to cover his debts, mortgaged what was left, and fought lawsuits from Gold Rush settlers trying to clear squatters. In 1864 the sheriff, I. A. Mayhew, presided over the sale of the last of it for 400,000 dollars to Faxon Atherton.

That is the whole nineteenth century here in one story, and the ground Castro lost is now Hayward, San Lorenzo and Castro Valley.

The twentieth century fact that matters more to a buyer is administrative. Castro Valley is the largest community in unincorporated Alameda County. There is no city hall, no city council and no city planning department. Alameda County issues the permits, sets the zoning and provides most of the services, and there have been repeated efforts over the years to incorporate it as a city or annex it to Hayward or San Leandro, none of which took.

Castro Valley has 22,957 housing units and 16.9 percent of them predate 1950. 73.3 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 72.5 percent, both high for this series. The ACS median value is 1,104,600 dollars and the median gross rent is 2,554. That 73.3 percent detached figure and 72.5 percent owner occupancy are both high for this series, and they describe a valley of houses instead of apartments.

So three things here that do not apply the same way elsewhere.

Confirm jurisdiction. The county, not a city, answers your permit and zoning questions, and you should ask directly what services come with a specific address.

Ask the fault question. The Hayward Fault runs along the western edge of this valley. Ask the title company whether a parcel sits inside an Alquist-Priolo earthquake fault zone, and ask whether the house has ever been bolted.

And on the canyon and hillside ground, a large share of this valley, ask for the soils and drainage history, the condition and ownership of any retaining structures, and a written fire insurance quote on the exact address.

What the market is doing right now

As of 2026-09-30 the typical Castro Valley home is worth $1,088,972. That is up 0.16 percent on the month and up 1.2 percent on the year.

Listing activity in Castro Valley for 2026-09. Active inventory is 56, up 15.1 percent from the prior month and down 13.5 percent from a year earlier. 34 new listings came on, up 5.4 percent month over month, and 25 went pending. Inside it

This report covers 7 areas of Castro Valley. What each one actually is:

  • Downtown Castro Valley. A man lost this whole valley at the card table, more or less, and it carries his name anyway.
  • Five Canyons. Five Canyons is a later planned development on the hill ground at the eastern edge of Castro Valley.
  • Jensen Ranch. Jensen Ranch is hillside development on the ridge above Castro Valley, built late and built large.
  • Lake Chabot Area. The Lake Chabot area is the northern edge of Castro Valley, up against the reservoir and the regional parkland around it.
  • Palomares Hills. Palomares Hills sits above the valley in one of the canyons that was part of Guillermo Castro's rancho, and the canyon still carries the Palomares name.
  • Parsons. Parsons is a Castro Valley neighborhood named for the avenue running through it.
  • Proctor. Proctor is one of Castro Valley's established residential neighborhoods, named for the school at its centre.

What it costs to own here

Owning that typical home runs about $10,309 a month, $123,708 a year, once the loan, the property tax, insurance, utilities and a maintenance budget are all counted. HOA dues are not in it, because they vary by building.

On 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed average was 7.4 percent, where the year-ago figure was 6.27 percent. The 2026 one-unit loan limit in Alameda County is $1,249,125, and that figure is both the conforming ceiling and the FHA ceiling.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Figure $1,198 in transfer tax on a $1,088,972 sale here, at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Convention puts about $599 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $16,335.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about $31,504 here. The five year figure is $96,873.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
