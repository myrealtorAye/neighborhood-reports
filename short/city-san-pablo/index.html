San Pablo: the October 2026 read

San Pablo kept the rancho's name while the cities around it were carved out of the same grant.

San Pablo carries the name of the rancho. Don Victor Castro's Rancho San Pablo covered this ground and the neighbouring cities were carved out of it, El Cerrito among them, settled after the 1906 earthquake by people leaving San Francisco. San Pablo itself stayed its own place.

San Pablo has 9,943 housing units and 18.4 percent of them predate 1950. 45.1 percent are single family detached and owner occupancy runs 42.0 percent, the lowest owner occupancy rate of any city in this county. The ACS median value is 551,700 dollars and ZHVI is about 554,000, down 0.7 percent over the year.

That 42.0 percent owner occupancy is the number to understand here. A majority of the housing in this city is renter occupied, so a buyer is frequently looking at property that has been a rental, and the inspection and the permit history matter more than usual.

So the questions here start with what the property has been. Confirm the legal unit count with the city, not the listing, and get the permit history, because conversions and additions without permits are the common problem in a market with this much rental stock. Ask whether the building is nonconforming, since that limits what you can rebuild after a loss.

Pull the year built. 18.4 percent of this stock is pre war, so foundation bolting, knob and tube wiring and galvanized supply lines are live questions on a good share of addresses.

Then pull the zoning for the parcel and everything touching it, and get the tax bill for the address.

And one habit worth keeping in this county. Pull the year built before you form any opinion on a listing, because it decides the entire inspection list and it is the single most useful number on a property profile. Then get the tax bill for the exact address off the county roll, not the listing estimate, and read which districts and assessments are on it. Those two documents cost nothing, they take a day, and between them they settle most of what a buyer actually needs to know before writing an offer.

Typical home value in San Pablo is $553,824 as of 2026-09-30, up 0.24 percent from the prior month and down 0.7 percent over the trailing year.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $553,824 here, transfer tax runs about $609 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. Custom is to split it, so about $305 a side, and who pays is set by the contract.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $6,646.
