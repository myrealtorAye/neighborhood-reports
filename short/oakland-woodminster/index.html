Woodminster, Oakland: the October 2026 read

Woodminster takes its name from the amphitheatre in Joaquin Miller Park on the ridge above it, and the published history on the neighborhood itself is thin, so this is the development record and the practical read.

What places it is the hills. The San Antonio redwood forest covered this ground until loggers levelled it inside about fifteen years from the late 1840s, with the first steam sawmill going up in 1850 at the head of Dimond Canyon and a logging road running down to a wharf at the foot of 13th Avenue. The parkland on the ridge today is second growth on that cut ground.

Then the fire. On the morning of Sunday, October 20, 1991 a grass fire that had been knocked down the day before near the Caldecott Tunnel came back to life shortly before eleven. Winds gusting to 65 miles an hour drove it down the south ridge of Claremont Canyon, and inside thirty minutes it had jumped Highway 24 and Highway 13 and set hundreds of houses alight. By the time it was fully controlled on October 23 it had killed 25 people, injured 150, and destroyed 2,843 single family homes and 437 apartment and condominium units. Losses ran to about 1.5 billion dollars. Cal Fire lists it as the Tunnel Fire and the cause was never determined. Whether or not a particular street burned, the firestorm is what set how this entire side of Oakland is insured.

The housing is hillside single family on sloped lots, built for the view, later in era than the Oakland average. Across the city 46.6 percent of the 191,713 housing units predate 1950; up here the share is lower and owner occupancy runs well above the citywide 42.3 percent.

So the order is the ridge order.

Fire insurance first: a written quote on the exact address before your contingencies expire, the risk rating explained, the defensible space requirement confirmed, and a straight answer about which carriers will write here.

Then the hillside list: soils and drainage history, the condition and ownership of any retaining structures, and where water leaves the property in a wet month.

Then the house matched to its year built, with foundation bolting and the sewer lateral Oakland certifies at sale on anything pre war.

Then walk up into Joaquin Miller Park from your street. Having a large park and an amphitheatre at the top of the road is the actual amenity here, and it is worth confirming how long that walk takes with your own legs.

Inside it

For context, the other parts of Oakland I cover, and what each one is:

  • Acorn. Fifty blocks of this neighborhood were demolished.
  • Adams Point. Adams Point is named for Edson Adams, one of the men who got hold of Oakland's land early, and in 1906 it filled up with San Franciscans who had nowhere else to go.
  • Allendale. The Allendale tract map was recorded in 1892, and there was a cinema operating here by 1913.
  • Brookfield Village. Twelve hundred houses went up here during the Second World War and they sold for four thousand dollars each.
  • Bushrod. Bushrod was annexed by Oakland in 1897, in the same batch as Temescal, Golden Gate and Piedmont Avenue, and that date is the most solid thing in its published record.
  • Caballo Hills. Caballo Hills is a hillside pocket at the southeastern edge of Oakland with very little published history, so this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Castlemont. Castlemont takes its name from the high school at its centre, and its published history is thin enough that this is the development record and the buying read.
  • Chabot Park. Anthony Chabot dammed a creek in 1868 and built Oakland its first water supply at municipal scale, and his name is on the parkland at the edge of this neighborhood.

What it costs to own here

Owning that typical home runs about $12,586 a month, $151,028 a year, once the loan, the property tax, insurance, utilities and a maintenance budget are all counted. HOA dues are not in it, because they vary by building.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Woodminster charges 16.10 per thousand, so the typical $1,314,195 home owes roughly $21,159 at transfer. Expect it split, near enough $10,579 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $2,000,000 the rate steps from 16.10 to 18.60 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,000 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $2,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Dublin charges 1.10 per thousand against Oakland at 16.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $19,713.

What waiting costs

On the same trailing rate, two years of waiting adds up to roughly $176,434 in price and payment combined, and five years to $579,607.

The input is a single trailing year, projected. It is not a forecast, and the five year number is the one to distrust first.
