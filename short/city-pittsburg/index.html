Pittsburg: the October 2026 read

Pittsburg has had three names and a steel mill through all of them.

Pittsburg has had three names. Col. Jonathan D. Stevenson, a New Yorker, bought the Rancho Los Medanos grant in 1849 and founded a town he called New York of the Pacific. It had failed by 1850. Coal out of Nortonville turned the waterfront into a shipping port, and the landing took the name Black Diamond from the mining firm. The town incorporated in 1903 under that name, though some accounts have the post office using Black Diamond as far back as 1868.

Steel made the third name. C.A. Hooper bought the land in 1900 and helped bring in Columbia Steel. The plant opened here in 1910 with one foundry and 60 employees and poured its first heat that November. One source puts the opening at 1906. Columbia Steel became a US Steel subsidiary in 1930 and the mill runs today as USS POSCO. On February 11, 1911 the citizens voted to rename the town Pittsburg, for Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, and dropped the h. Camp Stoneman here was a major Army staging area through World War II and Korea.

Pittsburg has 24,482 housing units and 8.3 percent of them predate 1950. 68.2 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 62.3 percent, and the ACS median value is 597,900 dollars. Citywide ZHVI is about 559,000 dollars, down 0.8 percent over the year.

So a buyer here is mostly buying postwar housing in a city with a working industrial waterfront. The split runs north to south. Old Town and the streets near the water are the oldest, with the pre war inspection list and a zoning question, because the permitted use on the next parcel over can be industrial. The subdivisions climbing south toward Kirker Pass are postwar and newer, and out there the tract map, the assessment districts and the commute over the pass are what matter.

Ask for the site history in writing on anything near the water. Pull the year built and the permit history everywhere. And drive Kirker Pass Road at your real commute hour before you decide.

What it costs to own here

Figure about $5,647 a month to own the typical home in Pittsburg, $67,767 a year. Loan, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance are in that figure. HOA dues are not, and they vary too much to average.

On 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed average was 7.4 percent, where the year-ago figure was 6.27 percent. For 2026, one-unit financing in Contra Costa County tops out at $1,249,125 on both the conforming and the FHA side.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

On a home at $558,908 here, transfer tax runs about $615 at 1.10 dollars per thousand. It is normally split down the middle, about $307 on each party. The contract governs, not the custom.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $6,707.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about -$10,796. Stretch it to five and the figure is -$32,178.

One year of data, extended. That is worth saying plainly, because compounding it out to five years makes the total look more certain than it is.
