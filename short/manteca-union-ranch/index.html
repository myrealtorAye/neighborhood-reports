Union Ranch, Manteca: the October 2026 read

Union Ranch is a newer Manteca subdivision on former ranch ground.

Manteca is named after a typographical error. Joshua Cowell arrived in 1861 and the place carried his name, but when the Central Pacific came through in 1873 a new one was needed, because a Cowell's Station already existed to the south near Tracy. They settled on Monteca and the first railroad tickets came back printed Manteca. The Manteca Bulletin treats the ticket misprint as the account with the most credence; a weaker telling puts the error in a newspaper. The town incorporated in 1918, with May 28 given as the date, and Cowell became its first mayor.

The rail line that named this town shaped the whole southern end of the county. The Central Pacific came through here in 1873 and the farm towns along it, Ripon and Escalon among them, sit where they do because of it. Ripon was called Stanislaus Station before it was Ripon. Tracy, twenty miles west, was founded on September 8, 1878 at a junction Southern Pacific built around Suisun Bay, and it took the headquarters away from Lathrop in 1894.

Manteca has 28,771 housing units and 4.5 percent of them predate 1950. 80.6 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 72.4 percent, and the ACS median value is 589,900 dollars. ZHVI is about 573,000 dollars, down 2.9 percent over the year.

So the buying questions here are newer-construction questions.

Get the homeowners association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, and ask exactly what the association maintains and what it does not.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate, and ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in and the year each one expires, in writing. A newly built Central Valley subdivision frequently carries a community facilities district on top of the base rate, and that line runs for decades and does not come off with the mortgage.

Then ask the inspector about grading and drainage, because a young subdivision on engineered fill shows its settlement in the first fifteen years.

Then the part specific to this address. Pull the tract map for the subdivision from the county recorder. The recording date, the original lot dimensions and the approved street layout are all on it, and that record beats anything a listing will tell you about when and how this neighborhood was built.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What it costs to own here

Figure about $5,684 a month to own the typical home in Union Ranch, $68,202 a year. Loan, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance are in that figure. HOA dues are not, and they vary too much to average.

On 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed average was 7.4 percent, where the year-ago figure was 6.27 percent. For 2026, one-unit financing in San Joaquin County tops out at $832,750 on both the conforming and the FHA side.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Union Ranch charges 1.10 per thousand, so the typical $572,552 home owes roughly $630 at transfer. Expect it split, near enough $315 apiece, with the contract having the final say.

What waiting costs

On the same trailing rate, two years of waiting adds up to roughly -$38,604 in price and payment combined, and five years to -$111,488.

This rests on one observed year carried forward. The two year figure is the defensible one; the five year figure is arithmetic, not a forecast.
