Brickyard Cove, Richmond: the October 2026 read

Brickyard Cove is named for what used to be here, and the shoreline is the whole reason to buy.

The cove sits on the far side of the ridge from Point Richmond, where the Santa Fe put its western terminus around 1900 and ferries ran to San Francisco. The brickworks that gave the cove its name are gone. What stands now is waterfront housing and a marina, built late, on ground that worked for a living first.

Standard Oil built its refinery here in 1901, with one account giving 1902. By 1904 it was reported as the second largest oil refinery in the world. Chevron runs it today. The city incorporated in 1905, after the refinery, not before it.

Richmond has 42,509 housing units and 28.5 percent of them predate 1950. 59.9 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 54.7 percent, and the ACS median value is 668,200 dollars. Brickyard Cove runs newer and more expensive than the citywide figures suggest.

So the questions here are waterfront questions and they come before the house.

Get the flood zone determination with a written insurance quote before your contingencies expire. Ask the inspector about settlement and about the condition of any seawall, bulkhead or revetment the property sits behind. Ask who owns that structure and who is obliged to maintain it, because on the water that is the most expensive thing that can go wrong and it is frequently shared.

If there is a homeowners association, get the budget and the reserve study, not the dues number, and ask what it maintains on the shoreline.

If a berth is part of the appeal, get the slip arrangements in writing separately from the house.

Then ask for the site history on the parcel, because a cove named after a brickyard had industry on it.

Then drive the route out through the tunnel at your commute hour. Brickyard Cove is further from the freeway than the map suggests.

What it costs to own here

Carry on the typical home here is roughly $6,007 monthly, or $72,082 annually. That covers the loan payment, property tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance at a stated budget. HOA dues are excluded, because they differ building to building.

The survey average for a 30-year fixed is 7.4 percent at 2026-10-15, compared with 6.27 percent a year back. In Contra Costa County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 8.10 dollars per thousand, a $600,691 sale in Brickyard Cove carries about $4,866 in transfer tax. It is normally split down the middle, about $2,433 on each party. The contract governs, not the custom.

The part that costs people money is the bracket. The rate that applies is charged on the ENTIRE sale price, not only the amount above the line. At $1,000,000 the rate steps from 8.10 to 13.50 per thousand, so a sale one dollar over that line pays about $5,400 more than a sale one dollar under it. On anything pricing near $1,000,000, the number to run is not the percentage. It is which side of the line the contract price lands on.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $7,208.

What waiting costs

Hold off two years and the combined price and payment cost is near -$13,716. At five years it reaches -$40,789.

One year of data, extended. That is worth saying plainly, because compounding it out to five years makes the total look more certain than it is.
