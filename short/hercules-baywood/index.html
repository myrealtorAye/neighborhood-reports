Baywood, Hercules: the October 2026 read

Baywood is a residential subdivision in Hercules.

Hercules is named after dynamite. The California Powder Works developed this site in 1881 to make its patented explosive, Hercules powder, and the town took the product's name. The company's managers incorporated the town itself on December 15, 1900, because they could not get the county supervisors to pass the laws they wanted for the plant. The corporate history is tangled: an earlier Hercules Powder Company was incorporated in 1882 by DuPont with Laflin and Rand and DuPont dissolved it in 1904, and a later one was incorporated on October 18, 1912 and began business in January 1913.

Hercules has 9,581 housing units and 0.5 percent of them predate 1950. For a town incorporated in 1900 that is the whole modern history of the place in one number: almost everything standing here went up long after the powder works. 57.9 percent are single family detached, owner occupancy runs 79.2 percent, and the ACS median value is 769,400 dollars. ZHVI is about 736,000 dollars, down 0.7 percent over the year.

So the buying questions here are newer-construction questions.

Get the homeowners association budget and the reserve study, not the dues figure, and ask exactly what the association maintains and what it does not.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate, and ask which assessment districts the parcel sits in and the year each one expires, in writing. A newly built East County subdivision can carry a community facilities district on top of the base rate, and that line runs for decades and does not come off with the mortgage.

Then ask the inspector about grading and drainage, because a young subdivision on engineered fill shows its settlement in the first fifteen years.

Then the part specific to this address. Pull the tract map for the subdivision from the county recorder. The recording date, the original lot dimensions and the approved street layout are all on it, and that record beats anything a listing will tell you about when and how this neighborhood was built.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

Baywood charges 1.10 per thousand, so the typical $735,539 home owes roughly $809 at transfer. Convention puts about $405 on each side of the table. That is custom, and it is negotiable.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $8,826.

What waiting costs

The two year cost of waiting, price plus payment, comes to about -$11,121 here. The five year figure is -$33,238.
