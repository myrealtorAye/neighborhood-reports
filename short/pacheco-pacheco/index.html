Pacheco, Pacheco: the October 2026 read

Pacheco has an ACS median value of 343,300 dollars and only 44.6 percent of its housing is detached. Both numbers are the lowest in the county and they are the same fact.

Pacheco carries the name of Salvio Pacheco, who was granted Rancho Monte del Diablo in 1834 and whose family laid out the town of Todos Santos in 1868, the place the Contra Costa Gazette renamed Concord on April 17, 1869. Pacheco itself never incorporated and it is unincorporated Contra Costa County territory today.

Pacheco has 1,625 housing units. 44.6 percent are single family detached, and the ACS median value is 343,300 dollars, the lowest of any place in this county. 2.8 percent of the stock predates 1950 and owner occupancy runs 79.2 percent. A scope note on price. Neither Zillow nor Realtor.com publishes a separate value series for this community, so there is no citywide figure to quote for it. Work from closed comparable sales on the specific streets and treat any citywide number a listing shows you as belonging to somewhere else.

So the buying questions are mostly postwar questions.

Pull the year built and the permit history for the parcel. On a tract house of this era, ask about the electrical panel and its capacity, the supply lines, the sewer lateral, the roof, and whether the foundation has ever been bolted.

An original kitchen and original aluminium windows are either the discount or the asset. The inspection report tells you which.

Get the tax bill for the address, not the listing estimate.

Then the part specific to this address. Confirm first that the address is unincorporated county territory, because it changes who issues your permits, who provides your services, who polices it and how your tax bill is built. Take it from the county, not from a listing. Then ask which school district serves the parcel. Then establish what kind of housing the address actually is, because a 44.6 percent detached share and a 343,300 dollar median together mean a large part of this market is not conventional detached housing. If it is a manufactured or mobile home, ask whether you are buying the land or renting a space, get the park's rules and rent history, and ask your lender early whether it will finance that at all. Those answers change the entire transaction.

Then drive your commute at the hour you would really drive it, and walk the block after dinner.

What it costs to own here

Put the minimum down on the typical Pacheco home and the monthly carry lands near $7,287. That is $87,448 across a year, counting loan, tax, insurance, utilities and maintenance. HOA dues are not in it, since no two are alike.

On 2026-10-15 the 30-year fixed average was 7.4 percent, where the year-ago figure was 6.27 percent. In Contra Costa County the 2026 one-unit limit is $1,249,125. It serves as the conforming conventional ceiling and the FHA ceiling at once.

The transfer tax line that costs real money

At 1.10 dollars per thousand, a $749,473 sale in Pacheco carries about $824 in transfer tax. Local practice splits it down the middle, roughly $412 each, though the contract decides.

And it is set by the city, not the county. Antioch charges 1.10 per thousand against El Cerrito at 13.10. Same county, same price, and the spread is $8,994.

What waiting costs

Price and payment together, a two year wait here runs about -$22,177. Stretch it to five and the figure is -$65,660.

Treat it as a scenario, not a prediction. The appreciation input is one year of observed change carried forward, and five years of compounding on one year of data flatters the result.
